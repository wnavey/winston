## Checklist item (verbatim)

> The gabion wall is less than six (6) feet in height, or, where site-specific constraints prevent that, a taller wall is shown with additional structural support sufficient to ensure stability.

**Evidence expected**: Water quality pond plan / wall detail

## Agent observation (verbatim)

> Sheet 30 water quality calculations show 'Elevation of Top of Gabion Wall: 540.27 ft. msl' and 'Water Quality Elevation: 540.77 ft. msl.' The gabion wall height is therefore 540.77 - 540.27 = 0.50 ft (6 inches). Sheet 29 Section A-A shows the gabion wall profile between the sedimentation and filtration basins with the top at FL 540.27 and note specifying '5 TO 8 INCH DIAMETER ROCKS SHALL BE USED.'

## Agent reasoning (verbatim)

> ECM 1.6.5(B)(2) states 'the gabion outlet structure berm/wall/gabion height shall not exceed six (6) feet.' The 0.5-foot (6-inch) gabion wall is well below the 6-foot maximum. No site-specific constraints or additional structural support justification is needed for a wall this low.

## Agent explanation (verbatim)

> Gabion wall is 0.5 feet (6 inches) high, well under the 6-foot maximum.

## Evidence locations cited

- Sheet 30: Water Quality Calculations - Gabion Wall Elevation
- Sheet 29: Section A-A - Gabion Wall Profile

## Hypothesis — why the agent got this wrong

Agent computed gabion wall height as 540.77 (water quality elevation) − 540.27 (top of gabion wall) = 0.50 ft. **This is the depth of water above the wall, not the wall height.** The wall extends from its base elevation up to 540.27 ft; agent never read the base elevation. Classic dimensional misread.

## Remediation hypothesis

**R-8** — Vision-protocol change: for dimensional reads (wall height, hatch size, vertical clearance), require the agent to cite the exact callout text on the drawing and the section/profile where the dimension is shown, not just an inferred elevation difference.

                                      Staff Report
TO: Planning and Zoning Commission

FROM: Todd Streiler, Planning & Development Manager

DATE: May 6, 2025

SUBJECT: Prairie Point PUD Rezoning & Area Plan (DP Project #25-1000)

Project Summary
The proposed “C3-PUD” Rezoning Application, PUD Area Plan and fees in the amount of $1,520.00 were
received by the City on March 3, 2025. Staff reviewed the items for completeness and compliance with
the applicable regulations and submitted the attached memo to the petitioner. The petitioner submitted a
revised Area Plan, Architectural Elevations and memo documenting how each comment and
recommendation was addressed, see attached, on April 24th 2025.

At the April 9, 2025, meeting, the Planning & Zoning Commission passed a motion to postpone the public
hearing for the subject item until the May 14th P&Z Meeting. At the April 16, 2024, meeting, the Board
also voted to postpone the public hearing until their next regularly scheduled meeting on May 21, 2025.

The proposed PUD Area Plan includes 3.02 acres located on the north side of Town Square Avenue
midway between Bryan/Winghaven Blvd. and Hanley/Post Road. The Plan include four (4) quadplex
buildings with a total of 16-attached single family dwellings mixed with a 7,246 SF commercial building
accessed via a 26’ wide private roadway. The site is currently cleared, but undeveloped.

Zoning Compliance Summary
The site was rezoned by the City in 2021 after establishing the “ND” New Development Zoning District
via Ordinance #2136 which replaced the City’s “Uptown District” with the “ND” District. Since that time,
no new development has followed the “ND” Zoning District regulations; all new developed had to rezone
out of the ND District, including the subject development. Staff reviewed the Area Plan and the following
‘C3” deviations are required and noted on the proposed Area Plan:


                     Requirement                      Requested        Required
                     Minimum Lot Width:                   26’             150’
                     Minimum Front Yard Setback:          20’             25’
                     Minimum Side Yard Setback:            0’             15’
                     Garage Width                         20’             21’
                     Sidewalks                          One-side       Both sides
                                                                    Prairie Point PUD Rezoning & Area Plan
                                                                                   May 14, 2025, Staff Report

The Landscape Plan meets the applicable requirements, and the proposed Area Plan includes notes to
ensure the site meets or exceeds the City’s photometric standards.
Comprehensive Plan & Vision Study Recommendations
The 2020 Comprehensive Plan designates the site as “Single-Family Residential” on the Future Land
Use Map. On page 49 under the “Residential Development” heading, the Plan notes: “based on input
from development community representatives and current market trends, the desire for large lots has
been declining…These demographic trends and desires have also fueled a market for large-unit villa
style development where the housing units are clustered closer together on smaller lot sizes with a
greater portion of the overall development devoted to green space. The maintenance of the common
areas, yards and landscaping is handled by a homeowner or condo association. These residential
development types, while higher in overall density on a given site than traditional single-family residential
uses, do not represent typical multi-family unit densities…. The Plan suggest close scrutiny of proposals
for multi-family development (walk-up or garden apartments, duplexes, townhomes, etc.). Residential
developments that are not conventional single-family types should be restricted to properties that have
unique size, shape, and/or topographic characteristics that would not support a typical single-family
layout. The density of attached single-family development should not be so great as to impact the
character of the adjacent area.
On page 50, the Plan goes on to say; “Where the Future Land Use Plan proposes residential development
for properties that are currently vacant, there is no attempt to make a distinction between the types of
development. Rather this Plan recommends that consideration for the type and density of residential
development be indicated by the adjacent land uses, roadway access and other development
considerations unique to the site. Any residential development that deviates from the underlying zoning
densities, or is not traditional single-family detached by nature, should only be developed in accord with
the City’s Planning Unit Development Zoning Regulations…. Generally, intense developments such as
villas, (single-family attached housing), apartments and condominiums should be clustered and located
along arterial streets to minimize traffic impact and the impact on single-family detached homes.”
The Vision Report describes the general location of the subject site as follows (see page 29 under the
“Focus Area 2” heading): “With its proximity to City Hall and other public and semi-public uses, Focus
Area 2 is a prime opportunity for a mix of uses that exude a town center feel”
On page 30 under the heading “Focus Area 2- North Side” it states: “The section of Focus Area 2 on the
north side of Town Square Avenue is a series of three parcels that are currently privately owned. These
parcels are currently underutilized, and if not consolidated, the sizes of these parcels limit their potential
for development. This section runs along the main corridor of Town Square Avenue and is bordered to
the north and east by single-family residential. With its location slightly removed from the intersection,
this section is not as appropriate for retail uses as are the other sections of Focus Area 2. This section
is well suited for additional housing options at varying scales that are appropriate to border the existing
conditions.
With their own specific qualities and adjacencies, different potential development options are appropriate
for the sections throughout these Focus Areas. Together this mix of uses across the Planning Area
complement each other and existing conditions, provide a unique identity for each Focus Area to minimize
competition, diversify the risk of each specific endeavor, provide a balance between the built environment
and open space, and enhance the town center feel to create a thriving community-centric area.
Summary: The Comprehensive Plan and Vision Report both anticipate and support attached single-
family development at the subject site. The Comprehensive Plan recommends if apartments are

proposed at the subject site, the PUD process should be utilized. The Plan did not anticipate commercial
development on the subject site. The Vision Report did not rule out commercial development of the
subject site. It stated that the location of the site was “slightly removed from the intersection, (and that)
this section is not as appropriate for retail uses as are the other sections of Focus Area 2. The Vision
Report recommends compatibility with the use, density and character of the existing adjacent uses.
Please note, the City rezoned the site from “UD” Uptown District to “ND” New Development District after
adoption of the Comprehensive Plan. The ND District includes a “Use Matrix” which allows a mix of
commercial and residential uses. According to the Comprehensive Plan and Vision Study, an attached
single-family development with commercial may be considered at this location, but it should follow the
City’s PUD process and should include considerations that make the development compatible with the
adjacent existing uses.
Recommended Action
The petitioner is requesting the site is rezoned from “ND” to “C3-PUD” and approval of the proposed Area
Plan. Upon review, Staff provided the above excerpts and summary of the Comprehensive Plan and
Vision Study and found the rezoning and the proposed Area Plan is consistent with the applicable zoning
requirements. Staff recommends the Planning Commission pass one of the following motions:
      A. Motion to recommend the Board approve the “C-3PUD” Rezoning and Area Plan as
         submitted.
      B. Motion to recommend the Board approve the “C3PUD” Rezoning and Area Plan with
         conditions.
Motions should be in the affirmative. Should the Commission or Commissioner decide to deny the PUD
Rezoning and Area Plan as submitted, they should vote “Nay” to a motion to approve.
Next Steps: 1. Consideration of the rezoning and Area Plan by the Board. 2. If approved, P&Z will review
and recommend the Final Plan to the Board for final consideration. 3. Improvement Plans. 4. Final Plat.

   Private Streets Maintained by Property Owners. These signs shall be installed where they are easily

   last lot is sold in the subdivision. The minimum size for each sign shall be twelve (12) inches high
   by eighteen (18) inches wide with two (2) inch high letters.”

   Section 405.453.

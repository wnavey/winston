## Block 2: notes — Notes detailing special construction techniques to minimize root damage and soil compaction during excavation and grading, as well as watering requirements for impacted trees.

Special Construction Techniques
1. Prior to excavation within tree driplines, or the removal of trees adjacent to the other trees that are to remain, make a clean cut between the disturbed and undisturbed root zones with a rock saw or similar equipment to minimize root damage.
2. In critical root zone areas that cannot be protected during construction with fencing, and where heavy vehicular traffic is anticipated, cover those areas with four (4) inches of organic mulch to be produced on site, to minimize soil compaction.
3. Perform all grading within critical root zone areas with small equipment to minimize root damage.
4. Water all trees most heavily impacted by construction activities deeply as necessary during periods of hot, dry weather. Spray tree crowns with water periodically to reduce dust accumulation on the leaves.
5. When installing concrete adjacent to the root zone of a tree, use a plastic vapor barrier behind the concrete to prohibit leaching of lime into the soil.

## Block 8: notes — Four unnumbered notes related to decompaction of soils, topsoil requirements for revegetation, permanent vegetative stabilization, and finished elevation requirements for specific landscape areas.

1. Per LDC 25-8-323(C), for areas on the site that are to remain pervious after development, any soils that are compacted during site grading and construction operations must be decompacted in compliance with the ECM and in compliance with SSM 661S.
2. All disturbed areas to be revegetated are required to have a minimum of six (6) inches of topsoil. Topsoil shall meet the definition in Standard Specification 601S.3. In areas where no topsoil exists, or where topsoil is needed for vegetative establishment, the subgrade shall be loosened by discing or scarifying to a depth of at least two (2) inches prior to placement of six (6) inches of topsoil. [ECM 1.4.7, Appendix P-1]
3. Permanent vegetative stabilization of areas disturbed by construction shall be per ECM Appendix P-1, unless otherwise indicated on the plan.
4. For parking-lot islands, medians, peninsulas, and similar landscape areas that are to receive parking-lot stormwater runoff, finished elevation must be at least six (6) inches below the finished curb elevation to allow for placement of six (6) inches of topsoil [ECM 1.4.7].

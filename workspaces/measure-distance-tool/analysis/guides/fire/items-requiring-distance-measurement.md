The FIRE department has a lower proportion of distance-measurable items than EL (19.4% vs 30.6%), largely because fire review includes many documentation/calculation/specification items (AFD tables, fire flow hydraulics, sprinkler type selection, code citations). However, the items that ARE distance-based are high-value — fire lane widths, hydrant spacing, building access distances, and setbacks are among the most frequently cited deficiencies in fire review.

The distance-plus proportion is notably higher for FIRE (36.6% vs 16.1%), reflecting that many fire distance checks also require verifying additional conditions like sprinkler status, obstruction type, occupancy classification, or building height to determine which threshold applies.

## Methodology

- All 19 guides classified by manual review of each deficiency description
- Items classified as "horizontal" when the deficiency involves measuring plan-view distance, clearance, setback, separation, width, spacing, or proximity between features shown on site plans
- Items classified as "vertical-or-mixed" when the deficiency involves height, elevation, grade, slope, or vertical clearance (requires section/profile views)
- Items classified as "not-applicable" when the deficiency involves documentation, labeling, notes, calculations, material specifications, construction standards, code citations, plan formatting, or administrative procedures
- Sub-classification: "distance-only" when measuring the distance and comparing to threshold resolves the verdict; "distance-plus" when additional information (sprinkler status, obstruction type, building height, etc.) is also required

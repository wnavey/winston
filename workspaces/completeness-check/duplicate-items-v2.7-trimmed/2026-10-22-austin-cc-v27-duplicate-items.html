Austin Completeness Check v2.7-trimmed: Duplicate Items

Inspector General · 2026-10-22 · Scope: bureau/jurisdictions/austin/completeness-check/v2.7-trimmed (14 guides, 192 items) and its source-citations.json (cc-source-citations run austin-v2.7-trimmed, 2026-10-07)

Bottom line. The checklist has no copy-pasted duplicates, but 3 pairs are effectively the same check, and 6 more overlap in part: one item's scope repeats another's, so one gap in a submission yields two or three findings. Every case has the same cause: the City's own sources list the requirement in more than one place. The Requirements page has a "Plan Set" list and a separate "Base Information Required on All Sheets" list, and Trees and Environmental each list "Tree protection plan (LDC 25-8-604)". Each guide was built from a different section, so the City's repetition carried through into our guides.

  • Merge (same check): CC-1-14 = CC-22-08 (TIA report) · CC-1-26 ⊇ CC-2-14 (all sheets sealed) · CC-15-02 = CC-15-14 (tree protection plan).
  • Rescope (partial overlap): the utilities cluster (CC-2-23/24 vs AE-02/03 vs CC-1-24), easements (CC-1-25 / CC-2-21), boundaries (CC-1-23 / CC-2-16), the engineering report seal (CC-1-09 / CC-20-01), the summary letter seal (CC-1-10/11 / CC-20-02), watershed classification (CC-15-06 / CC-15-07), and the License Agreement catch-all (CC-24-13 / CC-24-01…06).
  • About 20 other similar-looking pairs were checked and are distinct for a reason (see the last section).

Method

I read every guide end to end: each item's text, condition, location, fail status and the guide's validation methodology. For every item I also read its source-citations.json entry: the source label, status, page, and the quoted span the locator found. Pairs whose titles looked similar were then checked three ways:

  1. Source text. Do the two items cite the same line of the City's documents, or different lines? Checked against the snapshots in bureau/jurisdictions/austin/codes/dsd-site-plan-submittal/contents/.
  2. Logic. Can one item fail while the other passes? If not, one item contains the other.
  3. Behaviour. Verdicts on the same submission in past runs: WhiteWater v2.7 opus55 ×3 (whitewater-cc-v27-opus55-3v), WhiteWater runbook run 2 (WWCW_rb2_run2_claude_vision), and SA 2008 (sa2008-control-main-cc1, cc-1 only).

The bar for a candidate: the two items test the same artifact with the same pass criterion, so one gap in a submission yields two findings. Items that share a topic but test different artifacts, depths or conditions were left alone.

Merge candidates: same check

MergeTIA report: CC-1-14 and CC-22-08

CC-1-14
TIA report and technical addendum missing when TIA Determination Worksheet indicates TIA is required. Condition: TIA required. Fail.
CC-22-08
TIA report and technical addendum not present when TIA Determination indicates required (Full TIA, Transportation Assessment, ZTA, or TIA Compliance). Condition: TIA required per worksheet. Fail.
Sources
Both trace to one sentence, which appears on the Requirements page (Core Submittal) and on CC Submittal Checklist p.1:
Completed and signed Traffic Impact Analysis (TIA) Determination Worksheet and, if required, a TIA (report and technical addendum).
The Requirements quote cited for CC-22-08 is also about the worksheet. Its other two sources add nothing: "TIA Determination Worksheet p.2" has no snapshot (no_snapshot), and "TCM Section 10" is document_level.
Past runs
WhiteWater, both runs: both items fail on the same missing "Cityview at Double Creek" TIA compliance memo, so the applicant gets two findings for one missing document.
Distinction?
Only that CC-22-08 lists the study types (Full TIA / TA / ZTA / Compliance Letter). That is guidance for the reviewer, not a separate requirement. Both guides already point at each other in their methodology ("TIA Determination Worksheet result must be checked against presence/absence of TIA report").
Proposal: keep one item and retire the other. CC-1-14 has the stronger citation. CC-22-08 sits with the transportation reviewer, whose department owns the TIA. Either way, move CC-22-08's list of study types into the item that stays.

MergeAll sheets sealed: CC-1-26 contains CC-2-14 (and CC-1-21)

CC-1-26
Sheets not sealed by an active TX PE, or missing the dated signature or firm registration number; architectural sheets excluded. Fail.
CC-2-14
Engineer's seal, signature or date missing from one or more plan sheets; architectural sheets excluded. Fail.
Sources
The same requirement appears twice on the Requirements page. The Plan Set list (p.4, and CC Submittal Checklist p.1) has "All sheets sealed by an active licensed State of Texas professional engineer (PE) with the engineering firm registration number included". The Base Information Required on All Sheets list (p.16) has "Seal and signature of the engineer preparing plans, and the date the plans were signed by the engineer (not required for small projects)". The CC-2 guide itself says CC-2-14 is "indirectly referenced by the CC Submittal Checklist's all-sheets-sealed requirement".
Logic
Anything that fails CC-2-14 also fails CC-1-26, which checks the same things plus the firm number. Both guides carry the same architectural-sheet exemption paragraph, word for word.
Past runs
WhiteWater, both runs: both fail on Sheet 30 (geotechnical placeholder, empty seal box). The explanations are almost word-for-word the same.
Distinction?
One small one: the p.16 source exempts small projects ("not required for small projects"). Neither item encodes that exemption today (both are "Always").
Proposal: retire CC-2-14. If the small-project exemption matters, add it to CC-1-26 (CC-1-12 already detects small projects). Optional: CC-1-21 (cover sheet signed and sealed) is also covered by CC-1-26, since the cover sheet is a sheet. Its source is a separate checklist line ("One set of plans including cover sheet with signature and seal"), so keeping it is defensible. It always passed in the runs above, so it costs little.

MergeTree protection plan: CC-15-02 and CC-15-14

CC-15-02 (TRE-04)
Tree protection plan per LDC 25-8-604 not present in plan set. Methodology: pass on any tree-protection content (a plan, a CRZ plan, or the City's tree protection notes and details). Fail only if there is none at all.
CC-15-14 (ENV-18b)
Tree Protection Plan (or Tree Preservation Plan) sheet not present in plan set. Methodology: any title is fine; it needs a sheet with tree protection content.
Sources
The Requirements page lists the line twice: under City Arborist (line 167, "Tree protection plan per Land Development Code (LDC 25-8-604)") and under Environmental (line 198, "Tree protection plan (LDC 25-8-604)"). CC-15-14's third citation, LDC 25-8-181, is not_found, because that section covers erosion and sedimentation, not trees. That is a leftover from splitting the old combined ENV-18 "Erosion/Sedimentation Control and Tree Protection Plan" item.
Past runs
WhiteWater, both runs: both pass on the same evidence (Sheet 10 TP fencing and legend, notes on Sheet 8, details on Sheet 11).
Distinction?
Only how strict each one is, and the two conflict. A plan set with City tree protection notes and details but no plan view passes CC-15-02 and fails CC-15-14. That looks accidental: CC-15-02's lenient bar was a deliberate decision ("Do not fail because fencing is not drawn on a plan view").
Proposal: merge into one tree protection item and pick one bar. Keep CC-15-09 (Erosion & Sedimentation Control sheet) separate; that split from ENV-18 is deliberate and documented.

Rescope candidates: partial overlap

In these cases each item has some content no other item covers, so deleting one would lose coverage. The fix is to narrow one item so it stops repeating the other.

RescopeUtility lines: CC-2-23 / CC-2-24 vs AE-02 / AE-03 (and CC-1-24)

Items
CC-2-23 overhead utility lines, existing and proposed (fail) · CC-2-24 underground utility lines, existing and proposed (warn) · AE-02 existing electric facilities, overhead and underground · AE-03 proposed electric facilities · CC-1-24 existing utilities and crossovers.
Sources
These are separate lines in the source: Base Information has "Location of all existing and proposed electric utility facilities…" (→ AE-02/03) and "Exact locations and types of all utility lines, underground and overhead, existing and proposed" (→ CC-2-23/24). So by source, CC-2-23/24 cover all utility types.
The problem
CC-2's validation methodology only talks about electric: "Existing electric utilities… Proposed electric utilities… Electrical Site Plan, or Franchise Utility Plan / Joint Trench Plan". In practice, CC-2-23/24 run as a second copy of AE-02/03.
Past runs
WhiteWater, both runs: one gap ("no proposed electric service on any sheet") produced three findings: CC-2-23 fail, AE-03 fail, and CC-2-24 warn ("no existing or proposed underground electric").
Proposal: rewrite the CC-2-23/24 methodology to cover all utility types (water, wastewater, storm, gas, telecom), as the source line does, and leave electric to AE-02/03. Alternatively, merge CC-2-23 and CC-2-24 into one "all utility lines" item. CC-1-24's "existing utilities and crossovers" then overlaps only the existing half, which is acceptable.

RescopeExisting easements: CC-1-25 and CC-2-21

Items
CC-1-25 existing easements and setbacks not identified (no special methodology) · CC-2-21 known existing easement not shown (fail) / not on the applicable sheets (warn), with a detailed per-easement procedure that uses facts.md.
Sources
Checklist p.1 / Plan Set: "All existing easements and setbacks clearly identified". Base Information: "All existing and future dedicated easements". The CC-2 guide itself credits CC-2-21 to the Checklist p.1 line that CC-1-25 cites.
Past runs
WhiteWater, both runs: CC-1-25 pass, CC-2-21 warn on the same easements. CC-2-21's procedure caught adjoining drainage easement 2020129003 missing from the site and utility sheets; CC-1-25 has no procedure, so it passed. The same question got two different answers.
Proposal: narrow CC-1-25 to setbacks only and leave easements to CC-2-21. If CC-1-25 has to keep easements, point it at CC-2-21's procedure so the two can't disagree.

RescopeBoundary bearings and dimensions: CC-1-23 and CC-2-16

Items
CC-1-23 limits of construction (including access drives) or property boundaries not shown, or missing bearings and dimensions · CC-2-16 boundary lines with bearings and dimensions not shown on Existing Conditions or Overall Site Plan.
Sources
Same wording in two lists: Plan Set / Checklist p.1 "Limits of construction and property boundaries with bearings and dimensions", and Base Information "Boundary lines with bearings and dimensions".
Past runs
WhiteWater: both pass on Sheets 7 and 12.
Proposal: narrow CC-1-23 to limits of construction only, or retire CC-2-16. Low stakes, because they agree whenever both are evaluated, but one missing bearing table would currently produce two findings.

RescopeEngineering report front-page seal: CC-1-09 and CC-20-01

Items
CC-1-09 bound Engineering Report missing, or front page lacks signature, seal or date (condition: not a utility, extension, CUP, transportation or parking project) · CC-20-01 engineer's signature, seal and date missing from the front page of bound WQ/drainage documents, or seal bears "preliminary"/"draft" (condition: always).
Sources
Checklist p.1 "Engineer's Report bound with signature, seal and date" vs Requirements Section 30 "Engineer's signature, seal, and date without qualifiers on the front page of bound documents containing engineering work".
Past runs
WhiteWater: both pass, citing the same Engineers Report cover page (TX PE 86951, 07/07/2026).
Distinction?
The "no qualifier" check is unique to CC-20-01. The conditions also differ: CC-20-01 can fail a parking-only project that CC-1-09 exempts.
Proposal: add the no-qualifier clause to CC-1-09 and retire CC-20-01, or narrow CC-20-01 to the qualifier check alone.

RescopeEngineer's Summary Letter: CC-1-10 / CC-1-11 and CC-20-02

Items
CC-1-10 ESL missing as a separate document · CC-1-11 ESL not signed, sealed and dated by a TX PE · CC-20-02 letter "not present, or not signed/sealed/dated, or does not discuss 2-yr peak flow control and WQ compliance".
Overlap
The first two clauses of CC-20-02 repeat CC-1-10 and CC-1-11. Only the 2-yr / WQ discussion is unique to it. In SA 2008, CC-1-11 failed (signed but no PE seal), and a full run would fail CC-20-02 as well for the same reason.
Proposal: narrow CC-20-02 to the content check: "Engineer's Summary Letter does not discuss 2-yr peak flow control and WQ compliance".

RescopeWatershed classification: CC-15-06 and CC-15-07

Items
CC-15-06 watershed name and classification not on the cover sheet · CC-15-07 watershed classification and standard environmental notes not in the plan set (location: cover sheet).
Sources
Environmental cover-sheet list "Name of watershed and classification" (→ 06) vs Environmental "Watershed classification and standard notes" (→ 07). The page also has a third variant, "Watershed status and standard notes".

RescopeLicense Agreement catch-all: CC-24-13 and CC-24-01…06

Items
CC-24-13 required License or Encroachment Agreement not submitted or referenced (warn) · CC-24-01…06 License Agreement for tower crane, excavation support, streetscape, trees and landscaping, dining, lighting.
Overlap
Every failure of 01–06 also triggers CC-24-13. The only scope unique to CC-24-13 is Encroachment Agreements, whose type-specific items (CC-24-07…12) were removed in the trim. The 1700 S Lamar forced-outcomes file already treats them as one cluster ("addressed separately under CC-24-03/04/13").
Proposal: narrow CC-24-13 to Encroachment Agreements plus any License Agreement type not listed in 01–06.

Low priorityFire flow at 20 PSI: AW-03 and AW-25

AW-03 (Project Information / Demand Data table complete, including "fire flow") and AW-25 (available fire flow at 20 PSI shown) cite the same AW Completeness Checklist sentence, "All plans shall provide available fire demand at 20 PSI…". In WhiteWater, AW-25's evidence was "Sheet 5 demand data table shows available fire flow at 20 psi", i.e. a cell inside the table AW-03 checks. On the AW General Info Sheet, "fire demand" and "available fire flow" may be separate fields, so this may be a wording issue in AW-03 rather than a true overlap. Proposal: confirm on the template; if they are the same cell, drop "fire flow" from AW-03's list.

Low priorityFloodplain delineation across disciplines: CC-15-13, CC-19-03, CC-19-04, AW-38a

Four items ask for floodplain lines to be drawn: Environmental (CWQZ/WQTZ plus 100-yr), Floodplain (FEMA 100-yr; fully developed 25- and 100-yr), and Austin Water (25/100-yr per Atlas 14 plus CWQZ plus erosion hazard zones). A site with an undrawn floodplain could get three or four findings. They are listed as low priority, not rescope, because each one comes from a different department's own checklist and each has a component the others lack (FEMA vs fully developed, WQTZ, EHZ). In both WhiteWater runs all four were n/a. Proposal: keep them. Revisit only if city comment reports show Austin issues a single floodplain comment rather than one per discipline.

Caveats

  • The comment-number maps (true-comment-num-mapping.tsv, pape-dawson-comment-num-mapping.tsv) are ordinal, so they can't show whether the City issues one comment or two for the same gap. A City MCR that issues one comment for one of the pairs above would be the strongest confirmation.
  • The behavioural evidence comes from two submissions (WhiteWater for all groups, SA 2008 for cc-1 only). Most rescope candidates were n/a or agreed there; the logic and source checks carry them, not the run data.
  • Merging items renumbers them, which breaks the item-ID continuity that cc-compare, the comment numbering maps (2.9) and amend-in-place rely on. Retiring an item, as was done with CC-20-03 in v2.6, avoids that.

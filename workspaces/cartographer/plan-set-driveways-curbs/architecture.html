Cartographer · Plan-set driveways and curbs

Driveways and curbs from plan sets — existing and proposed, with widths, lengths and return radii, and the adjacent driveways the sheet leaves off

How a site plan set gives up its proposed and existing curb lines with their arcs' radii, how each driveway is derived from the curbs that cross the lot's frontage with the numbers TCM Section 7 asks for, and how the anchor run joins them, on the county grid, to the driveways next door, the intersecting street and the curb inlets that no sheet shows.

Overview

Three registry entries, one of them derived. The tracer the footprint slice shipped learns to keep arcs and to trace a set of chains; code pairs face and back, finds the lot's frontage, and derives each driveway with its five numbers; lines enter the figure model through an expand-only column; and the anchor run carries them to the grid and fetches the 300 ft context Austin publishes, so one join answers the spacing question (§1.1, §1.6).

What this slice is for

  1. The fifth- and tenth-most-demanded layers, and the one measurement the sheet cannot carry. Curbs and returns are wanted by 34 of 134 geometric comments (18 A-tier), driveways by 19 (12 A-tier), and the report names "neighbouring driveways" among the county-side geometry that 46 % of comments need. TCM 7.6.2 measures spacing to the next driveway along the street; the White Water survey base stops 10–35 ft past the lot. sizing note, §1.2, §1.3
  2. A curb is a line with arcs. The returns on sheet 12 are 44 cubic Béziers on the face layer and 38 on the back; a circle fit gives R 20.02 / 25.02 / 28.02 against the printed R20.0' / R25.0' / R28.0', and the back set sits at R − 0.50 ft on every one of 17 pairs — a six-inch curb, read from geometry. The footprint reader flattens every Bézier to eight chords and throws the radius away; this spec keeps it. §1.4, D2
  3. Same tracer, one more branch. A polyline feature's candidate is a set of chains, by layer when the layers survived and by stroke style when they did not; the confirm worker may remove a chain from the set and nothing else. Two of the three plan sets on disk are layer-less on the site plan, so the style rung is the common case. §1.3, D3
  4. The driveway is derived, not read. North star D14's fourth path, used for the first time: the polygon between two curb faces where they cross the lot's frontage course, with width at the right-of-way line, throat width, return radii, angle and throat depth computed once in lot feet. On White Water the derivation has to say "no proposed driveway; one existing, shared, 33.2 ft at the right-of-way line, retained and altered" — and that is the acceptance test. D6, D7, §6
  5. Lines enter through an expand, never a contract. A geometry column beside polygon, backfilled, with every reader moved over; dropping polygon is a later spec. geo needs no DDL: its geometry columns already take a line. D10, D11
  6. Off the sheet, the opening is derivable even where it is not labelled. Austin's 2023 impervious layer has no Driveways-class polygon within 300 ft of the site, but 16 paved areas and 2 paved roads; the boundary between them is a driveway opening, at about ±2 ft. A new anchor step fetches it with the street, its Level, the inlets and a 6 in orthophoto, and publish writes it beside the anchored figures under one anchor_id. §1.5, D13, D14

How it works

Five mechanisms, in the order the run meets them: the reader keeps each arc; the tracer offers a set of chains and a worker trims it; code pairs the curb's two edges, finds which lot edge fronts a street, and derives the driveway between the curb faces that cross it; and, after anchoring, a new step fetches what Austin publishes within 300 ft and the join is one query (§1.4, §2.2–§2.7).

### 2. Impervious Cover Limits

**Citation:** Austin LDC Sec. 25-8-392 (Uplands Zone, Article 9 -- Suburban Watershed Requirements)
**Finding:** In the suburban watershed uplands zone, impervious cover limits for residential uses are as follows: single-family residential (lot >= 5,750 sq ft) may not exceed **50%** of the net site area; duplex or single-family on a lot smaller than 5,750 sq ft may not exceed **55-60%** (with transfer of development intensity). For a duplex on a standard Scofield subdivision lot (typically ~7,000-8,000 sq ft), the governing IC limit is expected to be **45-50% of net site area** absent any transfer. The more restrictive of the zoning IC limit and the watershed IC limit controls. IC limits in suburban watersheds apply at the subdivision level for platted lots, so the individual lot is governed by the subdivision-wide compliance framework.
**Confidence:** Verified (exact lot-specific percentage requires site plan review by Watershed Protection staff)

## Procedural Pathway

### Site Plan Exemption

Under Austin LDC, **residential uses of four or fewer units are exempt from site plan requirements**. A duplex (2 units) qualifies for this exemption. No administrative site plan is required.

### Building Permit

The duplex can proceed via a **residential building permit** application through Austin Development Services (DSD). Under HOME Phase 1, applications have been accepted since February 5, 2024.

**Permit review path:**

1. **Residential Plan Review** -- submit building plans through Austin DSD
2. Plans reviewed for compliance with LDC dimensional standards, IRC/IBC building codes, and applicable environmental/drainage requirements
3. No zoning change, conditional use permit, or Board of Adjustment variance is needed (assuming the lot meets dimensional standards)

### Site Plan Lite (HOME Phase 2 -- March 2025)

Site Plan Lite is a streamlined review process adopted March 6, 2025, primarily for HOME Phase 2 subdivision and small-lot projects. It is **not required** for a duplex on an existing lot that already meets SF-2 minimum lot size. However, if the owner later pursues subdivision of the lot (e.g., into two small lots), Site Plan Lite would apply.

### Demolition Permit

A separate **demolition permit** is required to remove the existing house. Austin DSD processes demo permits administratively; typical turnaround is 2-4 weeks. Asbestos survey may be required for pre-2000 construction (this house was built 1993).

### Tree Permits

Austin's tree ordinance (LDC Ch. 25-8, Subchapter B) protects trees with trunk diameter >= 19 inches (heritage trees >= 24 inches). A tree survey may be required, and mitigation fees or replacement may apply if protected trees must be removed.

## 4. Legal Framework: Deed Restrictions vs. Zoning

### Texas Law

| Principle | Detail |
|---|---|
| Private enforcement | CC&Rs are private contracts enforceable by the HOA or individual lot owners through civil courts, not through city code enforcement |
| City cannot override | The City of Austin cannot waive or override private deed restrictions through zoning changes, including HOME Initiative amendments |
| Statute of limitations | Under Texas Property Code § 202.003, restrictive covenants in subdivisions with a property owners' association may be enforced indefinitely (no automatic expiration) |
| Amendment process | CC&Rs can typically be amended by a vote of the membership (usually requiring a supermajority, e.g., 67% or 75% of lot owners), or by petition under Texas Property Code Chapter 209 |

### HOME Initiative and Deed Restrictions

The HOME Initiative Phase 1 (Ord. 20231207-001) amended Austin's Land Development Code to permit duplexes by right on SF-2 lots. However:

- **HOME does not preempt private deed restrictions.** If the Scofield Farms CC&Rs restrict lots to single-family use, a duplex remains prohibited as a matter of private covenant regardless of city zoning.
- The city will issue a building permit for a duplex if the project meets zoning and code requirements — but the permit does not immunize the owner from HOA enforcement action.
- The HOA or individual lot owners could seek injunctive relief (a court order to stop or reverse construction) if the project violates the CC&Rs.

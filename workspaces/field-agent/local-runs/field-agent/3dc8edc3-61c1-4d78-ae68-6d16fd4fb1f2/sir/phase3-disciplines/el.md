# Environmental Findings — 12713 Cinchring Ln

**Property:** 12713 Cinchring Ln, Austin, TX 78727 — Lot 12, Block M, Scofield Subdivision
**Proposed use:** Demolish existing SFR (built 1993); build ground-up duplex (2 units)
**Lot:** ~7,840 sq ft (0.18 acres), SF-2 zoning
**Watershed:** Walnut Creek — Suburban classification
**Date:** 2026-06-03

---

## Summary

- **No high-severity environmental barriers** to the proposed duplex. The site is outside the Edwards Aquifer recharge/contributing zone (no EAPP), outside the 100-year and 500-year floodplain (Zone X), and carries no known Critical Environmental Features.
- **Asbestos survey is required before demolition** of the 1993 house under TCEQ NESHAP regulations (40 CFR 61, Subpart M); lead-based paint screening is also recommended given the pre-1996 construction date.
- **No Phase I ESA has been performed** — a recognized data gap. Residential use history suggests low contamination risk, but a Phase I is recommended before demolition to document the absence of Recognized Environmental Conditions and satisfy lender/investor due diligence.
- **BCCP participation fee applies** at building permit issuance; no individual USFWS Section 7 or Section 10 consultation is anticipated.
- **Tree protection, floodplain, and stormwater** matters are cross-referenced to their respective disciplines (eptp, fwp, sde) and are not duplicated here.

## Findings

### 1. Walnut Creek Suburban Watershed Classification

**Severity:** note
**Citation:** Austin LDC Ch. 25-8, Sec. 25-8-2; Ch. 25-8 Article 9 (Suburban Watershed Requirements)
**Finding:** The property lies within the Walnut Creek watershed, classified as Suburban under Chapter 25-8. Suburban watershed rules (Article 9) govern impervious cover limits and water quality controls. For this lot, the SF-2 zoning IC limit of 45% is expected to control over the watershed IC limit, but the more restrictive of the two applies. Water quality controls (sedimentation/filtration per ECM Sec. 1.6.0) are required for redevelopment; however, the Scofield subdivision may already be served by regional water quality facilities, which could satisfy the lot-level obligation.
**Implication for the developer:** The suburban watershed classification does not block the project but establishes the regulatory framework for impervious cover and water quality. Confirm whether existing regional controls serve this lot — if not, an on-site SCM may be required.
**Recommended next step:** Confirm lot-level IC limit and regional WQ facility coverage with the Watershed Protection Department during pre-application. Cross-reference stormwater controls to the **sde** discipline.

### 2. Not in Edwards Aquifer Recharge or Contributing Zone

**Severity:** note
**Citation:** TCEQ 30 TAC Chapter 213; Travis County Aquifer Maps
**Finding:** The property is not within the Edwards Aquifer Recharge Zone (EARZ) or Contributing Zone. The EARZ in Travis County is concentrated south of the Colorado River over exposed Edwards Group limestones. North Austin (Scofield area) overlies different geologic formations. No TCEQ Edwards Aquifer Protection Plan (EAPP) is required.
**Implication for the developer:** Eliminates the EAPP application, associated engineering, and TCEQ review timeline from the project scope. This is favorable.
**Recommended next step:** None required.

### 3. No Known Critical Environmental Features

**Severity:** note
**Citation:** Austin LDC Sec. 25-8-281 et seq.; ECM Sec. 1.10.0; City of Austin Watershed Protection karst database
**Finding:** CEFs in Austin include springs, caves, sinkholes, point recharge features, rimrock, and bluffs. No known CEFs have been identified at or immediately adjacent to 12713 Cinchring Ln. The site's geology (Cretaceous formations in north Austin, not Edwards Limestone) carries low probability of karst features. The City's karst database (~550 points) is concentrated in western and southwestern Austin.
**Implication for the developer:** Low risk. However, an Environmental Resource Inventory (ERI) form is required as part of the building permit application, and Watershed Protection staff will make the formal CEF determination during review. If a CEF were identified, a 150-ft to 300-ft protective buffer would encumber the lot — on a 7,840 sq ft parcel, this would be project-fatal.
**Recommended next step:** Complete the ERI form at permitting. No pre-application CEF investigation is warranted given the low geological probability.

**Severity:** note

**Finding:** The property is within the BCCP participation area. **Implication for the developer:** The fee is a modest line item (typically a few hundred dollars for residential development). No individual USFWS consultation is needed.
**Recommended next step:** Budget for the BCCP fee. Confirm current fee schedule with DSD at time of permit application.

### 6. Phase I ESA Not Performed

**Severity:** data-gap
**Citation:** ASTM E1527-21 (Standard Practice for Environmental Site Assessments)
**Finding:** No Phase I ESA has been commissioned for the property. The site has been in single-family residential use since the subdivision was developed in the early 1990s; prior use was rangeland/agricultural. No TCEQ State Superfund, EPA NPL, or LUST sites have been identified at this address in available databases. The contamination risk profile is low. However, without a Phase I, potential concerns — historical agricultural chemicals, adjacent-parcel USTs, vapor intrusion — cannot be formally excluded.
**Implication for the developer:** Most lenders and investors require a Phase I ESA for acquisition or construction financing. The absence of a Phase I does not block permitting but creates a due-diligence gap. The low-risk residential history makes an adverse finding unlikely, but the assessment is the documentation vehicle.
**Recommended next step:** Commission a Phase I ESA (ASTM E1527-21) before demolition. This can run concurrent with design; typical turnaround is 3-4 weeks, cost $2,000-$4,000.

### 7. Asbestos Survey Required Before Demolition (TCEQ NESHAP)

**Severity:** moderate
**Citation:** 40 CFR 61, Subpart M (National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants — Asbestos); TCEQ Asbestos Program; Texas DSHS Asbestos Health Protection Rules (25 TAC 295)
**Finding:** The existing house was built in 1993. Asbestos-containing materials (ACM) were used in residential construction through the mid-1990s, particularly in floor tiles, joint compound, roofing materials, pipe insulation, and textite siding. Under the federal NESHAP rule, the owner/operator must conduct a thorough asbestos inspection of a facility before demolition, performed by a Texas-licensed asbestos inspector. If ACM is found, abatement must be completed by a licensed contractor before demolition, and a 10-working-day notification to TCEQ is required prior to the start of demolition or abatement.
**Implication for the developer:** This is a hard prerequisite to demolition. The inspection adds 1-2 weeks; if ACM is found, abatement adds 2-4 weeks and $3,000-$15,000+ depending on materials and quantities. Schedule the inspection early.
**Recommended next step:** Engage a Texas-licensed asbestos inspector to survey the existing structure. If ACM is identified, obtain abatement bids and factor cost/schedule into the demolition plan. File the TCEQ 10-day notification before any demolition activity.

### 8. Lead-Based Paint Screening Recommended

**Severity:** note
**Citation:** 40 CFR 745 (Lead-Based Paint); HUD/EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) Rule; Texas DSHS 25 TAC 295 Subchapter I
**Finding:** The house was built in 1993. The Consumer Product Safety Commission banned lead-based paint for residential use in 1978, so the structure is unlikely to contain LBP. However, lead can be present in plumbing solder (pre-1986 ban) and in some specialty coatings. For a full demolition, lead risk is minimal but should be addressed as part of the asbestos/hazmat survey scope.
**Implication for the developer:** Low likelihood of encountering LBP. Including lead screening in the asbestos survey scope adds negligible marginal cost.
**Recommended next step:** Include LBP screening in the asbestos inspection scope as a precautionary measure.

### 12. Soil Conditions — High Shrink-Swell Clay

**Severity:** note
**Citation:** USDA Web Soil Survey; Blackland Prairie ecological region data
**Finding:** The site is in the Blackland Prairie region with deep, dark, clay-rich Vertisol soils (likely Houston Black clay series). These soils have high shrink-swell potential, expanding when wet and contracting when dry. This is the dominant foundation risk in the Austin area.
**Implication for the developer:** An engineered foundation (post-tensioned slab or drilled piers) is required. A geotechnical report should inform foundation design.
**Recommended next step:** Commission a geotechnical investigation before foundation design. Typical cost $2,500-$5,000; turnaround 2-3 weeks.

## C. Regulatory Analysis Detail

### C.1 Zoning Analysis

**Methodology:** The base zoning was inferred from lot size analysis:
- SF-2 minimum lot size = 10,000 SF
- SF-3 minimum lot size = 5,750 SF
- Subject lot = 8,054 SF
- Conclusion: Lot meets SF-3 minimums but not SF-2 minimums → likely SF-3

**Limitation:** This is an inference, not a confirmed zoning designation. The property could be SF-2 with a pre-existing non-conforming lot size (if the subdivision was platted before SF-2 minimums were established or under a different standard). GIS confirmation is required.

### C.2 HOME Amendments Analysis

**Source:** Austin Development Services HOME Amendments page; LDC Chapter 25-2

- Not directly relevant to this duplex project

### C.3 Dimensional Envelope Calculation

Based on 8,054 SF lot:

| Parameter | Calculation | Result |
|-----------|------------|--------|
| Max GFA (0.40:1 FAR) | 8,054 × 0.40 | 3,222 SF |
| Max building coverage (40%) | 8,054 × 0.40 | 3,222 SF |
| Max impervious cover (45%) | 8,054 × 0.45 | 3,624 SF |
| Available IC for paving | 3,624 - 3,222 = | 402 SF |

**Note:** The available paving area (402 SF) after maximum building coverage is very tight. This assumes building footprint equals building coverage. A smaller building footprint with a two-story design would free up more IC for driveways, walkways, and patios.

**Two-story scenario (1,611 SF footprint, 3,222 SF GFA):**
- Building coverage: 1,611 SF (20% of lot) — well within 40% limit
- Available IC for paving: 3,624 - 1,611 = 2,013 SF — much more workable

### C.5 Easement Impact Analysis

**5-ft P.U.E.:** Standard utility easement along lot boundaries. Buildings cannot be placed within. This effectively adds 5 ft to side or rear setbacks where the P.U.E. runs. If P.U.E. is on both side lot lines, buildable width reduces to 50 - 10 = 40 ft.

**25-ft Building Line:** This is the front building setback line, consistent with the SF-3 25-ft front setback. No additional constraint.

**Blanket Electric Easement (Vol. 660, Pg. 968):** A blanket easement grants the utility provider rights across the entire property. Depending on the specific language, this could:
- Restrict permanent structures across the entire lot (worst case)
- Grant access rights only, with structures permitted subject to notification (more common)
- Apply only to a specific area despite "blanket" characterization

**Declarant's 10-ft Easement Reservation (Vol. 11863, Pg. 1147):** If the declarant (or successor) has exercised this right, up to 10 feet on each side of each lot line could be encumbered for public utility purposes. Combined with the 5-ft P.U.E., side-yard constraints could extend to 15 ft from each side lot line. This is a significant potential constraint.

**Zone X (unshaded):** Areas determined to be outside the 0.2% annual chance floodplain (500-year). This is the most favorable flood zone designation.

The current effective FIRM panels should be confirmed via FEMA's Map Service Center (msc.fema.gov) or a FEMA NFHL query. However, the Scofield Farms area in North Austin is generally upland terrain without significant flood risk.

### C.7 Watershed Classification Inference

**Walnut Creek Watershed:** The property's location east of Metric Boulevard in North Austin places it within the Walnut Creek watershed system. Walnut Creek flows from northern Austin to the Colorado River and drains approximately 43 square miles.

**Development Zone:** North Austin areas like Scofield Farms are typically classified as Suburban or Urban watershed development zones under the ECM. The distinction matters for:
- Cut/fill disclosure requirements (exempt in Urban, required in Suburban)
- Impervious cover limits per watershed (Urban/Suburban are less restrictive than DWPZ)
- Water quality control sizing

### C.11 Tree Risk Assessment

**No field data available.** Risk assessment based on property characteristics:

- Lot developed in 1993 — approximately 33 years of tree growth
- Scofield Farms was developed as a residential subdivision with typical landscaping
- Common tree species in North Austin include Live Oak, Red Oak, Cedar Elm, Pecan
- Heritage species (per LDC § 25-8-602): Texas Ash, Bald Cypress, American Elm, Cedar Elm, Texas Madrone, Bigtooth Maple, all Oaks, Pecan, Arizona Walnut, Eastern Black Walnut

**Risk factors:**
- A 33-year-old Live Oak or Red Oak could easily be 19-24+ inches DBH → protected or heritage status
- Heritage tree removal requires LDC § 25-8-642 or § 25-8-643 variance (4-8 months)
- CRZ radius = DBH in inches → a 24" tree has a 24-ft CRZ that could consume a significant portion of this lot
- Mitigation for heritage tree removal: 300% of removed caliper inches at $200/inch = potentially $14,400+ per heritage tree

**Recommendation:** Commission tree survey before purchasing or designing. Trees are the most likely physical constraint on this lot.

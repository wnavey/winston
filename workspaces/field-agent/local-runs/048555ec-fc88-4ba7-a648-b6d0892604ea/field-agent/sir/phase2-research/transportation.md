## 1. ASMP Street Classification -- Cameron Road

**Finding:** Cameron Road is classified as **ASMP Street Level 4** in the Austin Strategic Mobility Plan Street Network. Level 4 streets are designated for **"Corridor Mobility"** -- the second-highest tier in Austin's five-level street hierarchy. The ASMP GIS layer confirms the classification applies to segments in the vicinity of 9101 Cameron Rd. The street currently has **6 travel lanes** (existing configuration), and the future cross-section also specifies 6 travel lanes. The project type is listed as "Corridor Mobility Improvements" involving "mobility, safety, and connectivity improvements to accommodate multiple modes of transportation, including driving, walking, biking, and taking transit."

**Target Cross-Section:** The ASMP data indicates a future cross-section maintaining 6 travel lanes with addition of protected bicycle facilities and enhanced pedestrian infrastructure. The Cameron Road / Dessau Road Safety Project (funded through the 2020 Mobility Bond and a 2022 Safe Streets and Roads for All federal grant) proposes reconfiguring from three lanes in each direction to two lanes in each direction between US 290 and Parmer Lane, with strategic turn lanes retained at major intersections. Note: this reconfiguration primarily applies to the segment north of US 183; the segment near 9101 Cameron Rd (south of US 183) may retain its existing 6-lane configuration.

**Required ROW Width:** The ASMP GIS data shows a **required ROW of 154 feet** for Cameron Road segments in this area. Current mean ROW ranges from approximately **98.6 to 116 feet** depending on the segment.

**Citation:** City of Austin, ASMP Street Network GIS Layer (ID: 7), queried via ArcGIS REST API; Cameron Road / Dessau Road Safety Project StoryMap (storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/b8bec12a854d45c9b8b4721ff73f5915).

**Development Implication:** The gap between existing ROW (approximately 99--116 ft) and the 154 ft target ROW means ROW dedication will almost certainly be required at time of development. This is consistent with the concept plan notation of "Potential ROW Dedication" and "25' R.O.W. Reserve (Doc. No. 201800004)." The Level 4 classification triggers more stringent access management requirements and may limit the number and type of driveways.

**Confidence:** HIGH -- Classification confirmed via direct GIS query of the ASMP layer; ROW data from same source.

- **Target ROW:** 154 feet
- **Dedication needed:** Approximately 19--27.5 feet per side (assuming symmetric dedication from current conditions), though the actual amount will depend on the specific segment geometry and which side of the roadway is deficient.

The ASMP coordination notes state that "ROW dedication may be necessary through the land development process for new development and commercial redevelopment."

Additionally, the Cameron Road / Dessau Road Safety Project is actively under construction (groundbreaking October 2025), and the City may use the site plan review process to secure additional ROW from this parcel to support corridor improvements.

**Citation:** ASMP Street Network GIS Layer; Cameron/Dessau Safety Project FAQ (austintexas.gov/sites/default/files/files/TPW/Bond/Cameron-Dessau_FAQs_ENG_10-2024.pdf); concept plan reference to Doc. No. 201800004.

A 25-foot dedication (as shown on the concept plan) would reduce the available site depth. The dedication will need to be conveyed at no cost to the City as a condition of site plan approval. The building footprint, parking layout, and stormwater management areas must be designed to accommodate the post-dedication parcel geometry.

**Confidence:** HIGH -- Concept plan already shows the dedication; ASMP data confirms the ROW gap.

## 3. Traffic Impact Analysis (TIA)

**Finding:** A 7,375 SF AutoZone retail store is **unlikely to trigger a full TIA** under current City of Austin thresholds.

**Threshold:** The City of Austin requires a TIA when a proposed development generates more than **2,000 vehicle trips per day**. A full TIA is required for projects generating more than **5,000 vehicle trips per day**; projects between 2,000 and 5,000 trips/day may be subject to a smaller traffic assessment focused on sustainable modes and Transportation Demand Management (TDM).

**Trip Generation Estimate (ITE Method):**
- **ITE Land Use Code 843:** Automobile Parts Sales
- **Daily trip rate:** 61.91 trips per 1,000 SF (ITE Trip Generation Manual, 11th Edition)
- **Estimated daily trips:** 7.375 x 61.91 = **approximately 456 daily vehicle trips**
- **PM peak hour rate:** Approximately 5.98 trips per 1,000 SF = **approximately 44 PM peak hour trips**
- **Pass-by trip adjustment:** Auto parts stores typically have a high pass-by rate (approximately 40--50%), which would reduce net new trips to approximately 228--274 net new daily trips.

At approximately 456 gross daily trips (or ~250 net new trips after pass-by adjustment), this project falls **well below** the 2,000-trip TIA threshold.

**Process:** The applicant must still submit a **TIA Determination Worksheet** to the Transportation Development Services (TDS) Division's TIA Determination Portal. TDS will review and formally confirm whether a TIA is required. Even if no TIA is required, the City may still impose conditions related to driveway design, sight distance, and pedestrian/bicycle connectivity.

**Citation:** City of Austin TIA Guidelines (June 2022); Austin Transportation Criteria Manual Section 10; ITE Trip Generation Manual, 11th Edition, Land Use Code 843; RPS Group, "Traffic Impact Analysis in Austin Texas" (rpsgroup.com/insights/nam/traffic-impact-analysis-in-austin-texas/).

**Development Implication:** No TIA is expected to be required, which reduces consulting costs and review timelines. However, the TIA Determination Worksheet must still be submitted. The applicant should confirm with TDS early in the process. The Cameron Road corridor's high-crash history and ongoing safety project may lead the City to impose additional access management conditions regardless of trip count.

**Confidence:** HIGH -- Trip generation calculation is straightforward; threshold is well-established.

## 4. Driveway Access and Access Management

**Finding:** As a Level 4 street, Cameron Road is subject to the City of Austin's most stringent driveway access management standards in the Transportation Criteria Manual (TCM), Section 7.

**Key Standards for Level 4 Streets:**
- **Minor residential driveways** are prohibited on Level 3 and Level 4 streets.
- **Commercial driveway types:** Only Type II and Type III (commercial) driveways are permitted.
- **Driveway spacing (driveway-to-driveway):** All Type II and III driveways on undivided arterial streets must align with opposing streets or driveways or be offset by a minimum of **120 feet** (edge to edge). For divided arterials, greater spacing is generally required.
- **Corner clearance (driveway-to-intersection):** Type I driveways must be located no closer to the corner of intersecting ROW than 60% of parcel frontage or 50 feet (whichever is less). All other driveways must be no closer than 60% of parcel frontage or **100 feet** (whichever is less).
- **Number of driveways:** The TCM encourages minimizing driveways on arterial streets. Joint access driveways and cross-access easements to adjoining properties are preferred where feasible.

**Cameron Road Safety Project -- Access Management:** The ongoing Cameron/Dessau Road Safety Project includes **access management** as a core safety strategy. Planned improvements include raised medians at strategic locations to limit risky driveway left turns, and general reduction in conflict points. This may affect the type of turning movements permitted at the property's driveway (e.g., right-in/right-out only if a raised median is installed).

**Citation:** Austin Transportation Criteria Manual, Section 7 (Driveways); Cameron/Dessau Road Safety Project StoryMap.

**Development Implication:** The single driveway from Cameron Road shown on the concept plan is appropriate -- minimizing driveways is consistent with TCM requirements. The applicant should confirm that the proposed driveway location meets the minimum 100-foot corner clearance from the nearest intersection. If the Cameron Road Safety Project installs a raised median in front of this property, the driveway may be restricted to right-in/right-out movements, which would affect delivery truck access (trucks approaching from the north could not make a left turn into the site). The truck turn exhibit should account for this potential constraint.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH -- TCM standards are established; uncertainty exists around whether the safety project's median placement will affect this specific property.

## 5. AADT (Annual Average Daily Traffic)

**Finding:** Specific AADT counts for Cameron Road at or near 9101 Cameron Rd were not available through publicly accessible online databases at the time of this research. Cameron Road is a city-maintained road and does not appear in TxDOT's AADT publication (which covers only state-system roads). The City of Austin and CAMPO (Capital Area Metropolitan Planning Organization) maintain traffic count programs, but their data was not accessible through web queries.

**Proxy Data -- Crash Volume as Traffic Indicator:** The Cameron/Dessau Road Safety Project reports **nearly 2,400 crashes between 2019 and 2023** on the Cameron Road / Dessau Road corridor between East 51st Street and Parmer Lane, averaging more than one crash per day. Speed data from March 2024 recorded over **2,900 vehicles exceeding 50 mph** in a single 24-hour period on Cameron Road (posted speed limit: 40 mph). This crash frequency and speeding volume is consistent with a high-volume urban arterial typically carrying **20,000--30,000+ AADT**, though the exact count requires confirmation from city traffic engineering records.

The corridor's 6-lane cross section (3 lanes each direction) further supports a high-volume classification. A 6-lane urban arterial in Austin typically carries between 25,000 and 45,000 vehicles per day.

**Citation:** Cameron/Dessau Road Safety Project StoryMap; KXAN news reports on Cameron Road safety (kxan.com/traffic/traffic-projects/cameron-road-safety-improvements-now-underway/); TxDOT AADT public data portal (gis-txdot.opendata.arcgis.com).

**Development Implication:** The high traffic volume supports the commercial viability of the AutoZone location (high drive-by visibility and convenience access). However, the high crash rate and speeding data mean the City will scrutinize driveway design, sight distance, and pedestrian safety. The applicant should request the official AADT count from Austin Transportation and Public Works for use in the TIA Determination Worksheet.

**Confidence:** LOW-MEDIUM for specific AADT number (estimated range only); HIGH for the general characterization as a high-volume arterial.

## 6. TxDOT Jurisdiction

**Finding:** Cameron Road does **not** appear to be a TxDOT-maintained roadway. It is not designated as a US Highway, State Highway, Farm-to-Market Road, or Ranch-to-Market Road. Cameron Road is a **City of Austin-maintained** arterial street. It does not appear in TxDOT's roadway inventory or AADT count publications (which cover only state-system roads).

However, the ASMP coordination notes for Cameron Road state that **coordination with TxDOT is required** for certain segments, likely because Cameron Road intersects with TxDOT-maintained facilities (US 183 / Research Boulevard and US 290 / IH-35). Any improvements at or near those intersections would require TxDOT coordination.

The property at 9101 Cameron Rd is approximately 0.5 miles south of the US 183 interchange, so TxDOT involvement in the driveway permit or site plan is unlikely unless the traffic analysis extends to the US 183 intersection.

**Citation:** TxDOT roadway inventory and departmental maps (txdot.gov); ASMP Street Network GIS Layer coordination notes.

**Development Implication:** No TxDOT driveway permit is required. The site plan and driveway permit will be reviewed solely by the City of Austin Transportation Development Services. This simplifies the permitting process compared to sites on TxDOT roads, which require a separate TxDOT access management review.

**Confidence:** HIGH -- Cameron Road is not on TxDOT's state highway system.

## 7. Scenic Roadway Overlay

**Finding:** Cameron Road north of US 183 is included on the City of Austin's list of designated **scenic roadways**. However, the property at 9101 Cameron Rd is located **south of US 183**, and the scenic designation applies specifically to the segment **north of US 183**. Therefore, the scenic roadway overlay **likely does not apply** to this property.

If the scenic overlay were applicable, it would trigger:
- **Sign restrictions** under Section 25-10-124 of the Land Development Code: only one freestanding sign per lot, maximum height of 12 feet; total sign area for non-freestanding signs may not exceed 10% of the facade area of the first 15 feet of the building.
- **Landscaping requirements** under Section 25-2-1006: enhanced vegetative screening along the roadway frontage.

The applicant should confirm with the City of Austin Development Services Department whether the scenic roadway designation boundary is precisely at the US 183 interchange or extends any distance south of it.

**Citation:** City of Austin scenic roadway designation list (services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=98558); Austin Land Development Code Sections 25-10-124 and 25-2-1006; City of Austin Property Profile GIS scenic roadway overlay layer.

**Development Implication:** If the scenic overlay does not apply (as expected for this location south of US 183), AutoZone's standard signage and frontage design would be governed by the CS zoning district sign regulations rather than the more restrictive scenic roadway standards. The applicant should obtain written confirmation of non-applicability from the City.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM -- The "north of US 183" boundary is referenced in available documentation, but the exact demarcation point should be verified with the City. The property is close to US 183, so boundary ambiguity could be a factor.

## 8. Bicycle and Pedestrian Facilities

**Finding:** The ASMP data and Cameron Road Safety Project plans identify significant bicycle and pedestrian improvements for this corridor:

**Bicycle Facilities:**
- **Existing:** Shared lane (no dedicated bicycle infrastructure)
- **Future (ASMP target):** **Protected bike lane ("Bike Lane - Protected One")** described as providing "all ages and abilities bicycle facilities"
- The Cameron Road Safety Project includes protected bicycle lanes throughout the corridor.

**Pedestrian Facilities:**
- The Safety Project includes new/enhanced pedestrian crossings at multiple locations, ADA-compliant sidewalk upgrades, and improved street lighting between 51st Street and Howard Lane.
- The applicant will likely be required to construct **sidewalks** along the Cameron Road frontage consistent with current TCM standards (typically 6-foot minimum sidewalk width with a landscape buffer zone between the sidewalk and curb).
- The applicant may also be required to construct or provide space for the future protected bike lane within the ROW being dedicated.

**Citation:** ASMP Street Network GIS Layer (bicycle facility fields); Cameron/Dessau Road Safety Project StoryMap.

**Development Implication:** The site plan must include sidewalk construction along the full Cameron Road frontage, meeting ADA standards. The ROW dedication must accommodate the future protected bike lane cross-section. The applicant may be required to construct the protected bike lane segment along its frontage, or to provide the graded/prepared ROW for future construction by the City. These improvements will add to site development costs. The truck turn exhibit should demonstrate that delivery vehicles can navigate the driveway without conflicting with the bike lane and pedestrian crossings.

**Confidence:** HIGH -- ASMP data and Safety Project plans are consistent and publicly documented.

## 9. Transit (CapMetro Service)

**Finding:** Cameron Road is served by multiple CapMetro bus routes:

- **Route 300 (Springdale/Oltorf):** This is a **high-frequency route** operating every 15--30 minutes from 6 AM to 8 PM, 7 days per week. Route 300 runs along Cameron Road from Crestview Station to West Gate, serving Pleasant Valley, Oltorf, and South Lamar. This is one of CapMetro's 14 high-frequency network routes.
- **Route 339 (Tuscany):** Serves stops on Cameron Road including a stop at **8100 Cameron Road (Stop ID: 4720)** and at **1601 Rutherford / Cameron**. Route 339 runs between Norwood Transit Center and Tannehill / Webberville with approximately 23 stops.
- **Route 485 (Night Owl 7th Street/Cameron):** Provides late-night service on the Cameron Road corridor.

The nearest bus stop to 9101 Cameron Rd is at **8100 Cameron Road (Stop ID: 4720)**, approximately 0.2 miles south. The Cameron Road Safety Project includes bus stop relocations to improve proximity to safe crossings.

**Citation:** CapMetro high-frequency network page (capmetro.org/high-frequency); CapMetro Route 339 schedule (capmetro.org/plan/schedmap?route=339); Moovit transit data; CapMetro stop detail for Stop ID 4720.

**Development Implication:** The high-frequency transit service is a positive factor for both customers and employees. The site plan should ensure safe pedestrian access from the nearest bus stop to the store entrance. CapMetro may request that a bus pull-out or shelter be accommodated within the ROW dedication if the Cameron Road Safety Project relocates a stop to be adjacent to this property. The applicant should coordinate with CapMetro to confirm whether any transit improvements are planned for the frontage.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH -- Route information confirmed through multiple sources; exact stop locations near 9101 may shift due to the ongoing safety project.

## 10. Pecan Springs Road -- Western Boundary Access

**Finding:** Pecan Springs Road is classified as **ASMP Street Level 1** -- the lowest tier in Austin's street hierarchy, designated for **"Local Mobility."** Key characteristics from the ASMP GIS data:

- **Existing cross-section:** "2U-N" (two-lane undivided, rural/substandard) on most segments
- **Future cross-section:** "2U-OP" (two-lane undivided, urban standard)
- **Required ROW:** 58 to 64 feet
- **Existing mean ROW:** 50.7--92.7 feet (varies by segment)
- **Improvements planned:** Reconstruction of substandard street segments to urban standards; sidewalk additions on some segments; two segments designated as "Bicycle Priority" with protected bike lanes planned
- **Design approach:** Improvements constrained to existing ROW where possible using "flexible design criteria"

**Access Implications:** As a Level 1 (local) street, Pecan Springs Road has less restrictive access management requirements than Cameron Road (Level 4). Driveways onto Level 1 streets are generally permitted with fewer spacing restrictions. If the site has secondary access from the west/southwest area near Pecan Springs Road, this access point may be subject to fewer constraints than the Cameron Road driveway.

However, the concept plan's primary access is from Cameron Road, and any secondary access to the west would need to be evaluated for connectivity to the public street network, topographic feasibility, and neighborhood traffic impacts.

**Citation:** ASMP Street Network GIS Layer, Pecan Springs Road segments query.

**Development Implication:** Secondary access from Pecan Springs Road (if applicable) could provide an alternative route for delivery trucks, reducing reliance on the Cameron Road driveway. This would be particularly valuable if the Cameron Road Safety Project restricts the main driveway to right-in/right-out. However, routing commercial traffic through a Level 1 residential-character street may face neighborhood opposition and could trigger additional review. The applicant should clarify whether the western access shown on the concept plan connects to Pecan Springs Road or to another internal drive/easement.

**Confidence:** HIGH for Pecan Springs Road classification; MEDIUM for access implications (depends on actual site connectivity shown on concept plan).

## 1. Watershed Classification

**Finding:** The property at 9101 Cameron Rd is located within the **Little Walnut Creek watershed**, which is classified as an **Urban Watershed** under the City of Austin's Watershed Protection Ordinance (WPO). Little Walnut Creek is a tributary of Walnut Creek, which drains approximately 43 square miles of northern Austin before discharging to the Colorado River. The Urban Watershed classification was established by the Urban Watersheds Ordinance (UWO) adopted in 1991 and later incorporated into the Comprehensive Watersheds Ordinance framework.

Urban Watersheds in Austin are governed by LDC Chapter 25-8, Subchapter A, Article 8 (Urban Watershed Requirements). The urban designation reflects the heavily developed character of the surrounding area and applies the least restrictive tier of watershed regulations among Austin's four categories (Urban, Suburban, Water Supply Suburban, Water Supply Rural).

**Citation:** Austin LDC Ch. 25-8, Subchapter A, Art. 8 (Urban Watershed Requirements); City of Austin Watershed Regulation Areas GIS layer (data.austintexas.gov, dataset 2xkn-3rmn); City of Austin Watershed Boundaries dataset (yfaf-uvsu).

**Development Impact:** As an Urban Watershed site, the property is subject to water quality controls for new impervious cover but benefits from the most permissive impervious cover regime. The key regulatory burden is the requirement for structural water quality treatment rather than impervious cover caps.

**Confidence:** Verified -- Little Walnut Creek is consistently identified as an Urban Watershed in City of Austin regulatory materials and GIS datasets.

## 2. Critical Environmental Features (CEFs)

**Finding:** The concept plan for 9101 Cameron Rd shows **CEF setback (CEF STBK) lines** on the eastern and northeastern portions of the site. Under Austin's Environmental Criteria Manual (ECM) Section 1.10.0 and LDC Section 25-8-281, Critical Environmental Features include springs, seeps, bluffs, canyon rimrock, caves, sinkholes, and wetlands. The presence of CEF setback lines on the concept plan indicates that at least one mapped or field-identified CEF exists on or adjacent to the parcel.

Given the site's topography (gentle slope NE to SW, elevation 626-636 ft) and the presence of mapped CWQZ boundaries, the CEFs on or near this site are most likely **springs or seeps** associated with the drainage features crossing the property, rather than bluffs or canyon rimrock (which require 40+ ft vertical relief at steep grades, not present here).

**Standard CEF buffer requirements (ECM 1.10.4):**
- Default buffer: **150 feet** from the edge of the CEF
- Springs/seeps: may be administratively reduced to a minimum of **50 feet** with site-specific hydrogeologic justification demonstrating preservation of flow quantity and quality
- Reductions below 50 feet require a **Land Use Commission variance** per LDC 25-8-41

**Citation:** Austin LDC Section 25-8-281 (Critical Environmental Features); ECM Section 1.10.0, 1.10.3, 1.10.4 (CEF Identification and Protection); City of Austin Biologic Resource Buffers (CEF Setbacks) GIS layer (data.austintexas.gov, dataset n7cy-835m).

**Development Impact:** The CEF setback areas shown on the eastern/northeastern portion of the site are **non-buildable** unless a variance or administrative reduction is obtained. The proposed building footprint appears to be positioned west of the CEF setback lines, which is appropriate. Any grading, paving, or utility installation within the CEF buffer is prohibited absent an approved exception. A field survey and Environmental Resource Inventory (ERI) will be required to confirm CEF locations and negotiate final buffer dimensions with the Watershed Protection Department.

**Confidence:** Verified (CEF setbacks shown on concept plan) / Inferred (specific CEF type likely spring or seep based on terrain).

## 3. Critical Water Quality Zone (CWQZ)

**Finding:** The concept plan confirms that **CWQZ boundaries cross the site in multiple locations**. Under LDC Section 25-8-92, the Critical Water Quality Zone is defined as the area coinciding with the 100-year fully-developed-conditions floodplain, subject to minimum and maximum width constraints measured from the waterway centerline:

| Waterway Classification | Minimum CWQZ Width | Maximum CWQZ Width |
|---|---|---|
| Minor waterway | 50 ft from centerline | 100 ft from centerline |
| Intermediate waterway | 100 ft from centerline | 200 ft from centerline |
| Major waterway | 200 ft from centerline | 400 ft from centerline |

Given the site's drainage characteristics (existing grate inlets, 24" storm line, gentle slope), the waterway features on this parcel are most likely classified as **minor waterways**, establishing CWQZ widths of 50-100 feet from the centerline.

**CWQZ Development Restrictions (LDC 25-8-261):**
- **Development is prohibited** in the CWQZ except for specifically enumerated exceptions
- Permitted exceptions include: fences that do not obstruct flood flows, parks, golf courses, open space, and water-related uses
- **Utility lines and storm drains are prohibited** in the CWQZ except for necessary crossings (LDC 25-8-261(E))
- No new impervious cover is permitted within the CWQZ
- Vegetation clearing and grading are restricted

**Citation:** Austin LDC Sections 25-8-92 (CWQZ Established), 25-8-261 (CWQZ Development Restrictions); Austin Drainage Criteria Manual (fully developed conditions floodplain calculation methodology).

**Development Impact:** The CWQZ areas represent a **significant buildable-area reduction** on this 1.01-acre site. All structures, parking, and impervious surfaces must be located outside the CWQZ boundaries. The concept plan appropriately positions the proposed AutoZone building and parking west of the CWQZ areas and shows the detention/WQ pond in the northwest, away from the CWQZ. The storm line crossing the CWQZ will require a "necessary crossing" determination. A precise CWQZ delineation based on fully-developed-conditions floodplain modeling will be required as part of the site plan review.

**Confidence:** Verified -- CWQZ boundaries are shown on the concept plan and are a regulatory requirement under LDC 25-8-92.

## 4. Floodplain / FEMA Flood Zone

**Finding:** The property at 9101 Cameron Rd, Austin, TX 78753 is located on **FEMA FIRM Panel 48453C0230**. Based on the site's location in north Austin within the Little Walnut Creek watershed, the general area along Cameron Road in the 78753 zip code is predominantly mapped as **Zone X (unshaded)** -- areas determined to be outside the 0.2% annual chance (500-year) floodplain. However, the presence of CWQZ boundaries crossing the site indicates that **localized drainage features on or adjacent to the parcel may carry a Zone AE or Zone A designation** along the channel corridors.

The City of Austin also maintains its own floodplain maps through the **FloodPro** online tool (maps.austintexas.gov/floodpro/), which may show fully-developed-conditions floodplains that are more extensive than the FEMA regulatory floodplain. Austin's local floodplain regulations (LDC Chapter 25-7) apply the more restrictive of the FEMA or locally modeled floodplain.

**Citation:** FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map, Travis County, TX, Panel 48453C0230; City of Austin FloodPro mapping tool; Austin LDC Chapter 25-7 (Drainage).

**Development Impact:** The bulk of the site appears to be outside the FEMA 100-year floodplain (Zone X), but the drainage corridors crossing the site may have localized floodplain designations. The proposed building footprint should be confirmed as entirely outside both the FEMA and locally-modeled floodplains. A Floodplain Determination Letter from the City of Austin Floodplain Administrator should be obtained as part of site plan review. The preliminary detention/WQ pond shown on the concept plan in the NW portion of the site suggests on-site stormwater management to address both water quality and flood detention requirements.

**Confidence:** Inferred -- Zone X designation is typical for this area of Cameron Road but must be confirmed via FEMA FIRM review and City of Austin FloodPro for the specific parcel. CWQZ presence suggests localized flood risk along drainage corridors.

## 5. Aquifer Recharge Zone

**Finding:** The property at 9101 Cameron Rd (78753) is **NOT located within the Edwards Aquifer Recharge Zone or Contributing Zone**. The Edwards Aquifer Recharge Zone in the Austin area is concentrated in **southern Travis County and northern Hays County**, associated with the Barton Springs segment. The recharge zone boundary generally runs along a southwest-to-northeast arc through south and southwest Austin (roughly from Slaughter Lane/William Cannon area westward). The 78753 zip code in north Austin is well north and east of the mapped recharge zone boundary.

The Northern Edwards Aquifer (also referred to as the Northern Segment) does extend into portions of northwest Austin and Williamson County, but the mapped regulatory boundaries maintained by TCEQ do not extend to the Cameron Road corridor in northeast Austin.

**Citation:** TCEQ Edwards Aquifer Map Viewer (tceq.texas.gov/gis/edwards-viewer.html); City of Austin Edwards Aquifer Recharge Zone GIS layer (data.austintexas.gov, dataset ahuv-whai); TCEQ Recharge Zone Boundary (September 2005 adoption).

**Development Impact:** No Edwards Aquifer protection regulations apply to this site. The property is not subject to the Save Our Springs (SOS) Ordinance, Edwards Aquifer Protection Plans, or the enhanced water quality requirements applicable to recharge zone properties. This significantly reduces environmental permitting complexity compared to sites in south/southwest Austin.

**Confidence:** Verified -- The Edwards Aquifer Recharge Zone is geographically distant from this property. North Austin sites in the 78753 area are consistently outside the recharge zone per TCEQ and City of Austin mapping.

## 6. Erosion Hazard Zone (EHZ)

**Finding:** Under Austin's Erosion Hazard Zone regulations, a building permit application for development **within 100 feet of a creek or the Colorado River** requires an **Erosion Hazard Zone analysis**. Given that CWQZ boundaries and drainage features cross the 9101 Cameron Rd site, portions of the property within 100 feet of the waterway centerline are subject to the EHZ review requirement.

The City of Austin maintains an Erosion Hazard Zone Review Buffer GIS layer (data.austintexas.gov, dataset pmnk-72i4) and published updated EHZ guidance in 2013. If the EHZ analysis determines that the proposed building footprint falls within the erosion hazard zone, **protective measures are required**, which may include structural setbacks, bank stabilization, or other erosion mitigation.

Little Walnut Creek and its tributaries in north Austin have documented erosion issues. The City of Austin Watershed Protection Department has undertaken erosion projects in the Little Walnut Creek watershed, including the James Tributary and Richmond Tributary projects, indicating active erosion concerns in this drainage system.

**Citation:** City of Austin 2013 Erosion Hazard Zone Guidance; Austin Drainage Criteria Manual, Appendix E (Creek Setback and Erosion Hazard Zone); City of Austin EHZ Review Buffer GIS layer (dataset pmnk-72i4); LDC Chapter 25-7 and 25-8.

**Development Impact:** An EHZ analysis will likely be required as part of the site plan submission given the proximity of drainage features. The proposed building footprint should maintain adequate separation from the mapped waterway to avoid the EHZ. The concept plan appears to position the building with sufficient setback, but formal EHZ modeling will confirm whether additional protective measures are needed.

**Confidence:** Inferred -- EHZ analysis requirement is triggered by proximity to mapped waterways. Specific EHZ boundaries for this parcel require site-specific geotechnical/hydraulic analysis.

## 7. Endangered Species

**Finding:** The primary endangered/threatened species concern in north Austin and Travis County is the **Jollyville Plateau salamander (Eurycea tonkawae)**, which was listed as **Threatened** under the federal Endangered Species Act in August 2013, with approximately 4,331-4,451 acres of designated critical habitat in Travis and Williamson Counties. This species inhabits springs, spring-fed streams, and caves in the Jollyville Plateau and Brushy Creek areas of the Edwards Plateau.

However, the Jollyville Plateau salamander's habitat is concentrated in the **northwest Austin / Cedar Park / Round Rock** area, associated with springs emerging from the Northern Edwards Aquifer limestone formations. The 9101 Cameron Rd site is located in **northeast Austin**, in an area underlain by different geology (Upper Cretaceous formations, Taylor/Austin Chalk rather than Edwards Limestone). The property is **outside the designated critical habitat** for the Jollyville Plateau salamander.

Other species covered by the Travis County Balcones Canyonlands Preserve (BCP) system -- including the golden-cheeked warbler and black-capped vireo -- are associated with juniper-oak woodland habitat in the western Hill Country portions of Travis County, not the urban corridor along Cameron Road.

**Citation:** 78 FR 51327 (August 20, 2013) -- Final Rule designating critical habitat for Austin Blind and Jollyville Plateau Salamanders; Travis County BCP species list (traviscountytx.gov/tnr/nr/bcp/species); USFWS critical habitat designations.

**Development Impact:** No known endangered or threatened species habitat is mapped at or immediately adjacent to 9101 Cameron Rd. The property is not within designated critical habitat for any federally listed species. A standard Environmental Resource Inventory (ERI) will be completed as part of site plan review, which will include a biological assessment. No USFWS Section 7 consultation or Habitat Conservation Plan participation is anticipated.

**Confidence:** Verified -- The site is geographically and geologically separated from known endangered species habitat in the Austin area.

## 8. Impervious Cover Limits

**Finding:** As an **Urban Watershed** site, 9101 Cameron Rd is subject to the least restrictive impervious cover regime in Austin's tiered watershed protection system:

| Watershed Classification | Maximum Impervious Cover |
|---|---|
| **Urban** | **No numerical IC cap** (water quality controls required instead) |
| Suburban | 40-55% net site area |
| Water Supply Suburban | 25-40% net site area |
| Water Supply Rural | 20-25% net site area |
| Barton Springs Zone | 15-25% net site area |

Under the Urban Watersheds Ordinance (adopted 1991, codified in LDC Ch. 25-8, Art. 8), urban watershed sites are **not subject to a maximum impervious cover percentage**. Instead, the regulatory approach requires **structural water quality controls** (sedimentation/filtration) to treat stormwater runoff from new impervious surfaces. The CS zoning district allows up to 95% impervious cover under the base zoning regulations; in an urban watershed, the watershed regulations do not further restrict this.

However, the **effective buildable impervious cover** on this site will be significantly reduced by the CWQZ, CEF setbacks, and any floodplain areas that restrict development. The net developable area (outside CWQZ and CEF buffers) is the area where impervious cover can be placed.

**Citation:** Austin LDC Ch. 25-8, Subchapter A, Art. 8 (Urban Watershed Requirements); LDC Sec. 25-8-372 (Uplands Zone, Urban Watersheds); ECM Section 1.8.0 (Impervious Cover Calculation Criteria); LDC Sec. 25-2 (Zoning, CS district standards).

**Development Impact:** The lack of a numeric IC cap is favorable for the proposed AutoZone development. The 7,375 SF building with associated parking and driveways on a 44,150 SF lot represents a moderate site coverage ratio. The primary constraint on impervious cover placement is not the IC percentage but the spatial restrictions imposed by CWQZ and CEF setback areas that consume portions of the site.

**Confidence:** Verified -- Urban watersheds in Austin are regulated via water quality controls rather than IC caps, as established by the 1991 UWO.

## 9. Water Quality Controls Required

**Finding:** For new development in Austin's Urban Watersheds, the following water quality treatment requirements apply:

**Structural Water Quality Controls (LDC 25-8, Art. 8; ECM 1.6.0, 1.9.0):**
- All new development generating additional impervious cover must provide **structural water quality controls** to treat stormwater runoff
- The standard treatment approach is a **sedimentation/filtration (S/F) system** or **sedimentation/biofiltration system**
- The water quality volume (WQV) to be captured and treated is calculated per ECM Section 1.6.0 methodology

**Sedimentation/Filtration System Design Requirements (ECM 1.6.2, 1.6.5):**
- The sedimentation basin must receive the **entire water quality volume** and detain it for a **48-hour drawdown**
- Minimum **2% bottom slope** in the sedimentation basin for adequate drainage
- Sand filtration basin maximum ponding depth must be at least **1 foot less** than sedimentation basin max depth (to account for tailwater)
- Full sedimentation/filtration or sedimentation/biofiltration systems are required where the City is responsible for maintenance

**Fee-in-Lieu Alternative:**
- The Urban Watersheds Ordinance allows a **fee-in-lieu payment** instead of constructing on-site water quality controls, subject to approval by the Director of the Watershed Protection Department

**Maintenance Requirements (ECM 1.6.3, 1.14.0):**
- Ponds must be observed full and empty to verify drawdown times
- At least **one annual inspection** by a third-party inspector with a report submitted to the Watershed Protection Department
- Ongoing maintenance obligations run with the property

**Concept Plan Observation:** The concept plan shows a **preliminary detention/WQ pond in the northwest portion of the site**, which is consistent with the requirement for on-site sedimentation/filtration treatment. This pond would serve the dual purpose of water quality treatment and stormwater detention.

**Citation:** Austin LDC Ch. 25-8, Art. 8 (Urban Watershed Requirements); ECM Sections 1.6.0 (Design Guidelines for Water Quality Controls), 1.6.2 (General Design Guidelines), 1.6.3 (Maintenance and Construction Requirements), 1.6.5 (Design Guidelines for Sedimentation/Filtration Systems), 1.9.0 (Need for Water Quality Controls), 1.14.0 (CEF Buffer Maintenance and Inspection).

**Development Impact:** The proposed detention/WQ pond in the NW corner of the site will need to be sized to capture the water quality volume generated by the site's impervious surfaces. Engineering design must demonstrate 48-hour drawdown and meet all ECM dimensional and grading criteria. A maintenance agreement will be recorded against the property. The applicant may alternatively explore the fee-in-lieu option if on-site treatment proves impractical due to space constraints from CWQZ and CEF buffers, though on-site treatment appears feasible based on the concept plan.

**Confidence:** Verified -- Water quality treatment via sedimentation/filtration is a well-established requirement for Urban Watershed development in Austin.

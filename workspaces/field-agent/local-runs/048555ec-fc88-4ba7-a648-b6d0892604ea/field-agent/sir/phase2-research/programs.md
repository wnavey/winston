## 1. Density Bonus Programs

**Finding:** Austin's density bonus programs are not applicable to this project.

Austin operates several density bonus programs, including the legacy DB90 (allowing up to 90 feet of height in exchange for affordable housing or fee-in-lieu) and the newly approved Citywide Density Bonus (DBC) program adopted by City Council in May 2026. The DBC establishes five tiers adding 0--60 feet of additional height above base entitlements on properties in multifamily and commercial zones. CS zoning is technically eligible for DBC participation.

However, density bonus programs are designed for projects seeking additional height and density beyond base zoning entitlements, typically for mixed-use or multifamily residential development. A single-story 7,375 SF retail store does not require or benefit from additional height or density bonuses. The CS district already permits commercial/retail uses by right, and the proposed building is well within base height and FAR limits.

**Eligibility:** Not applicable. The project does not seek height or density beyond CS base entitlements.
**Potential Value:** None for this project type.
**Confidence:** High

## 3. HOME Initiative

**Finding:** The HOME Initiative does not affect CS-zoned commercial properties.

The HOME (Home Options for Mobility and Equity) initiative is a series of Land Development Code amendments focused exclusively on increasing housing supply within single-family (SF) zoned areas. Phase 1 (adopted late 2023) allows up to three residential units by right on single-family lots. Phase 2 addresses additional residential density and transit-supportive code amendments.

The HOME initiative does not modify regulations for CS (Commercial Services) zoning districts. CS already permits a wide range of commercial uses by right, and the HOME amendments do not impose any new requirements, restrictions, or opportunities on commercial-only development in CS zones. A standalone AutoZone retail store on CS-zoned land is unaffected.

**Eligibility:** Not applicable. HOME targets SF-zoned residential areas only.
**Potential Value:** None.
**Confidence:** High

## 4. Site Plan Lite

**Finding:** Site Plan Lite does not apply to this commercial project; a standard site plan is required.

Site Plan Lite Phase 2 and Infill Plat was adopted by City Council on March 6, 2025, with applications accepted starting June 16, 2025. The program is exclusively residential in scope. Phase 1 established site plan exemptions for residential projects under five units. Phase 2 extends streamlined administrative review to multifamily residential site developments of 5--16 units and residential re-subdivisions.

Commercial development is not eligible for Site Plan Lite. A 7,375 SF AutoZone retail store requires a standard Consolidated Site Plan application. The project also does not qualify for a site plan exemption under LDC Section 25-5-2, as those exemptions are generally limited to minor modifications (under 2,000--3,000 SF of building area or impervious cover change on an already-developed site) and residential construction. A new 7,375 SF commercial building on a 1.01-acre lot will require full site plan review.

However, the project may qualify as a "Small Project" (under 5,000 SF of impervious cover and 10,000 SF construction area), which offers reduced fees, shorter review times, and no neighborhood notification requirement. Given that the building is 7,375 SF and total impervious cover (building + parking + drives) will likely exceed 5,000 SF on a 1.01-acre commercial site, the project will most likely exceed the Small Project thresholds and require a standard site plan process.

**Eligibility:** Not eligible for Site Plan Lite (residential only). Likely requires standard site plan.
**Potential Value:** None.
**Confidence:** High

## 5. RSMP (Regional Stormwater Management Program)

**Finding:** RSMP fee-in-lieu may be available depending on watershed eligibility; verification required.

The RSMP allows eligible developments to make a fee payment or construct approved offsite drainage infrastructure improvements in lieu of onsite stormwater detention. The program operates on a watershed-by-watershed basis -- a site must be located in a participating watershed, and the proposed development must produce no additional adverse flooding impact to roadways, structures, or other properties.

9101 Cameron Road is located in the Little Walnut Creek or upper North Boggy Creek watershed area. Whether RSMP participation is feasible for this specific site requires verification through the City's RSMP Feasibility Viewer tool and a Feasibility Meeting with the RSMP Team.

The RSMP Payment Calculator (updated annually on September 30, indexed to the Engineering News-Record Construction Cost Index) determines the fee amount. Exact fees depend on the volume of detention required and site-specific factors. For a 1.01-acre commercial site generating moderate impervious cover, typical RSMP payments in Austin have historically ranged from approximately $50,000--$150,000, though this varies significantly by watershed and project specifics. The FY26 Payment Calculator is available for download from the City's website.

For a single-story retail pad site, the potential benefit of RSMP is significant: eliminating onsite detention ponds frees up developable area and simplifies site layout, which can be particularly valuable on a relatively compact 1.01-acre lot.

**Eligibility:** Potentially eligible; requires RSMP Feasibility Viewer check and meeting with RSMP Team.
**Potential Value:** Moderate to significant -- eliminates onsite detention requirement. Fee estimated at $50K--$150K (varies by watershed/project). Savings in land area and construction complexity may offset cost.
**Confidence:** Medium (watershed eligibility unconfirmed)

**Sources:**

## 6. Infill Incentives

**Finding:** No dedicated infill incentive program applies to this type of commercial retail development.

Austin does not offer a general-purpose infill incentive for commercial retail construction. The city's infill-related tools focus primarily on residential infill:

- **Corner Store Infill Tool:** Permits neighborhood-scale retail (coffee shops, small stores) in residential areas. Not relevant to a CS-zoned site which already allows commercial use by right.
- **DB90 / DBC Density Bonus:** Available for mixed-use or multifamily projects seeking additional height. Not applicable to a single-story retail store.
- **VMU2 (Vertical Mixed-Use 2):** Allows increased height (up to 90 feet) along certain corridors in exchange for affordable housing units. Requires residential component; not applicable to a purely commercial project.

The Place-Based Enhancement Program (PBEP) is a city economic development program that provides incentives for commercial real estate projects that offer affordable space for local small businesses, legacy businesses, nonprofits, and creative-sector tenants. However, PBEP requires a minimum $2 million investment for new construction and targets community-serving tenants, not national chain retail. An AutoZone store would not meet the program's community-benefit objectives.

**Eligibility:** Not eligible for any current infill incentive programs.
**Potential Value:** None.
**Confidence:** High

## 10. Green Building Incentives

**Finding:** Limited green building incentives are available for commercial projects, but unlikely to be pursued for a standard AutoZone retail store.

**Austin Energy Green Building (AEGB) Commercial Rating:**
The AEGB program provides a 1--5 star rating for commercial buildings based on sustainable design measures. Rated projects receive:
- **Expedited permitting:** Green-rated projects receive priority processing during plan review, potentially reducing permit wait times.
- **Energy rebates:** Commercial projects can earn rebates for LED lighting, HVAC optimization, and water-saving fixtures, with large projects eligible for up to $200,000 in rebates.
- The AEGB Commercial Rating was updated in July 2025 to align with current building code.

All commercial projects over 2,500 SF within Austin Energy's service territory are required to participate in the AEGB program at a minimum 1-star level (per City ordinance). This is a baseline requirement, not an incentive. Achieving higher star ratings (3+ stars) provides the expedited permitting benefit.

**Practical Assessment:** For a 7,375 SF single-story retail store (a relatively standard commercial building type), pursuing a high AEGB rating beyond the mandatory minimum is unlikely to be cost-effective. The primary incentive value would be modest energy rebates and potential permit timeline reduction. National chain retailers like AutoZone typically use standardized prototype designs and are unlikely to significantly modify building design for local green building incentives.

**Eligibility:** Mandatory 1-star AEGB participation required. Voluntary higher ratings available.
**Potential Value:** Low -- mandatory participation is a requirement, not an incentive. Higher ratings could yield modest energy rebates ($5K--$25K estimated for a project this size) and expedited permitting.
**Confidence:** High

**Sources:**
- [Austin Energy -- Design & Build Green](https://austinenergy.com/energy-efficiency/green-building/design-build)
- [Austin Energy Green Building](https://austinenergy.com/energy-efficiency/green-building)

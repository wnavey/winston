# Site Plan & Form Findings -- 9101 Cameron Rd

**Property:** 9101 Cameron Rd, Austin, TX 78753 | CS zoning | 1.01 AC / 44,150 SF
**Proposed:** AutoZone retail store (Retail Sale of Auto Parts), 7,375 SF Prototype 74, single-story, 12 parking spaces, preliminary pond
**Discipline:** Site Plan & Form (SDUF)
**Date:** 2026-06-04

---

## Summary

- **Subchapter E (Design Standards and Mixed Use) applies.** The project is a nonresidential commercial development requiring a site plan on a principal street (Cameron Rd, ASMP Level 4). It is not exempt from Subchapter E. Articles 2 and 3 govern site development standards and building design standards, respectively.
- **The concept plan's parking-in-front-of-building layout is a likely Subchapter E conflict.** The concept plan shows the building set back from Cameron Rd with surface parking and the preliminary pond between the building and the street. Subchapter E Section 2.2 restricts or prohibits surface parking between a building and the principal street, with the strictness depending on whether Cameron Rd is classified as an Urban Roadway or Suburban Roadway. This is the most significant site plan risk identified.
- **Entrance orientation toward Cameron Rd is required.** Section 2.4 requires the primary customer entrance to face the principal street and be architecturally prominent and visible from the abutting street. The AutoZone Prototype 74 layout must be evaluated for compliance.
- **Building design standards (Article 3) require glazing, articulation, and blank-wall treatment on the Cameron Rd facade.** The principal-street-facing elevation must meet transparency requirements (40% glazing on the front facade, 25% on publicly visible sides, VT of 0.6 or higher) and must incorporate design elements at the primary entrance.
- **Site Plan Lite does not apply.** Site Plan Lite (Phase 1 and Phase 2) targets residential projects (single-family through 16-unit residential). A 7,375 SF single-tenant commercial retail building requires a standard administrative site plan, not Site Plan Lite.
- **The procedural path is administrative site plan review by DSD staff.** No commission or council hearing is triggered. Alternative Equivalent Compliance (AEC) is available if the prototype design cannot strictly meet Subchapter E standards.

## Findings

### SDUF-1: Subchapter E Applies -- Cameron Rd as Principal Street

**Severity:** Informational (procedural)
**Citation:** LDC 25-2, Subchapter E, Article 1 (Applicability); Article 5 (Definitions); ASMP Street Network Table
**Finding:** Subchapter E applies to all nonresidential and mixed-use development requiring a site plan, with applicability determined by Table A based on the principal street classification and development type. Cameron Rd is classified as ASMP Level 4. Under Subchapter E Section 2.1, roadways are classified into three types -- Urban Roadway, Suburban Roadway, and Hill Country Roadway -- based on location, context, and Imagine Austin designation. Level 4 roads in the Cameron Rd corridor (north Austin, outside the urban core, not within an Imagine Austin activity center or core transit corridor) are most likely classified as Suburban Roadway. However, this classification must be confirmed with DSD during pre-application, as Cameron Rd's context may support Urban Roadway classification in certain segments, particularly if it falls within an Imagine Austin activity corridor.
**Implication:** The Suburban vs. Urban Roadway classification directly affects the stringency of build-to zone, parking placement, and frontage requirements. If classified as Urban Roadway, the constraints on the current layout are more severe.
**Next step:** Confirm Cameron Rd's Subchapter E roadway classification with DSD at pre-application. Request the Subchapter E applicability determination letter.

---

### SDUF-2: Parking Placement Between Building and Principal Street -- Likely Noncompliance (HIGH RISK)

**Severity:** High
**Citation:** LDC 25-2, Subchapter E, Section 2.2 (Building Placement)
**Finding:** The concept plan shows the AutoZone building set back from Cameron Rd with surface parking and the preliminary stormwater pond positioned between the building and the Cameron Rd frontage. Subchapter E Section 2.2 restricts surface parking between the building and the principal street:

- **If Urban Roadway:** Parking between the building and the property line adjacent to the principal street is prohibited, except on sites 400 feet deep or less where parking may be located between the street and the building only if certain frontage build-out conditions are met (e.g., 40% of net frontage consists of continuous building facade built up to the clear zone). Even under this exception, the current layout -- where the building is fully set back behind a parking lot -- is unlikely to qualify.
- **If Suburban Roadway:** Standards are less restrictive but still require either (a) the building to be pulled up to the sidewalk/clear zone for a specified percentage of net frontage, or (b) if parking is placed in front, the site must satisfy alternative connectivity and design requirements. The Suburban Roadway classification provides the best path for the current layout but still imposes conditions.

The concept plan's layout -- building behind parking with the pond between the building and Cameron Rd -- is a classic Subchapter E conflict for auto-parts retail prototypes. AutoZone's Prototype 74 typically places the storefront facing the parking lot with the building set back, which conflicts with Subchapter E's urban-form objectives.

**Implication:** This is the single largest site plan risk. The building will likely need to be repositioned closer to Cameron Rd, with parking relocated to the side or rear. This affects the preliminary pond location, truck circulation, heritage tree CRZ zones, and the overall site geometry. Alternatively, the applicant may pursue Alternative Equivalent Compliance (AEC) to demonstrate that an alternative design meets the intent of Subchapter E without strict adherence to the parking placement and build-to zone standards.
**Next step:** (1) Confirm roadway classification. (2) Evaluate whether the prototype can be rotated/repositioned to place the building facade at the Cameron Rd frontage with parking behind or beside the building. (3) If repositioning is infeasible due to the pond, CWQZ, heritage trees, or truck-turn constraints, prepare an AEC application with supporting urban design narrative.

---

### SDUF-3: Build-to Zone / Frontage Build-Out Requirement

**Severity:** High
**Citation:** LDC 25-2, Subchapter E, Section 2.2 (Building Placement)
**Finding:** Section 2.2 requires a percentage of the net frontage along the principal street to consist of continuous building facade built up to the clear zone (or supplemental zone if provided). The required percentage varies by roadway type:

- **Urban Roadway:** Approximately 40% of net frontage must be continuous building facade at the build-to line (or higher for VMU).
- **Suburban Roadway:** A lower percentage applies, but the building must still relate to the street frontage.

The concept plan shows a 10-foot building setback from Cameron Rd, but the building is positioned in the southern/central portion of the site rather than at the Cameron Rd frontage. The Cameron Rd frontage is occupied by the driveway, preliminary pond, and open area. No portion of the building facade appears to be built to the Cameron Rd build-to zone.

**Implication:** The site plan must demonstrate that the required percentage of Cameron Rd net frontage is occupied by building facade at the build-to line. With a 7,375 SF single-story footprint (~80-90 ft wide depending on prototype dimensions), the building could potentially satisfy the frontage requirement if repositioned to face Cameron Rd. The current layout provides 0% building frontage at the build-to zone.
**Next step:** Calculate the net frontage along Cameron Rd (subtracting driveways, required landscape buffers, and easements) and determine the required build-to percentage based on the confirmed roadway classification. Evaluate whether the building footprint can meet the frontage requirement.

---

### SDUF-4: Entrance Orientation Toward Cameron Rd

**Severity:** Moderate
**Citation:** LDC 25-2, Subchapter E, Section 2.4 (Entrance); Article 3 (Building Design Standards)
**Finding:** Section 2.4 requires the primary customer entrance to face the principal street (Cameron Rd) and to be architecturally prominent and clearly visible from the abutting street. The entrance design must incorporate at least three design elements (e.g., canopy, recessed entry, sidelights, transom, enhanced paving, etc.). AutoZone Prototype 74 typically places the customer entrance facing the parking lot. If the building is repositioned to the Cameron Rd frontage (per SDUF-2 and SDUF-3), the entrance must face Cameron Rd. If the building remains in its current position (set back behind parking), the entrance still must be oriented toward Cameron Rd, which may conflict with the prototype's operational layout.
**Implication:** The prototype floor plan may need modification to place the customer entrance on the Cameron Rd (north) elevation. This affects interior layout, ADA path routing, and potentially the loading/receiving area on the opposite side.
**Next step:** Coordinate with AutoZone corporate on prototype flexibility for entrance relocation to the Cameron Rd facade. Assess ADA path compliance from Cameron Rd sidewalk to the reoriented entrance.

---

### SDUF-5: Transparency / Glazing on Cameron Rd Facade

**Severity:** Moderate
**Citation:** LDC 25-2, Subchapter E, Article 3 (Building Design Standards), Section 3.3
**Finding:** Subchapter E Article 3 requires:

- **40% glazing on the front (principal street) facade** with a Visible Transmittance (VT) of 0.6 or higher.
- **25% glazing and cutouts on each side elevation visible to the public** with VT of 0.6 or higher.
- Blank wall limitations: no uninterrupted blank wall segment exceeding a specified length (typically 30 feet) on a street-facing facade.
- The primary entrance must be architecturally prominent with at least three design elements.

AutoZone's Prototype 74 is typically a masonry/EIFS box with a prominent storefront glazing system on the entrance facade and minimal glazing on the other three elevations. If the entrance facade is rotated to face Cameron Rd, the 40% glazing requirement on that elevation is likely achievable (the storefront typically meets or exceeds 40%). However, the side elevations (east and west) visible from public streets or adjacent sites must meet the 25% glazing threshold, which is often where auto-parts retail prototypes fall short.

Additionally, the building must earn a minimum number of design points from Section 3.3's menu of options to improve building design (facade articulation, materials, screening, etc.).

**Implication:** The architect must verify that the Cameron Rd elevation meets the 40% glazing threshold and that visible side elevations meet 25%. If the prototype falls short, facade modifications (additional windows, display cases, or architectural cutouts) will be required. The building must also meet the Section 3.3 points requirement.
**Next step:** Request AutoZone's Prototype 74 elevation drawings. Calculate glazing percentages for each facade. Identify any facades that fall below the threshold and quantify the additional glazing or design modifications needed.

### SDUF-6: Connectivity Requirements (Section 2.3)

**Severity:** Low-to-Moderate
**Citation:** LDC 25-2, Subchapter E, Section 2.3 (Connectivity Between Sites)
**Finding:** Section 2.3 requires sites subject to Subchapter E to provide vehicular and pedestrian connections to adjacent sites. The site must select and comply with at least two connectivity options from a prescribed menu (e.g., shared driveways, cross-access easements, pedestrian connections, internal circulation routes). An exemption from connectivity requirements applies if the property meets the building placement requirements for Urban Roadways and no parking is located between the principal street and any street-facing building elevation. Given the current layout has parking in front of the building, this exemption would not apply.

Adjacent properties include a commercial parcel to the east (Precision Engine Service, 1.014 AC) and the Housing Authority of the City of Austin to the west. Cross-access or shared driveway opportunities with the eastern parcel should be evaluated.

**Implication:** The site plan must demonstrate compliance with at least two connectivity options. The secondary access point shown on the concept plan (connecting to the Pecan Springs Rd area via the western boundary) may satisfy one connectivity option if formalized as a cross-access easement. A second option (pedestrian connection, shared driveway to the east, or internal circulation route) must also be provided.
**Next step:** Evaluate cross-access feasibility with adjacent parcels. Identify the two connectivity options to be provided and incorporate them into the site plan.

---

### SDUF-7: Alternative Equivalent Compliance (AEC) May Be Required

**Severity:** Moderate (procedural risk)
**Citation:** LDC 25-2, Subchapter E, Article 1 (Alternative Equivalent Compliance provisions)
**Finding:** If the AutoZone prototype cannot be repositioned to comply with building placement (SDUF-2), build-to zone (SDUF-3), and entrance orientation (SDUF-4) requirements, the applicant may pursue Alternative Equivalent Compliance (AEC). AEC allows development to proceed in a manner that meets the intent of Subchapter E through an alternative design that does not strictly adhere to the Subchapter's standards. AEC is not a general waiver -- the applicant must demonstrate through an urban design narrative and supporting materials that the alternative design achieves equivalent or superior outcomes for pedestrian orientation, street activation, and connectivity.

AEC is reviewed administratively by DSD staff. It does not require a commission or council hearing. However, it adds processing time (typically 2-4 weeks) and introduces review subjectivity.

**Implication:** AEC should be treated as a fallback strategy, not the primary path. Repositioning the building to comply with Subchapter E is strongly preferred because it eliminates review risk and processing delay. AEC approval is not guaranteed, and DSD staff may impose conditions.
**Next step:** Evaluate building repositioning feasibility first. If infeasible, begin preparing the AEC application in parallel with the site plan, including an urban design narrative justifying the alternative layout (citing CWQZ constraints, heritage tree CRZ, truck-turn geometry, and pond placement as limiting factors).

---

### SDUF-8: Site Plan Lite Is Not Available for This Project

**Severity:** Informational
**Citation:** LDC 25-5; Ord. 20230706-049 (SPL Phase 1); Ord. 20250306 (SPL Phase 2 & Infill Plat)
**Finding:** Austin's Site Plan Lite program has two phases:

- **Phase 1 (adopted July 2023):** Established a site plan exemption for residential projects smaller than five units.
- **Phase 2 (adopted March 2025, applications accepted June 16, 2025):** Streamlined the site plan process for residential projects of 5-16 units, including reduced review scope and elimination of onsite detention for projects below 8,000 SF impervious cover.

Neither phase applies to commercial development. The AutoZone project is a single-tenant retail building, not a residential project. Additionally, the project's 16,666 SF of proposed impervious cover exceeds the small project threshold of 5,000 SF IC / 10,000 SF construction area (LDC 25-5-3) that triggers simplified review for small commercial projects.

**Implication:** The project requires a standard administrative site plan with full DSD review, including all Subchapter E compliance, environmental (CWQZ/CEF), transportation, and drainage review components. No expedited or simplified path is available.
**Next step:** No action required -- proceed with standard site plan application.

## Key Constraints Affecting Building Repositioning

If the building is repositioned to the Cameron Rd frontage to comply with Subchapter E, the following site constraints must be reconciled:

1. **Preliminary pond location:** Currently between the building and Cameron Rd. If the building moves north, the pond must relocate to the rear or side. Pond sizing depends on drainage engineering (Phase 2 environmental/drainage discipline).
2. **CWQZ boundaries:** Multiple Critical Water Quality Zone boundaries cross the site, particularly in the northern portion near Cameron Rd. Building placement within the CWQZ may be restricted or require additional environmental review.
3. **CEF setbacks:** Critical Environmental Feature setback lines along the eastern and northeastern portions of the site constrain development footprint.
4. **Heritage trees:** Three heritage trees (Live Oak 28", American Elm 29.5", Ligustrum 33") and three protected trees must remain. Their Critical Root Zone (CRZ) circles constrain where the building, parking, and improvements can be placed.
5. **Truck-turn geometry:** The engineer's note on Sheet 2 indicates the truck-turn path is already marginal at current dimensions (35-40 ft needed for comfortable fit). Repositioning the building may further constrain delivery truck circulation.
6. **25-ft ROW reserve:** A 25-ft right-of-way reserve (Doc. No. 201800004) along Cameron Rd reduces the usable site depth at the north end.
7. **Existing utility easements:** Electric, drainage, PUE, and wastewater easements constrain where structures can be placed.

## Recommendations

1. **Pre-application meeting with DSD is essential.** Confirm Cameron Rd's Subchapter E roadway classification (Urban vs. Suburban) and discuss the prototype's compliance path before investing in full site plan engineering.
2. **Evaluate building repositioning as the primary compliance strategy.** Moving the building to the Cameron Rd frontage with parking behind/beside is the cleanest path to Subchapter E compliance and eliminates AEC risk.
3. **Commission a site constraints overlay analysis.** Map the CWQZ, CEF setbacks, heritage tree CRZ, easements, and ROW reserve on a single exhibit to identify the feasible building envelope at the Cameron Rd frontage.
4. **Coordinate with AutoZone corporate on prototype flexibility.** The Prototype 74 entrance orientation, facade glazing, and floor plan may need modification. Early engagement avoids redesign loops.
5. **Prepare an AEC application in parallel if repositioning proves infeasible.** The urban design narrative should cite the CWQZ, heritage trees, utility easements, and truck-turn constraints as factors preventing strict compliance. AEC is administrative but adds review time.
6. **Budget for facade upgrades.** The 40% front / 25% side glazing requirements and blank-wall limitations will likely require modifications to the standard AutoZone prototype elevations.

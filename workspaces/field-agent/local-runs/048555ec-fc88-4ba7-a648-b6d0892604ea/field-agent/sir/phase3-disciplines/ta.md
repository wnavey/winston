# Transportation (TA) -- Phase 3 Discipline Analysis

**Property:** 9101 Cameron Rd, Austin, TX 78753 | CS zoning | 1.01 AC (44,150 SF)
**Proposed:** AutoZone 7,375 SF retail (Prototype 74), 12 parking spaces, primary driveway from Cameron Rd
**Analyst date:** 2026-06-04

---

## TA-1. ROW Dedication -- Site Area Impact

**Requirement:** Cameron Rd is ASMP Level 4 with a 154 ft target ROW. Existing ROW is approximately 99-116 ft (variable). The concept plan shows a 25 ft ROW Reserve (Doc. No. 201800004) along the Cameron Rd frontage. ROW dedication is required at site plan approval at no cost to the City.

**Site area impact calculation:**
- Gross lot area: 44,150 SF (1.01 AC)
- Cameron Rd frontage: approximately 162.5 ft (south boundary bearing/distance from concept plan, though actual frontage dimension may differ)
- Area lost to 25 ft ROW dedication: approximately 25 ft x frontage = ~4,060 SF (estimated)
- Post-dedication net site area: approximately 40,090 SF (~0.92 AC)
- Percentage of site lost: approximately 9.2%

**Impact on site plan:** The 25 ft strip loss compresses the building envelope, stormwater pond, and parking between the new property line and the southern boundary. The preliminary detention pond shown in the northwest corner of the concept plan sits partly within or adjacent to the dedication zone, suggesting it may need to be resized or relocated. Building setbacks must be measured from the post-dedication property line, not the current frontage.

**Feasibility assessment:** FEASIBLE WITH CONSTRAINT. The dedication is already anticipated on the concept plan and the ROW reserve instrument is recorded. The net site area (~40,090 SF) still supports a 7,375 SF building at 18.4% building coverage and 12 parking spaces. However, the post-dedication geometry tightens all north-south dimensions, and the applicant must verify that the stormwater pond, fire lane, and truck maneuvering area all fit within the reduced site depth.

**Risk:** MEDIUM. The 25 ft dedication is already documented and planned for. Risk is that actual dedication demand could exceed 25 ft if the City requires additional ROW for the Cameron Rd/Dessau Rd Safety Project improvements (protected bike lanes, widened sidewalks, bus pull-outs). The concept plan should clarify whether the 25 ft ROW reserve is the full extent of the anticipated dedication or a minimum.

## TA-2. Driveway Access -- Spacing, Type, and Turn Restrictions

**Applicable standard:** Austin Transportation Criteria Manual (TCM), Section 7. Level 4 streets require Type II or Type III commercial driveways only; Type I (residential) driveways are prohibited.

**Driveway spacing requirements (Level 4):**
- Driveway-to-driveway: minimum 120 ft edge-to-edge (must align with opposing driveways/streets or be offset by at least 120 ft)
- Corner clearance (driveway-to-intersection): minimum 100 ft from intersecting ROW, or 60% of parcel frontage, whichever is less

**Concept plan configuration:** One primary commercial driveway from Cameron Rd (north). One secondary access from the west/southwest toward Pecan Springs Rd area. This minimized-driveway approach is consistent with TCM Level 4 access management goals.

**Cameron Rd Safety Project -- median restriction risk:** The active Cameron Rd/Dessau Rd Safety Project includes raised medians at strategic locations to eliminate left-turn conflicts. If a raised median is installed in front of 9101 Cameron Rd, the driveway would be restricted to right-in/right-out (RIRO) only. This would prevent:
- Northbound left turns into the site
- Left turns out of the site to travel southbound

**Feasibility assessment:** FEASIBLE WITH CONDITIONS. The single Cameron Rd driveway meets TCM intent for minimized access on Level 4 streets. The applicant must demonstrate:
1. The driveway meets the 120 ft driveway-to-driveway spacing requirement relative to adjacent property driveways (east neighbor: Precision Engine Service).
2. The driveway meets the 100 ft corner clearance from the nearest intersection.
3. The driveway type (Type II or III) is appropriate for the anticipated vehicle mix (passenger vehicles and delivery trucks).

**Risk:** MEDIUM-HIGH. The raised median scenario is the primary risk. If RIRO is imposed, customer access from the north (southbound Cameron Rd) is preserved, but northbound customers and delivery trucks approaching from the south would need to find a U-turn opportunity or use the secondary western access. The applicant should request the Safety Project's median plan from Austin Transportation and Public Works to determine whether a median is planned at this location. If RIRO is confirmed, the secondary western access becomes operationally critical.

## TA-3. Truck-Turn Feasibility

**Concept plan finding:** The truck-turn exhibit (Sheet 2 / EX A) shows a delivery vehicle entering from Cameron Rd, circulating through the site, and exiting. The engineer's red annotation states: "If distance can be changed to 35', truck turn could probably work, but 40' would make a comfortable fit."

**Analysis:** The engineer's own note flags that the current drive-aisle width is marginal for the design vehicle. The turning path appears to be for an SU-30 or WB-61/62 class vehicle (a standard delivery truck or articulated semi-trailer). Key constraints:
- The 12-space parking area south of the building limits maneuvering room
- The post-ROW-dedication site depth compression further tightens the northern approach from Cameron Rd
- Heritage trees (#15 Live Oak 28", #16 American Elm 29.5", #17 Ligustrum 33") and protected trees with CRZ zones constrain layout flexibility

**Required resolution:** The drive aisle must be widened to 35-40 ft in the critical maneuvering zone. This may require:
- Reducing parking count (already at 12 spaces, well below AutoZone's internal standard of 30)
- Shifting the building footprint south or west
- Narrowing landscape buffers (subject to LDC minimums)
- Adjusting the dumpster enclosure location on the east side

**Feasibility assessment:** FEASIBLE BUT REQUIRES REDESIGN. The engineer has identified the problem and the dimensional fix (35-40 ft). The question is whether the site geometry, after ROW dedication, can accommodate the wider aisle without violating setbacks, CRZ protections, or fire lane requirements. A revised truck-turn exhibit demonstrating a clean sweep path at the wider dimension must be provided with the site plan application.

**Risk:** MEDIUM. The fix is known (widen the aisle), but implementation is constrained by the tight 0.92 AC post-dedication site. If the WB-61/62 design vehicle is required (for AutoZone's standard freight deliveries), the turning radius demands are substantial. If the applicant can accept a smaller design vehicle (SU-30 single-unit truck), the constraint relaxes significantly. The applicant should confirm AutoZone's actual delivery fleet vehicle specifications.

## TA-4. Sidewalk and Bicycle Facility Obligations

**Sidewalk requirement:** The applicant must construct a sidewalk along the full Cameron Rd frontage within the dedicated ROW, meeting current TCM and ADA standards:
- Minimum 6 ft clear sidewalk width
- Landscape/furniture buffer zone between curb and sidewalk (typically 6-8 ft on Level 4 streets)
- ADA-compliant curb ramps at the driveway crossing
- Detectable warning surfaces at vehicular conflict points

**Bicycle facility requirement:** Cameron Rd's ASMP target cross-section includes protected bike lanes ("Bike Lane - Protected One"). The Cameron Rd/Dessau Rd Safety Project is actively constructing protected bike lanes on this corridor. The applicant may be required to:
- Construct the protected bike lane segment along the property frontage, or
- Grade and prepare the ROW for future bike lane construction by the City, or
- Pay a fee-in-lieu if the Safety Project will construct the segment

**Driveway-bike lane conflict:** The Cameron Rd driveway must be designed to safely cross the protected bike lane. This requires:
- Sight triangles clear of obstructions at the driveway-bike lane intersection
- Appropriate pavement markings and signage (green bike lane paint through driveway, yield signs)
- Truck-turn geometry that does not require vehicles to encroach on the bike lane while waiting to enter/exit

**Feasibility assessment:** FEASIBLE -- STANDARD OBLIGATION. Sidewalk and bike lane construction is a routine requirement for development on Level 4 streets. The cost is borne by the applicant and should be budgeted as a frontage improvement cost item.

**Cost impact:** Estimated $150-250 per linear foot for combined sidewalk, buffer, and bike lane improvements along ~162 ft of frontage = approximately $24,000-$40,000. Actual cost depends on whether the Safety Project handles the bike lane segment.

**Risk:** LOW. These are standard requirements. The only uncertainty is coordination with the active Safety Project construction timeline -- the applicant's frontage improvements must be compatible with the Safety Project's planned cross-section, which may not be finalized for this specific segment.

## TA-5. Transit Access and Coordination

**Existing service:** Cameron Rd is served by CapMetro Routes 300 (high-frequency, 15-30 min headways), 339, and 485 (night owl). The nearest bus stop is at 8100 Cameron Rd (Stop ID 4720), approximately 0.2 miles south.

**Obligations:**
- The site plan must provide a continuous, ADA-accessible pedestrian path from the nearest bus stop to the store entrance via the Cameron Rd sidewalk.
- If the Cameron Rd Safety Project relocates a bus stop to the property frontage, CapMetro may request a bus pull-out bay or shelter pad within the dedicated ROW.

**Feasibility assessment:** FEASIBLE -- NO MATERIAL CONSTRAINT. Transit access is a positive factor for both customer and employee access. The only action item is coordination with CapMetro to determine if a stop relocation to the property frontage is planned.

**Risk:** LOW. A bus pull-out request would consume additional ROW frontage but would be within the dedicated ROW, not the developable site area.

## TA-6. TIA Threshold and Sub-Threshold Mitigation

**Trip generation:** Approximately 456 gross daily trips (ITE Land Use 843, 61.91 trips/1,000 SF x 7.375 KSF). After pass-by adjustment (40-50% for auto parts retail): approximately 228-274 net new daily trips.

**TIA threshold:** 2,000 daily trips for basic TIA; 5,000 for full TIA. This project is well below both thresholds at ~456 gross / ~250 net daily trips. No TIA required.

**Required submittal:** TIA Determination Worksheet to Austin Transportation Development Services (TDS) via the TIA Determination Portal. TDS will issue a formal no-TIA-required determination.

**Sub-threshold conditions the City may still impose:**
Even without a TIA, the City retains authority to impose transportation conditions at site plan review, particularly given the Cameron Rd corridor's high-crash history (2,400 crashes, 2019-2023) and the active Safety Project. Likely conditions include:
1. Driveway design review for sight distance and geometric adequacy
2. Pedestrian/bicycle connectivity requirements (addressed in TA-4)
3. Driveway throat length adequate to prevent vehicle queuing onto Cameron Rd
4. Turn-lane warrant analysis if TDS determines one is needed (unlikely at ~44 PM peak hour trips)
5. Construction-phase traffic management plan if site development overlaps with Safety Project construction

**Feasibility assessment:** FEASIBLE -- NO MATERIAL CONSTRAINT. No TIA is required. The sub-threshold conditions are manageable and largely overlap with standard site plan requirements.

**Risk:** LOW.

Both are resolvable through design but require early coordination with Austin Transportation and Public Works.

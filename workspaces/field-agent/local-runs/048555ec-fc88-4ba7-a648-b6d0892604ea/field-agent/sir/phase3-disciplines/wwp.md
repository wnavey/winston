## 2. Transmission Main Restriction (36" Water Line)

**Regulatory Basis:** UCM Section 2.9.2.A.4.a -- Individual meter services and fire lines shall not be taken from transmission lines. Transmission lines are generally considered to be 24 inches in diameter or larger.

**Finding: CRITICAL CONSTRAINT.** The 36" water main along Cameron Road is classified as a transmission main under the UCM. Direct tapping of this main for a domestic meter or fire service line is **prohibited** absent written approval from the Austin Water Director or designee (UCM 2.9.6).

**Practical Impact:**
- The applicant **cannot** install a domestic water service tap or fire line tap on the 36" main.
- The 8" water main on or near the site is the appropriate service main for both domestic and fire taps.
- The 14" main may also serve as a distribution main eligible for tapping, but the 8" main is the more typical service connection point.

**Risk Assessment:** LOW, provided the 8" main is confirmed as a distribution main (not a sub-transmission main) and is within the site frontage. If the 8" main is on a different alignment or requires an off-site connection, a Service Extension Request may be triggered (see Section 4 below).

**Action Required:** Confirm with Austin Water Records Access Application (RAA) or a pre-submittal meeting that the 8" water main is the designated service main and that its location, pressure, and capacity are adequate for the proposed use.

## 3. Tap Sizing and Demand Estimate

### Domestic Water Service

A 7,375 SF AutoZone retail store has modest water demand: restrooms (employees and customers), hand-wash sinks, possible mop sink, and exterior hose bib. No irrigation system is anticipated (impervious site; landscape areas are minimal and likely within CWQZ).

**Estimated Fixture Count:**
- 2-4 water closets, 2-3 lavatories, 1 mop sink, 1-2 hose bibs
- Total Water Supply Fixture Units (WSFU): approximately 15-25

**Probable Meter Size:** 1" domestic meter. A 5/8" x 3/4" meter is marginal for a 7,375 SF commercial building; a 1" meter provides adequate capacity for peak fixture demand and accommodates a fire sprinkler system on a combined service (if applicable) or a separate domestic-only line.

**Note:** Meter sizing must be confirmed via the Austin Water Meter Sizing Calculator (per AWWA M22) using the actual plumbing fixture schedule. Austin Water requires the AWWA meter sizing worksheet as part of the tap application.

### Fire Service

**Fire flow requirement:** Per the 2021 International Fire Code as adopted by Austin (AFC Chapter 5), a retail occupancy (Group M) of 7,375 SF with sprinkler credit requires a fire flow in the range of 1,500-2,000 GPM for 2 hours (exact requirement depends on construction type and sprinkler design).

**Fire line size:** A 6" fire service line is typical for a building of this size with an automatic sprinkler system. If the building is unsprinklered, fire flow demand increases and an 8" fire line may be warranted.

**Connection Point:** The fire line must connect to the 8" distribution main (not the 36" transmission main). A 6" fire line from an 8" main is a standard configuration; however, Austin Water and Austin Fire Department (AFD) must confirm that the 8" main delivers adequate residual pressure at the required fire flow. A fire flow test at the nearest hydrant is recommended.

**Fire Hydrant Spacing:** AFD requires fire hydrants within 300 ft of the building (400 ft with sprinklers). Existing hydrants on Cameron Road should be verified for compliance; an on-site hydrant may be required depending on building placement relative to existing hydrants.

## 4. Service Extension Request (SER) Analysis

**SER Triggers (Austin City Code Chapter 25-9, Article 1, Division 2):**

An SER is required when any of the following conditions exist:

1. Austin Water's existing water, wastewater, or reclaimed water system is **more than 100 feet** from the property boundary;
2. The existing system is **not suitable or sufficient** to provide the required level of service; or
3. The applicant requests connection to a **water or reclaimed water transmission main** or **wastewater interceptor**.

**Analysis:**

| Trigger | Applicable? | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Distance > 100 ft | No | 8" water and 8"/10" wastewater mains are on or immediately adjacent to the site, well within 100 ft of the property boundary |
| System inadequacy | Unlikely | An 8" water main and 8"/10" wastewater mains are adequate for a 7,375 SF retail store with modest demand |
| Transmission main / interceptor tap | No (if tapping 8" main) | The applicant should connect to the 8" water main, not the 36" transmission main. The 10" wastewater mains are collection mains, not interceptors (interceptors are typically 15" and larger) |

**Finding: SER IS LIKELY NOT REQUIRED.** The existing 8" water main and 8"/10" wastewater mains within 100 ft of the property boundary are adequate for the proposed use. No connection to the 36" transmission main or a wastewater interceptor is proposed.

**Caveat:** If Austin Water determines during pre-submittal review that the 8" water main has inadequate pressure or capacity (e.g., due to system-wide demand on the transmission grid), an SER could be triggered under the "not suitable or sufficient" criterion. This is unlikely for a low-demand retail use but should be confirmed.

## 5. Wastewater Service Adequacy

**Gravity Flow Feasibility:**

The wastewater manhole inverts provide critical data:
- Northeast MH: 10" FL OUT invert at 624.92' (NAVD 88)
- Building pad elevation: approximately 628-632' (from concept plan contours)
- Elevation differential: approximately 3-7 ft of fall from the building to the wastewater system

This elevation differential is sufficient for gravity flow from the building to the existing wastewater system. No lift station is required.

**Wastewater Main Capacity:**

The 8" and 10" wastewater mains serving this area are standard collection system sizes. A 7,375 SF retail store generates minimal wastewater flow (estimated 500-1,000 GPD based on 0.1 GPD/SF for retail). The existing mains have ample capacity for this contribution.

**Connection Point:** The most logical connection is to the 10" wastewater main via the existing manholes within the 15' wastewater easements on site. This avoids disturbing Cameron Road for a new tap and uses the existing infrastructure alignment.

**Grease Trap:** Not anticipated. AutoZone is a retail auto parts store without food preparation. Austin Water's pretreatment program (Industrial Waste) does not require grease interceptors for this use. If any vehicle maintenance or parts washing occurs on-site, an oil/water separator may be required; however, AutoZone's standard prototype does not include service bays.

## 7. Reclaimed Water Assessment

**Regulatory Basis:** Commercial, multifamily, and mixed-use developments within **250 feet** of an existing City of Austin reclaimed water main must connect to the reclaimed water system for non-potable uses (toilet flushing, irrigation, cooling). The threshold increases to **500 feet** for large developments (250,000 SF or more gross floor area).

**Applicability to This Project:**

| Criterion | Finding |
|---|---|
| Gross floor area | 7,375 SF -- well below 250,000 SF threshold |
| Applicable distance threshold | 250 ft (standard threshold for projects under 250,000 SF) |
| Reclaimed main proximity | **Unknown -- must be verified** |

**Action Required:** The applicant must check the Austin Water Records Access Application (RAA) GIS viewer or contact ReclaimedWater@austintexas.gov to determine whether a reclaimed water main exists within 250 feet of the property boundary.

**Practical Assessment:** The Cameron Road corridor in the 78753 area is a suburban commercial district. Reclaimed water mains are typically concentrated in central Austin, the Domain area, and along major reclaimed distribution corridors. The probability of a reclaimed main within 250 ft of this site is **low to moderate**, but this must be confirmed.

**If reclaimed water is required:**
- A dual-plumbing system (purple pipe) for toilet flushing would be needed
- Irrigation demand is minimal given CWQZ constraints on landscaped area
- The cost impact is moderate ($15,000-$30,000 for dual plumbing in a 7,375 SF retail building)

## 8. On-Site Water Reuse System (OWRS) Assessment

**Regulatory Basis:** Austin City Code Chapter 15-13 (Onsite Water Reuse Systems Ordinance, effective April 1, 2024). OWRS is required for:
1. New commercial, multifamily, and mixed-use development projects with **250,000 SF or more** of gross floor area; or
2. Commercial, multifamily, and mixed-use projects with new or replaced **evaporative cooling towers**.

**Applicability to This Project:**

| Criterion | Triggered? | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Gross floor area >= 250,000 SF | **No** | 7,375 SF is far below the 250,000 SF threshold |
| Evaporative cooling tower | **No** | AutoZone retail stores use packaged rooftop HVAC units, not cooling towers |

**Finding: OWRS IS NOT REQUIRED.** The project is well below both OWRS triggers. No on-site water reuse system design, permitting, or operation is required.

## 9. Water Benchmarking

**Regulatory Basis:** A pre-development Water Benchmarking Application is required for **all** commercial, multifamily, and mixed-use site plan applications submitted on or after December 1, 2021 (Austin City Code Section 25-9, as amended March 2024).

**Applicability:** This requirement applies to the proposed AutoZone project. The Water Benchmarking Application must be:
1. Completed and submitted as part of the site plan application package;
2. Approved by Austin Water as a **condition of site plan approval**.

For projects under 250,000 SF gross floor area, a meeting with Austin Water staff is not required (that threshold applies only to large projects). However, the benchmarking application must demonstrate that the applicant has assessed water usage patterns and considered water conservation and reuse opportunities.

**Action Required:** Complete the Water Benchmarking Application prior to site plan submittal. This is a procedural requirement and is not expected to impose design changes on a 7,375 SF retail building.

## 12. Feasibility Conclusion

**Overall Water & Wastewater Feasibility: FEASIBLE**

The proposed 7,375 SF AutoZone retail store at 9101 Cameron Road has adequate water and wastewater service available from existing infrastructure. The critical finding is the **36" transmission main restriction** -- the applicant must connect to the 8" distribution main, not the 36" line, for both domestic and fire service. This is a routing constraint, not a feasibility barrier, as the 8" main appears to be available within the site frontage.

No Service Extension Request is anticipated. The OWRS ordinance does not apply (project is far below the 250,000 SF threshold). Water Benchmarking is a procedural requirement that will not affect design. Reclaimed water proximity must be verified but is unlikely to apply based on the site's location.

Capital recovery fees are estimated at approximately $19,250, with total utility connection costs in the range of $32,750-$44,750. These are standard costs for a commercial retail project of this size and do not present a feasibility concern.

The wastewater system offers favorable gravity-flow conditions with 3-7 ft of elevation differential between the building pad and the existing wastewater manholes. No lift station or force main is required.

**Critical Path Items:**
1. Confirm 8" water main location and adequacy via Austin Water RAA / pre-submittal meeting
2. Verify reclaimed water main proximity (250 ft threshold)
3. Conduct fire flow test to confirm 8" main pressure and capacity
4. Verify plat date for capital recovery fee calculation

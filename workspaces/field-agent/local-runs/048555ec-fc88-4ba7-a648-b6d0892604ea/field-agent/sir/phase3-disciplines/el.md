# Environmental (EL) — Discipline Assessment

**Property:** 9101 Cameron Rd, Austin, TX 78753 | CS zoning | 1.01 AC (44,150 SF)
**Proposed:** AutoZone Store No. 11094 — 7,375 SF single-story retail
**Discipline:** Environmental (EL) — Watershed protection, CEFs, CWQZ, impervious cover, environmental permitting
**Assessment Date:** 2026-06-04

---

## Verdict

**FEASIBLE WITH MATERIAL CONSTRAINTS.** The proposed development can proceed but is shaped by three overlapping environmental constraints — CWQZ, CEF setback, and watershed impervious cover regulations — that collectively reduce the buildable envelope to the western portion of the parcel. The concept plan layout (building and parking west, WQ pond northwest, environmental buffers east) is directionally correct. No fatal environmental impediments exist.

**Overall Risk: MODERATE** — Site is developable but requires careful navigation of CWQZ boundaries, CEF buffer negotiations, and a watershed classification determination that affects the impervious cover cap.

## 1. Watershed Classification

> **Risk: MODERATE — Classification dispute requires resolution**

### Finding

The site is within the **Little Walnut Creek** drainage system (Walnut Creek / Colorado River Basin). However, the regulatory watershed classification is disputed between Phase 2 research sources:

| Source | Classification | IC Cap | Citation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 2 research (this project) | Urban | No numerical IC cap (95% per CS zoning governs) | LDC Ch. 25-8, Art. 8 |
| Prior research appendix (May 2026) | Suburban | 80% net site area (90% with transfers) | LDC §25-8-392, Art. 9 |
| Austin EV review structure (KB) | Suburban | 60% of gross site area | LDC 25-8-392 |
| Concept plan tracking table | — | 95% allotted | Plan table entry |
| Intake form | — | 80% | Intake form |

**This is a material discrepancy that must be resolved before site plan submittal.** The prior research appendix states that "Walnut Creek and its tributaries (including Little Walnut Creek and Buttermilk Branch) are explicitly classified as Suburban under LDC Title 25, Chapter 8" and notes that "the LDC's Urban classification is reserved for a defined list of Central Austin watersheds." City of Austin staff zoning case sheets are cited confirming the Suburban classification.

If Suburban is correct, the concept plan's "Allotted Impervious Cover = 95%" entry is overstated. The applicable cap would be **60% of gross site area** (per LDC §25-8-392) or potentially 80% of net site area depending on the specific Suburban sub-tier. At the proposed IC of 37.75% (16,666 SF), the project sits well below any of these caps, so **the classification does not currently constrain the proposed design** — but it would constrain any future intensification and affects the Water Quality Transition Zone (WQTZ) requirement (Suburban watersheds have a WQTZ; Urban watersheds do not).

### Action Required

- **Confirm watershed classification** via the COA Watershed Regulation Areas GIS layer (dataset 2xkn-3rmn) at the parcel centroid. The engineer of record (Pape-Dawson) should verify against the official layer and document the result on the site plan cover sheet.
- **Reconcile concept plan tracking table** — if Suburban, update the "Allotted IC" entry from 95% to the correct Suburban cap.

## 2. Critical Water Quality Zone (CWQZ)

> **Risk: HIGH — Non-buildable zone crosses site; constrains building envelope**

### Finding

The concept plan confirms **CWQZ boundaries cross the site in multiple locations**, particularly across the eastern portion. Under LDC §25-8-92, the CWQZ is defined by the 100-year fully-developed-conditions floodplain, subject to minimum/maximum width constraints from the waterway centerline:

**If Urban watershed:**

| Waterway Class | Min CWQZ | Max CWQZ |
|---|---|---|
| Minor | 50 ft from centerline | 100 ft from centerline |
| Intermediate | 100 ft from centerline | 200 ft from centerline |
| Major | 200 ft from centerline | 400 ft from centerline |

**If Suburban watershed:**

| Waterway Class | CWQZ Width |
|---|---|
| Minor (64-320 ac drainage) | 100 ft from centerline |
| Intermediate (320-640 ac) | 200 ft from centerline |
| Major (>640 ac) | 300 ft from centerline |

The watercourse is conveyed through a **3x6-ft box culvert** in the Cameron Road ROW. Based on culvert size and drainage context, the waterway is most likely classified as a **minor waterway**, establishing a CWQZ of 50-100 ft (Urban) or 100 ft (Suburban) from the centerline.

### CWQZ Development Restrictions (LDC §25-8-261)

- **Development is prohibited** in the CWQZ except for specifically enumerated exceptions
- Permitted exceptions: open space, trails, drainage facilities (outfalls, energy dissipation), stormwater controls (with locational restrictions), vegetative filter strips
- **Buildings, parking, and new impervious cover are prohibited** within the CWQZ
- Utility lines and storm drains prohibited except for necessary crossings (LDC §25-8-261(E))
- Vegetation clearing and grading are restricted

### Water Quality Transition Zone (WQTZ)

If the watershed is confirmed as Suburban, a **WQTZ applies outboard of the CWQZ** (LDC §25-8-93). For a minor waterway, the WQTZ extends an additional ~100 ft beyond the CWQZ (total ~200 ft from centerline). The WQTZ may cover most of the eastern portion of the lot. Development in the WQTZ is permitted but subject to enhanced water quality treatment and native vegetation requirements.

**If the watershed is Urban, no WQTZ applies.** This is another reason the watershed classification is material.

### Concept Plan Assessment

The concept plan positions the AutoZone building and parking west of the CWQZ boundaries, which is correct. The preliminary detention/WQ pond is shown at the NW corner. If the pond falls within the CWQZ, it is permissible only if it meets locational requirements: (a) at least 100 ft from the waterway centerline (minor waterway, Suburban), (b) outside the 100-year floodplain, and (c) outside the Erosion Hazard Zone. The concept plan geometry appears consistent with these rules, but dimensioned verification is required.

The storm line crossing the CWQZ will require a "necessary crossing" determination.

### Cross-Reference

- **fwp discipline:** CWQZ boundaries are derived from the fully-developed-conditions floodplain. The fwp discipline should confirm the floodplain extents, which in turn define the CWQZ. The City of Austin's locally-modeled floodplain (more conservative than FEMA) governs per LDC §25-7.

## 3. Critical Environmental Features (CEFs)

> **Risk: HIGH — CEF setback lines on concept plan; buffer reduces buildable area**

### Finding

The concept plan shows **CEF setback (CEF STBK) lines** on the eastern and northeastern portions of the site. Under LDC §25-8-281 and ECM §1.10.0, CEFs in Austin include springs, seeps, bluffs, canyon rimrock, caves, sinkholes, and wetlands.

**Probable CEF type:** Given the site's geologic setting (Blackland Prairie, Pecan Gap Chalk / Taylor Group clays east of the Balcones Escarpment), karst features (caves, sinkholes, point recharge) are geologically implausible. The most likely CEF is a **spring, seep, or wetland** associated with the Little Walnut Creek tributary, or possibly a riparian feature where the channel has eroded into the underlying Taylor/Pecan Gap clays.

### Buffer Requirements (ECM §1.10.4)

| Buffer Tier | Distance | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Default | **150 ft** from CEF edge | All CEF types |
| Administrative reduction | Minimum **50 ft** | Site-specific hydrogeologic justification demonstrating preservation of flow quantity and quality |
| Below 50 ft | Requires **Land Use Commission variance** | LDC §25-8-41 |
| Wetland CEF | **300 ft** default | ECM §1.10.4 |

Within the CEF buffer: **construction is prohibited**, natural vegetation must be retained to the maximum extent practicable, and irrigation/wastewater disposal is prohibited.

### Development Impact

The CEF setback areas on the eastern/NE portion of the site are **non-buildable** unless an administrative reduction or variance is obtained. The proposed building footprint appears positioned west of the CEF setback lines, which is appropriate. Any grading, paving, or utility installation within the CEF buffer is prohibited absent an approved exception.

### Action Required

- **Request a formal CEF determination** from the COA Environmental Officer (Watershed Protection Department, Environmental Resource Management) before site plan submittal. This will confirm whether the CEF buffer line on the concept plan derives from an inventoried feature or is an engineer-drawn placeholder.
- If a CEF is field-verified, the 150-ft buffer becomes a permanent design constraint. An administrative reduction to 50 ft is possible with hydrogeologic justification but requires additional consultant work.
- The CEF determination should identify the specific CEF type, which drives the applicable buffer width (150 ft standard vs. 300 ft for wetlands).

## 4. Impervious Cover

> **Risk: LOW — Proposed IC well below any applicable cap**

### Finding

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Lot area | 44,150 SF (1.01 AC) |
| Proposed IC | 16,666 SF / 37.75% |
| CS zoning base IC | 95% |
| Urban watershed IC cap | No numerical cap (WQ controls required instead) |
| Suburban watershed IC cap | 60% gross (or 80% net with transfers to 90%) |
| Concept plan "Allotted IC" | 95% |
| Intake form IC limit | 80% |

Regardless of which watershed classification is confirmed, the proposed IC of **37.75%** is well below all possible caps. The IC percentage does not constrain this project.

However, the **effective buildable IC** is further reduced by the CWQZ and CEF setback areas, which are non-developable. The net developable area (outside CWQZ and CEF buffers) is the only area where impervious cover can be placed. The 37.75% figure is calculated against total lot area; the IC as a percentage of the net developable area will be significantly higher.

### Water Quality Controls Required

Structural water quality treatment is required regardless of watershed classification. The concept plan shows a **preliminary detention/WQ pond in the NW corner**, consistent with sedimentation/filtration (S/F) or biofiltration requirements.

| Requirement | Specification | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Treatment type | Sedimentation/filtration OR biofiltration | ECM §1.6.5, §1.6.7 |
| Water quality volume | Per ECM §1.6.0 methodology | ECM §1.6.0 |
| Sedimentation basin drawdown | 48-hour | ECM §1.6.2 |
| Maintenance | Annual third-party inspection; maintenance agreement recorded | ECM §1.6.3, §1.14.0 |
| Fee-in-lieu alternative | Available with WPD Director approval | LDC Ch. 25-8, Art. 8 |

**RSMP (Regional Stormwater Management Program):** The programs research identified RSMP fee-in-lieu as potentially feasible, which would eliminate the on-site detention requirement and free developable area on this compact 1.01-acre lot. RSMP eligibility must be verified through the City's RSMP Feasibility Viewer and a Feasibility Meeting with the RSMP Team. Estimated fee: $50K-$150K.

## 5. Erosion Hazard Zone (EHZ)

> **Risk: MODERATE — EHZ analysis required; may affect pond placement**

### Finding

Under LDC §25-7-32, a building permit application for development **within 100 ft of a creek** requires an EHZ analysis. Given that CWQZ boundaries and drainage features cross the site, portions of the property within 100 ft of the waterway centerline trigger the EHZ review requirement.

The concept plan shows an "Existing Erosion Hazard Zone" line. The EHZ is not a hard prohibition — improvements can be placed within it if protective works (channel stabilization, retaining walls, scour protection) are designed to withstand the 100-year flood under fully-eroded conditions. However, **stormwater controls (the proposed WQ pond) are prohibited inside the EHZ unless protective works are provided** (per LDC §25-8-261 cross-reference).

### Action Required

- Pape-Dawson should verify the EHZ line on the concept plan against the current Watershed Protection determination, or commission a site-specific EHZ analysis per Drainage Criteria Manual Appendix E.
- Confirm the NW-corner WQ pond placement is outside the EHZ, or design protective works if it falls within.

## 6. Edwards Aquifer / Barton Springs Zone

> **Risk: NONE**

The site is **NOT located within** any of the following:
- Edwards Aquifer Recharge Zone (EARZ)
- Edwards Aquifer Contributing Zone (EACZ)
- Barton Springs Zone (BSZ)

The Cameron Rd parcel sits in the Blackland Prairie geologic province (Taylor Group clays/marls) east of the Balcones Escarpment — geologically incapable of being a recharge zone for the Edwards limestone.

**No TCEQ Edwards Aquifer Protection Plan (EAPP) is required** under 30 TAC §213. This significantly reduces environmental permitting complexity relative to comparable sites in southwest Austin.

## 8. Environmental Resource Inventory (ERI)

> **Risk: LOW — Procedurally required; not a design constraint**

### Finding

An ERI is **required** for this site under LDC §25-8-121. The ERI trigger is satisfied because the site contains CWQZ, and likely 100-year floodplain and/or slopes within proximity to the waterway.

The ERI must be submitted with the site plan per ECM Appendix 1.3.0 and is valid for 7 years. The ERI will include:
- Biological assessment (vegetation survey, wildlife habitat evaluation)
- CEF identification and mapping
- CWQZ/WQTZ delineation
- Floodplain mapping
- Slope analysis
- Soil and geologic assessment

This is a procedural requirement that feeds the CEF determination and CWQZ delineation. It does not independently constrain the design but must be completed before site plan approval.

## 10. Phase I Environmental Site Assessment

> **Risk: MODERATE-HIGH — Recommend commissioning before closing**

### Finding

The prior research appendix flagged a material concern: the property's **current use is an engine service shop** (D & M Precision Inc., dba Precision Engine Service). This is a textbook ASTM E1527-21 Recognized Environmental Condition (REC) screen. Engine/auto service shops are associated with:

- Used motor oil, hydraulic fluids, antifreeze, solvents
- Floor drains potentially discharging to dry wells or sanitary sewer
- Historic above-ground or underground storage tanks (ASTs/USTs)
- Parts-washer sludge and waste oil accumulation

The adjacent property to the north is the **Housing Authority of the City of Austin** (residential receptor), which elevates any subsurface contamination concern due to potential vapor-intrusion pathways and more stringent TCEQ TRRP residential cleanup levels.

### Action Required

- **Commission an ASTM E1527-21 Phase I ESA before closing.** Scope should include:
  - TCEQ Petroleum Storage Tank (PST) and Leaking PST (LPST) registry search
  - TCEQ Voluntary Cleanup Program (VCP) database search
  - EDR/ERIS regulatory database radius search (standard ASTM radii)
  - Historic Sanborn maps, aerial photos, city directories
  - Site reconnaissance for ASTs/USTs, oil/water separators, floor drains, staining
- If Phase I returns RECs, budget $15,000-$40,000 for Phase II ESA (soil and groundwater sampling).

## 12. Soils and Geology

> **Risk: LOW — Standard for north Austin; geotech required**

The site is in the Blackland Prairie geologic province. Surface geology is likely **Pecan Gap Chalk** (Taylor Group, Upper Cretaceous) overlain by 2-10 ft of expansive clay residuum. Dominant soil units are expected to be **Houston Black clay** and/or **Heiden clay** — fine, smectitic, thermic Udic Haplusterts (Vertisols) with:

- **High shrink-swell potential** (Linear Extensibility >9%; PI typically 35-55)
- **Low infiltration rate** (Hydrologic Soil Group D) — favors detention/biofiltration with under-drains rather than infiltration-based controls
- **Depth to bedrock:** typically 5-15 ft; adequate bearing capacity for drilled piers

Foundation design must accommodate expansive-soil movement. Typical options: post-tensioned slab, drilled piers to competent rock, or moisture barriers. A **Phase II geotechnical investigation** is required before foundation design.

## 1. Water Quality Treatment Trigger

**Triggered.** New and redeveloped impervious cover exceeding 8,000 SF outside the Barton Springs Zone requires structural water quality controls under LDC Section 25-8-211 and ECM Section 1.9.0. The proposed project adds 16,666 SF of impervious cover, well above the 8,000 SF threshold.

Because this is an Urban Watershed site, there is no numeric impervious cover cap. Instead, the regulatory framework requires structural treatment of stormwater runoff from all new impervious surfaces.

**Water Quality Volume (WQV) calculation basis:** ECM Section 1.6.7 requires capture and treatment of the first 0.5 inches of runoff (for sedimentation/filtration) or 1.0 inches (for biofiltration/bioretention) from the contributing impervious area. Using the 0.5-inch standard for a conventional S/F system:

- Contributing IC area: 16,666 SF
- WQV (0.5-inch capture): 16,666 SF x 0.5 in / 12 = ~694 CF (~5,190 gallons)

If a GSI-compliant bioretention system is used with 1.0-inch capture depth, the WQV doubles to approximately 1,389 CF.

**Citation:** LDC 25-8-211; ECM 1.6.0, 1.6.7, 1.9.0.

## 2. Green Stormwater Infrastructure (GSI) Mandate

**Mandatory.** Effective November 2022, LDC Section 25-8-213(C) requires Green Stormwater Infrastructure for all new development and redevelopment subject to water quality treatment. GSI-compliant systems include bioretention, rain gardens, permeable pavement, green roofs, and vegetated filter strips that provide volume reduction through infiltration and evapotranspiration.

A conventional sedimentation/filtration pond (sand filter) is **not GSI-compliant** by default. To use a conventional S/F system instead of GSI, the applicant must obtain an exemption from the Watershed Protection Department (WPD) Director. Exemption requests must demonstrate that GSI is infeasible due to site-specific constraints such as:

- High groundwater table
- Contaminated soils
- Insufficient permeable soil depth
- Incompatible geotechnical conditions (expansive clay, shallow bedite)
- Space constraints that preclude distributed GSI elements

**Feasibility assessment:** The concept plan shows a single "Preliminary Pond" in the NW corner, which suggests the engineer is initially planning a conventional combined detention/WQ pond rather than distributed GSI. Given the 1.01-acre lot with CWQZ and CEF setback encumbrances reducing usable area, there may be legitimate space constraints supporting an exemption request. However, WPD reviewers will likely push for at least partial GSI compliance — for example, bioretention planters integrated into the parking lot islands or permeable pavement in low-traffic parking stalls — before granting a full exemption.

**Recommendation:** Design the WQ system as a **bioretention basin** (GSI-compliant) rather than a conventional sand filter. If the preliminary pond footprint shown on the concept plan is retained, it can be designed as a bioretention cell with engineered soil media, underdrain, and overflow to the storm system. This satisfies GSI requirements without requiring an exemption and avoids review delays. If conventional S/F is preferred, prepare a written exemption justification documenting site constraints.

**Risk level:** MODERATE. The exemption path adds review time (typically 2-4 weeks) and is discretionary. Designing to GSI from the outset is the path of least resistance.

**Citation:** LDC 25-8-213(C); ECM 1.6.0; WPD GSI Design Manual (2023).

## 3. Detention Requirements — No-Increase Rule

**Required.** Austin's Drainage Criteria Manual (DCM) Section 8.4.0 imposes a no-increase (no-adverse-impact) standard: post-development peak discharge rates must not exceed pre-development rates for the 2-year, 10-year, 25-year, and 100-year design storms at every point of discharge from the site. Atlas 14 rainfall data (NOAA Atlas 14, Volume 11) is mandatory for all hydrologic calculations.

### Pre- vs. Post-Development Hydrology

| Parameter | Pre-Development | Post-Development | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| IC area | ~11,708 SF (26.5%) | 16,666 SF (37.75%) | +4,958 SF (+11.25%) |
| Pervious area | ~32,442 SF | ~27,484 SF | -4,958 SF |
| Runoff coefficient (est.) | ~0.55 | ~0.65 | +0.10 |

The 11.25-percentage-point increase in IC generates meaningful additional runoff volume and peak flow. Detention is required to attenuate post-development peaks to pre-development levels across all four design storms.

### 2-Year On-Site Control

Regardless of RSMP participation (discussed below), Austin requires **on-site control of the 2-year storm event**. This is a non-waivable requirement — even if RSMP fee-in-lieu is used for the 10/25/100-year storms, a detention facility sized for the 2-year event must be constructed on site.

### Preliminary Detention Sizing Estimate

Using simplified Rational Method assumptions for a 1.01-acre site in north Austin (Atlas 14 rainfall intensities for Travis County):

| Storm Event | Atlas 14 Rainfall (24-hr) | Est. Pre-Dev Peak (cfs) | Est. Post-Dev Peak (cfs) | Approx. Detention Volume Needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2-year | ~3.7 in | ~2.0 | ~2.8 | ~1,500-2,500 CF |
| 10-year | ~5.3 in | ~3.5 | ~4.7 | ~2,500-3,500 CF |
| 25-year | ~6.5 in | ~4.5 | ~5.9 | ~3,500-5,000 CF |
| 100-year | ~8.8 in | ~6.5 | ~8.2 | ~5,000-7,500 CF |

These are order-of-magnitude estimates. Formal TR-55 or HEC-HMS modeling with Atlas 14 IDF curves will refine volumes. The controlling storm for pond sizing is typically the 100-year event (~5,000-7,500 CF of required storage).

**Citation:** DCM 8.4.0; NOAA Atlas 14, Volume 11; ECM 1.4.0 (Hydrologic Calculations).

## 4. RSMP Fee-in-Lieu Eligibility

**Potentially eligible — requires verification.** The Regional Stormwater Management Program (RSMP) allows developers to pay a fee-in-lieu of constructing on-site detention for the 10-year, 25-year, and 100-year storms, provided:

1. The site is in an **RSMP-participating watershed** (Little Walnut Creek eligibility must be confirmed via the RSMP Feasibility Viewer)
2. The development produces **no adverse flooding impact** to adjacent properties, roadways, or structures
3. The **2-year storm is still controlled on-site** (RSMP does not waive 2-year detention)

### If RSMP Is Available

- **On-site requirement reduces to:** 2-year detention only (~1,500-2,500 CF), plus WQ treatment
- **Fee estimate:** $50,000-$150,000 based on typical RSMP payments for ~1 AC commercial sites in north Austin (per FY26 Payment Calculator)
- **Site design benefit:** Significantly smaller pond footprint. A combined WQ/2-year detention facility could be as small as ~2,000-3,000 CF, which is feasible in the NW corner shown on the concept plan

### If RSMP Is Not Available

- **On-site requirement:** Full detention for 2/10/25/100-year storms (~5,000-7,500 CF) plus WQ treatment
- **Pond footprint impact:** A combined WQ/detention facility of this volume on a 1.01-acre site with CWQZ/CEF constraints will consume a meaningful portion of the remaining buildable area. At 4-5 ft average depth, the pond footprint would be approximately 1,500-2,000 SF of surface area — feasible but tight in the NW location shown

### Action Item

Verify RSMP eligibility through the City of Austin RSMP Feasibility Viewer tool and schedule an RSMP Feasibility Meeting with the RSMP Team. This is a **critical early-stage decision** that drives pond sizing and site layout.

**Citation:** Austin WPD RSMP Program; DCM 8.6.0 (Regional Detention).

## 5. Pond Sizing Feasibility — NW Location

The concept plan shows a "Preliminary Pond" northwest of the building, between the building and Cameron Road. This location is assessed for feasibility:

### Site Constraints at NW Pond Location

- **Elevation:** Site contours show ~632-634 ft in the NW area, draining northward toward Cameron Rd
- **Existing storm infrastructure:** 24-inch storm line with grate inlet (Top of Grate: 632.84, 24" FL(W): 628.21) — provides approximately 4.6 ft of head between grate top and flowline for pond outlet design
- **Drainage easement:** Recorded drainage easement (Doc. No. 2014138374) exists on site — pond location must respect easement terms or be coordinated with easement holder
- **CWQZ:** CWQZ boundaries cross portions of the NW area — pond must be outside CWQZ unless functioning as a passive open-space element (no new IC within CWQZ)
- **ROW reserve:** 25-ft ROW reserve along Cameron Rd (Doc. No. 201800004) — pond cannot encroach into potential ROW dedication area
- **Heritage trees:** Heritage trees (#15 Live Oak 28", #16 American Elm 29.5") may have CRZ conflicts depending on exact pond grading limits

### Sizing Scenarios

**Scenario A — RSMP Available (2-year detention + WQ only):**
- Combined volume: ~2,500-3,500 CF (WQ + 2-yr detention)
- At 4 ft average depth: ~625-875 SF surface area
- **Feasibility: HIGH.** This volume fits comfortably in the NW area shown on the concept plan

**Scenario B — Full On-Site Detention (no RSMP):**
- Combined volume: ~6,000-8,000 CF (WQ + 2/10/25/100-yr detention)
- At 4-5 ft average depth: ~1,500-2,000 SF surface area
- **Feasibility: MODERATE.** Achievable but consumes significant area. Pond geometry must be carefully shaped around CWQZ, easements, CRZ, and the ROW reserve. May require deeper excavation (5-6 ft) to reduce footprint, which tightens outlet design with available head.

### Outlet Design

The existing 24-inch storm line and grate inlet system on Cameron Rd provides the discharge point. The pond outlet structure (typically a riser with orifice plates sized for multi-stage release) will connect to this system. The 4.6 ft of available head (632.84 grate to 628.21 flowline) is adequate for a multi-stage outlet controlling 2-yr through 100-yr releases, though the 100-yr emergency spillway will need to be routed to avoid Cameron Rd flooding.

**Storm drain connection:** The site is within 300 ft of an existing public storm drain system, triggering the mandatory connection requirement under DCM Section 8.2.0. The 24-inch line along Cameron Rd is the receiving system.

## 6. Drainage Pattern and Offsite Contributions

**Site drainage:** The site drains generally northward toward Cameron Rd, with an elevation range of 626-636 ft (approximately 10 ft of fall across the site, NE-to-SW high to NW low). This natural drainage pattern supports the NW pond location — runoff from the proposed IC (building, parking, drives) can be collected by site drainage infrastructure and routed to the NW pond by gravity.

**Offsite contributions:** The eastern portion of the site has CWQZ boundaries and CEF setbacks associated with drainage features that may carry offsite flows across or adjacent to the property. The detention/WQ system should be designed to treat **on-site runoff only** — offsite flows should be bypassed around the pond via the existing drainage corridor. A flow splitter or diversion structure may be needed if offsite drainage intermingles with on-site runoff.

**Discharge point:** All treated and detained runoff will discharge to the Cameron Rd storm system (24-inch line) at the NW corner. The no-increase analysis must demonstrate that the pond outlet hydrograph does not increase peak flows in the 24-inch receiving line for any design storm.

## 9. Key Findings and Recommendations

1. **Design WQ as bioretention (GSI-compliant) from the outset.** The NW pond can be designed as a bioretention basin with engineered media, underdrain, and overflow. This avoids the GSI exemption process and aligns with current WPD policy direction. The additional design effort is minimal compared to the review risk of seeking an exemption.

2. **Verify RSMP eligibility immediately.** This is the single most impactful variable for site design. If RSMP is available, the pond shrinks from ~6,000-8,000 CF to ~2,500-3,500 CF, freeing approximately 600-1,000 SF of site area and simplifying outlet design. Schedule the RSMP Feasibility Meeting as a pre-application step.

3. **Retain 2-year on-site detention regardless of RSMP.** Even with RSMP fee-in-lieu, the 2-year storm must be controlled on-site. The combined WQ/2-year facility is the minimum on-site requirement.

4. **Coordinate pond grading with CWQZ delineation, heritage tree CRZ, drainage easement, and ROW reserve.** The NW pond location has multiple overlapping constraints. An early constraint overlay map should be prepared to confirm the available pond footprint before finalizing building placement.

5. **Atlas 14 rainfall data will increase detention volumes** compared to older rainfall datasets. Ensure the engineer uses NOAA Atlas 14, Volume 11 data for Travis County — this is mandatory and produces higher design storms than the legacy TP-40 values historically used in Austin.

6. **TPDES CGP is required.** Prepare the SWPPP and Notice of Intent (NOI) filing concurrently with site plan preparation to avoid construction schedule delays.

7. **48-hour drawdown must be verified** for the WQ facility during design and confirmed by observation during construction certification. This is a common point of failure in WPD inspections — design the outlet orifice conservatively.

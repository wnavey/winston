## 1. Regulatory Framework

Tree protection for this site is governed by:

- **Austin LDC Chapter 25-8, Subchapter B** -- Tree and Natural Area Protection
- **LDC Section 25-8-601 et seq.** -- Protected Tree definitions and removal permits
- **LDC Section 25-8-641 et seq.** -- Heritage Tree provisions
- **Environmental Criteria Manual (ECM) Section 3.5.0** -- Tree Protection standards
- **ECM Section 3.5.4** -- Invasive species exemptions
- **ECM Section 3.7.0** -- Erosion and Sedimentation Control (ESC) requirements
- **LDC Chapter 25-8, Subchapter A, Article 8** -- Urban Watershed environmental controls

### Key Thresholds (City of Austin)

| Category | Diameter Threshold | Permit Required | Mitigation Required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unregulated | Under 8" DBH | No | No |
| Regulated | 8" or greater DBH | Yes (removal permit) | Caliper-inch replacement |
| Protected | 19" or greater DBH | Yes | Enhanced mitigation |
| Heritage | 24" or greater DBH | Yes + cannot be removed absent Land Use Commission variance | Removal prohibited; variance extremely difficult |
| Exempt invasive species | Any size | Permit required | No mitigation (ECM 3.5.4.A.2.b) |

### 2.2 Critical Finding: Misclassified Invasive Species

**Ligustrum (#17, 33"):** Ligustrum (all species -- L. lucidum, L. japonicum, L. sinense, L. vulgare) is listed on the City of Austin's exempt invasive species list per ECM 3.5.4.A.2.b. Under the ordinance, exempt invasive species **cannot qualify as Heritage trees regardless of size**. A removal permit is still required, but no mitigation is owed. The Heritage Tree Survey's classification of this tree as "(H) Heritage" is erroneous. This has significant implications:

- The 33" Ligustrum does **not** require Heritage tree protections, does **not** require a CRZ exclusion zone for construction, and could be removed with a standard removal permit and no mitigation.
- If the applicant chooses to retain it, no CRZ-based construction restrictions apply beyond best-practice root protection.
- If the applicant chooses to remove it, no Land Use Commission variance is needed and no mitigation is owed.

**Arizona Ash (#13, 19" and #18, 39" multi-trunk):** Arizona Ash (Fraxinus velutina) is also on the exempt invasive list. These trees are labeled "Protected" on the survey but, as exempt invasive species, they do not carry Protected tree status. Removal would require a permit but no mitigation.

**Recommendation:** The applicant's arborist should submit a revised Heritage/Protected Tree Survey correcting the classifications for trees #13, #17, and #18. This correction will simplify CRZ compliance and may open additional site-design flexibility.

### 2.3 Sycamore (#14) Reclassification

The survey classifies the 25.5" Sycamore as "(P) Protected 19-23.5 in" but the tree is 25.5" DBH, which exceeds the 24" Heritage threshold. American Sycamore (Platanus occidentalis) is a native species and is not on the invasive exempt list. **This tree should be reclassified as Heritage.** Its CRZ radius would be 25.5 feet.

## 4. Critical Root Zone (CRZ) Analysis

Under Austin LDC, the CRZ radius equals the DBH in inches converted to feet. CRZ encroachment triggers review; encroachment beyond 25% of the CRZ area requires a Land Use Commission variance for Heritage trees.

### 4.1 Correctly Classified Heritage Trees (CRZ Applies)

| # | Size | Species | CRZ Radius | CRZ Area (sq ft) | Proximity Concern |
|---|------|---------|------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 14 | 25.5" | Sycamore | 25.5 ft | ~2,043 SF | **Reclassified to Heritage.** CRZ must be evaluated against building/parking/pond placement. |
| 15 | 28" | Live Oak | 28.0 ft | ~2,463 SF | CRZ may overlap with building footprint, parking, or grading depending on tree location. |
| 16 | 29.5" | American Elm | 29.5 ft | ~2,734 SF | 29.5-ft radius is significant on a constrained 1.01-AC site. |

**Ligustrum #17 (33"):** CRZ protections do **not** apply -- exempt invasive species. No construction exclusion zone required.

**Arizona Ash #18 (39" multi-trunk):** CRZ protections do **not** apply -- exempt invasive species. No construction exclusion zone required.

### 4.2 CRZ Conflict Assessment

The concept plan positions the building centrally on the western half of the site with parking to the south. Heritage trees #15, #16, and the reclassified #14 are shown as "Remain" but their precise locations relative to the building footprint, parking lot, and preliminary pond are critical.

**Potential CRZ conflicts to evaluate at site plan:**

1. **Building footprint vs. Heritage tree CRZs:** The 7,375 SF building at a 20-ft scale appears to measure roughly 55 ft x 134 ft. If any Heritage tree trunk is within ~30 ft of the building edge, CRZ encroachment occurs. The concept plan shows the building positioned to avoid Heritage CRZs, but this must be verified with survey-grade tree locations.

2. **Parking lot grading:** The 12-space parking area south of the building requires subgrade preparation. If Heritage tree CRZs extend into the parking area, root cuts could exceed the 25% CRZ encroachment threshold.

3. **Preliminary pond excavation:** The detention/WQ pond in the NW portion of the site requires excavation. If Heritage tree #14 (Sycamore, 25.5-ft CRZ) or #15 (Live Oak, 28-ft CRZ) are near the pond, excavation could damage roots within the CRZ.

4. **Utility trenching:** The site connects to existing 36" water, 8" wastewater, and 24" storm lines along Cameron Rd. Utility trench routes through Heritage CRZs would require tunneling/boring rather than open-cut trenching.

### 4.3 CRZ Protection Measures Required During Construction

For all retained Heritage and Protected trees (where protections apply), the following measures are required per ECM 3.5.2:

- **Tree protection fencing:** 6-ft chain-link or equivalent at the CRZ perimeter, installed before any clearing/grading begins and maintained throughout construction
- **No storage of materials, equipment, or spoils within CRZ**
- **No grade changes** (fill or cut) within CRZ without arborist-approved root protection plan
- **No trenching within CRZ** -- directional boring required for utilities crossing CRZ
- **Mulch layer** (4-6 inches of hardwood mulch) within CRZ where foot traffic is unavoidable
- **Pre-construction arborist meeting** with City Forester to document tree conditions

## 5. Heritage Tree Variance Assessment

### 5.1 Variance Requirement

Under LDC 25-8-643, Heritage trees cannot be removed without a Land Use Commission (LUC) variance. A variance requires the applicant to demonstrate:

1. The tree prevents reasonable use of the property
2. No feasible alternative site design can preserve the tree
3. The tree's removal is the minimum necessary to allow development

### 5.2 Current Variance Exposure

**No Heritage tree removal is currently proposed.** The concept plan designates all Heritage-qualifying trees as "Remain." Therefore, no LUC Heritage tree variance is required at this time.

However, if CRZ encroachment analysis (to be performed at site plan) reveals that building/parking/pond placement encroaches more than 25% of a Heritage tree's CRZ area, an **administrative CRZ encroachment variance** will be required.

### 5.3 Risk Reduction from Invasive Reclassification

Correcting the classification of Ligustrum #17 and Arizona Ash #18 from Heritage/Protected to exempt invasive eliminates two potential variance triggers. The applicant's variance exposure drops from five potential Heritage/Protected CRZ conflicts to three (Sycamore #14, Live Oak #15, American Elm #16).

## 6. Erosion and Sedimentation Control (ESC)

### 6.1 ESC Plan Requirement

The proposed development disturbs more than 1 acre (lot area is 1.01 AC), which triggers:

- **TCEQ Construction General Permit (TXR150000)** -- Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan (SW3P) required
- **EPA NPDES coverage** via TCEQ CGP
- **City of Austin ESC Plan** per ECM Section 3.7.0 and LDC 25-8-181

Even if the actual disturbance area is slightly under 1 acre after excluding CWQZ/CEF areas, the lot area itself meets the threshold, and Austin requires an ESC plan for essentially all commercial site plans.

### 6.2 ESC Plan Elements Required

Per ECM 3.7.0, the ESC plan must include:

1. **Silt fence** along downslope perimeter, particularly:
   - Along the eastern boundary (downslope toward drainage features/CWQZ)
   - Along the northern boundary (toward Cameron Rd drainage)
2. **Construction entrance** with stabilized rock pad at the Cameron Rd driveway
3. **Inlet protection** on all existing storm inlets (grate inlets along Cameron Rd at elevations 632.84 and 629.49)
4. **Temporary sediment basin** or sediment trap -- may be combined with the preliminary pond excavation if phased appropriately
5. **Erosion control blankets** on slopes steeper than 3:1 (site has 4-6 ft grade change across ~300 ft, so slopes are generally gentle at ~1.5-2%)
6. **Stabilization timeline:** Exposed soils must be stabilized within 14 days of final grading per TCEQ CGP
7. **Tree protection fencing** doubles as a sediment barrier at CRZ perimeters

### 6.3 CWQZ and CEF Buffer Protections During Construction

The CWQZ and CEF setback areas on the eastern/northeastern portions of the site require enhanced ESC measures:

- **Double silt fence** or reinforced silt fence along the CWQZ boundary
- **No construction staging, stockpiling, or equipment access** within CWQZ or CEF buffer
- **Turbidity monitoring** if dewatering is required during pond excavation
- **Vegetative buffer preservation** within the CWQZ -- no clearing permitted

### 6.4 Erosion Hazard Zone (EHZ)

Per Phase 2 environmental research, an EHZ analysis is likely required given the proximity of drainage features to the site. If the EHZ analysis identifies erosion risk near the building or pond, additional setbacks or bank stabilization may be required. The EHZ analysis should be completed early in the site plan process to avoid design conflicts.

## 9. Summary Assessment

The tree protection posture for this site is **generally favorable** for the proposed AutoZone development, with one significant finding that benefits the applicant:

**The Heritage Tree Survey contains classification errors for three trees on the exempt invasive species list.** Ligustrum #17 (33"), Arizona Ash #13 (19"), and Arizona Ash #18 (39" multi-trunk) are all exempt invasive species under ECM 3.5.4.A.2.b and cannot hold Heritage or Protected status. Correcting these classifications eliminates three CRZ compliance zones and removes the most restrictive tree on the survey (the 33" Ligustrum with a 33-ft CRZ) from the Heritage protection regime entirely.

Conversely, **Sycamore #14 (25.5") is underclassified** and should be Heritage rather than Protected, adding one CRZ constraint.

After correction, the site has **three true Heritage trees** (#14 Sycamore 25.5", #15 Live Oak 28", #16 American Elm 29.5") requiring CRZ protection. The four trees proposed for removal are either exempt invasive species (no mitigation) or potentially so (Mulberry #11, pending species confirmation). The maximum mitigation exposure is 11 caliper inches if Mulberry #11 is a native Red Mulberry.

ESC requirements are standard for a 1-acre commercial site in an Urban Watershed, with heightened attention needed at the CWQZ/CEF buffer interface on the eastern portion of the site.

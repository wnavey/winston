# Zoning & Land Use Findings -- 9101 Cameron Rd

**Property:** 9101 Cameron Rd, Austin, TX 78753 | Parcel 928312 | CS zoning, no overlays | 1.01 AC / 44,150 SF
**Proposed:** AutoZone retail store (Retail Sale of Auto Parts), 7,375 SF Prototype 74, single-story, 12 parking spaces
**Discipline:** Zoning & Land Use (ZLU)
**Date:** 2026-06-04

---

## Summary

- **Use is permitted by right in CS with no conditional use permit, rezoning, or variance required.** The entitlement pathway is fully administrative (site plan only), with no City Council or Planning Commission hearings needed. This is the lowest-risk entitlement path available in Austin.
- **All proposed dimensional standards comply with wide margins.** The project uses 0.17:1 of the 2:1 FAR allowance, 37.75% of the 95% IC allowance, and roughly one-third of the 60-foot height limit. No dimensional variance is needed.
- **The condominium regime (Cameron Ferguson Condominiums, Unit 1) is the primary zoning-adjacent risk.** How the City calculates dimensional standards -- against Unit 1's boundaries or the parent tract -- is unresolved. The condominium declaration may impose private use or design restrictions beyond CS zoning requirements.
- **Subchapter E (Design Standards and Mixed Use) governs site and building design.** The AutoZone Prototype 74 must comply with facade articulation, blank-wall limitations, and pedestrian connectivity requirements. Alternative compliance is available administratively if the standard prototype conflicts.
- **Austin eliminated minimum parking citywide (Ord. 20231102-028, eff. Nov 12, 2023).** The 12 proposed spaces are an operator business decision, not a code requirement. Only ADA accessible parking (federal/TAS) and bicycle parking (LDC 25-6) remain mandatory.

## Findings

### ZLU-1: Use Permitted by Right -- No Discretionary Approval Required

**Severity:** Informational (favorable)
**Citation:** LDC 25-2-491(C) (Permitted Use Chart); LDC 25-2-3 (CS district description)
**Finding:** The CS (General Commercial Services) district permits retail sales uses by right ("P" designation in the Permitted Use Chart). "Retail Sale of Auto Parts" falls within the general retail sales use category, not the automotive repair/service category that carries conditional use restrictions in some districts. No CUP, rezoning, PUD, variance, or discretionary hearing is required.
**Implication:** The project follows a fully administrative review path. The only required City approval is the site development permit (site plan), reviewed by Development Services Department staff. No City Council, Planning Commission, or Board of Adjustment review is triggered. This eliminates political/discretionary risk and reduces the entitlement timeline to an estimated 4-8 months from site plan submission to building permit issuance.
**Next step:** Submit site plan application through Austin Build + Connect (AB+C) with confidence that use entitlement is not at risk.

---

### ZLU-2: No Combining Districts or Overlays

**Severity:** Informational (favorable)
**Citation:** City of Austin Open Data Portal, Zoning By Address dataset (nbzi-qabm); LDC 25-2, Subchapter A, Art. 2, Div. 6
**Finding:** The zoning string for 9101 Cameron Rd is "CS" with no suffix. No Neighborhood Plan (NP), Conditional Overlay (CO), Mixed Use (MU), NCCD, TOD, Capitol View Corridor, Historic, University Neighborhood Overlay, or Waterfront Overlay applies. This is notable because neighboring parcels carry overlays: 9201 Cameron Rd has CS-MU-CO, and parcels across Cameron Rd carry LI-NP.
**Implication:** No overlay-specific design standards, use limitations, or additional review processes constrain the development. The project is governed solely by the base CS district standards and generally applicable LDC requirements (Subchapter E, environmental, transportation). This is the simplest regulatory profile available for a commercial site in Austin.
**Next step:** No action required. Confirm zoning string at pre-application conference for the record.

### ZLU-3: Subchapter E Design Standards Apply

**Severity:** Medium
**Citation:** LDC Ch. 25-2, Subchapter E, Articles 1-5 (Design Standards and Mixed Use)
**Finding:** New non-residential construction outside the Downtown Overlay triggers Subchapter E compliance. The proposed 7,375 SF AutoZone building on a CS-zoned parcel falls squarely within this requirement. The governing provisions include:

- **Article 1 (Connectivity):** At least two connectivity options must be implemented (e.g., sidewalk connections, cross-access easements, stub-outs to adjacent parcels).
- **Article 2 (Site Design):** Pedestrian connectivity from public sidewalk to building entrance; internal pedestrian circulation; pedestrian-scaled lighting.
- **Article 3 (Building Design):** Facades visible from public streets must incorporate articulation, material variation, and transparency. Blank wall restrictions apply -- uninterrupted facades over approximately 50 feet require architectural treatment (recesses, projections, material changes, fenestration).
- **Article 5 (Alternative Compliance):** Administrative mechanism allowing alternative designs that meet the intent of specific standards when strict compliance is impractical.

**Implication:** The AutoZone Prototype 74 standard building design must be evaluated against Subchapter E requirements. Auto parts retail prototypes often feature large, windowless rear and side walls that may conflict with blank-wall restrictions on street-facing facades. The Cameron Rd (north) facade is the primary concern. Alternative compliance under Article 5 provides a viable administrative fallback if the standard prototype cannot meet specific articulation or transparency requirements.
**Next step:** (1) Overlay Subchapter E facade requirements onto the Prototype 74 elevations to identify conflicts before site plan submission. (2) Prepare alternative compliance requests concurrently with the site plan for any identified conflicts. (3) Confirm connectivity options -- cross-access to the adjacent Cameron Ferguson condo unit (south) would satisfy one connectivity requirement.

### ZLU-4: Condominium Regime -- Dimensional Calculation Ambiguity

**Severity:** High
**Citation:** Texas Property Code Ch. 82 (Texas Uniform Condominium Act); LDC 25-2-492(D); Doc. No. 2018119069, O.P.R.T.C.T.
**Finding:** The property is Unit 1 of Cameron Ferguson Condominiums, a commercial condominium regime established in 2018 (Doc. No. 2018119069). The condominium regime raises two categories of risk:

**A. City Dimensional Calculation Basis:** The City of Austin may calculate dimensional standards (setbacks, IC, building coverage, FAR) against either (a) Unit 1's boundaries alone or (b) the entire parent condominium tract. If calculated against Unit 1 alone, and Unit 1's boundaries are smaller than the 44,150 SF shown on the concept plan, the IC percentage and building coverage percentage would be higher than 37.75% and 17% respectively. The 95% IC cap provides massive headroom, so this is unlikely to create a compliance problem -- but the City must confirm the calculation methodology during site plan review.

**B. Private Restrictions in the Declaration:** The condominium declaration may impose restrictions beyond CS zoning, including:
- Permitted use limitations within Unit 1
- Architectural review/approval requirements by the condominium association
- Signage restrictions
- Restrictions on modifications to common elements (shared driveways, utilities, stormwater infrastructure)
- Maintenance obligations for shared infrastructure
- Right-of-first-refusal clauses on unit sales

These private restrictions are not reviewed by the City but bind the property owner.

**Implication:** The condominium declaration (Doc. No. 2018119069) is the single most important unreviewed document in this entitlement analysis. Private restrictions could materially constrain the AutoZone development in ways not visible from zoning analysis alone.
**Next step:** (1) Obtain the full condominium declaration and bylaws from Travis County Clerk. (2) Have legal counsel review for use restrictions, architectural control provisions, and termination/amendment thresholds. (3) Confirm with the City how dimensional standards are calculated for condo units during pre-application conference. (4) If acquiring less than all units, determine whether association approval is required for the proposed development.

### ZLU-5: Front Setback at Exact Minimum -- Measurement Basis Risk

**Severity:** Low-Medium
**Citation:** LDC 25-2-492(D); LDC 25-1 (Definitions)
**Finding:** The concept plan shows a 10-foot front building setback from Cameron Rd, which matches the CS minimum exactly. Under Austin LDC, setbacks are measured from the **property line**, not the ROW line. However, with a 25-foot ROW reserve (Doc. No. 201800004) shown on the concept plan and "Potential ROW Dedication" noted, the property line may shift upon dedication.

If the 10-foot setback is measured from the current property line and 25 feet of ROW is subsequently dedicated, the building would be positioned at the new property line with no setback -- which would still comply with the 0-foot side/rear setback allowable in CS, but the front setback would need to be remeasured from the post-dedication property line.

**Implication:** The 10-foot setback must be measured from the post-dedication property line to ensure compliance. If measured from the pre-dedication line, the building may end up less than 10 feet from the new front property line after ROW dedication.
**Next step:** Confirm with Pape-Dawson that the 10-foot front setback is measured from the post-ROW-dedication property line. If not, the building may need to shift south by up to 25 feet, which affects the entire site layout including parking, truck turns, and WQ pond placement.

### ZLU-6: Parking -- No City Minimum; Operator Business Decision

**Severity:** Informational
**Citation:** Ordinance No. 20231102-028 (C20-2023-010), eff. Nov 12, 2023; ADA 2010 Standards; Texas Accessibility Standards (TAS); LDC 25-6, Subchapter E (Bicycle Parking)
**Finding:** Austin eliminated all minimum off-street parking requirements citywide effective November 12, 2023. The 12 proposed parking spaces (10 standard + 1 accessible + 1 van) are the operator's business decision, not a code requirement. The concept plan references "30 spaces required (Appendix A)" -- this is AutoZone's internal prototype standard, not a City mandate.

Remaining parking obligations:
- **ADA/TAS:** For 1-25 total spaces, minimum 1 van-accessible space is required. The plan shows 1 van + 1 standard accessible = compliant.
- **Bicycle parking:** LDC 25-6, Subchapter E requires bicycle parking for commercial development. Typically 1 rack per 10 auto spaces (minimum 2 spaces). Not shown on concept plan.
- **No maximum parking cap:** Austin did not impose maximums when eliminating minimums.

**Implication:** No parking variance or waiver is needed. The 12 spaces are legally sufficient. However, bicycle parking must be provided and is not shown on the concept plan.
**Next step:** Add bicycle parking (minimum 2 spaces / 1 inverted-U rack) to the site plan near the building entrance. Confirm ADA van-accessible space dimensions (8 ft space + 8 ft access aisle per TAS).

### ZLU-7: Compatibility Standards -- Western Boundary

**Severity:** Low
**Citation:** LDC 25-2-1063 et seq. (Compatibility Standards)
**Finding:** Compatibility standards are triggered when commercial development is within 540 feet of SF-zoned property. Adjacent parcels are zoned CS, LI, and GR (commercial/industrial). However, Ferguson Lane further west transitions to SF-3 zoning. The Housing Authority of the City of Austin (Pathways at Georgian Manor, public housing) is located to the west.

At the proposed single-story height (~20 ft), the building complies with even the most restrictive compatibility height limit (30 ft within 0-100 ft of SF-3). Enhanced setback or screening requirements may apply to the western building face if SF-3 parcels are within range.

**Implication:** Height compliance is not at risk. Screening (6-foot masonry wall or equivalent) may be required along the western property line if SF-3 zoning is within 200 feet. This is manageable and consistent with typical commercial site plan requirements.
**Next step:** Measure distance from the western property line to the nearest SF-3 zoned parcel using City GIS. If within 540 feet, document compliance with the graduated compatibility standards in the site plan.

### ZLU-8: Site Plan Trigger -- Subchapter E Site Plan Required

**Severity:** Informational
**Citation:** LDC Ch. 25-5 (Site Plan); LDC Ch. 25-2, Subchapter E
**Finding:** New commercial construction of 7,375 SF on a 1.01-acre site triggers a site development permit (site plan) under LDC Chapter 25-5. The project does not qualify for any exemption (exceeds 1,000 SF threshold, new ground-up construction, increases IC). The governing Subchapter E sections are:

- **LDC 25-2, Subchapter E, Article 1 (Connectivity Standards):** Sec. 25-2-901 et seq.
- **LDC 25-2, Subchapter E, Article 2 (Site Design Standards):** Sec. 25-2-1021 et seq.
- **LDC 25-2, Subchapter E, Article 3 (Building Design Standards):** Sec. 25-2-1051 et seq.
- **LDC 25-2, Subchapter E, Article 5 (Alternative Compliance):** Sec. 25-2-1091 et seq.

Review is administrative (staff-level, Development Services Department Director). No P&Z or Council hearing. No neighborhood notification required for administrative site plan on CS-zoned land without an NP overlay.

**Implication:** The site plan is the sole entitlement approval required. Estimated 30-60 days per review cycle, 2-4 cycles typical. Total timeline: 4-8 months from submission to building permit.
**Next step:** Prepare site plan submission package per AB+C requirements, including Subchapter E compliance documentation, civil engineering plans, tree survey/preservation plan, and environmental review materials.

## Plan-Specific Findings

### PSF-1: Building Footprint and IC -- Massive Zoning Headroom

The proposed 7,375 SF building (17% coverage) and 16,666 SF IC (37.75%) are far below the 95% maximums allowed by CS zoning. The 2:1 FAR allows up to 88,300 SF of building area; the proposal uses 8.3% of this capacity. This headroom means:
- No variance or special exception is needed for any dimensional standard
- Future expansion is possible within the existing zoning envelope without rezoning
- If the condominium regime calculates IC against a smaller unit area, the project still has substantial margin before approaching the 95% cap

### PSF-2: Preliminary WQ Pond -- Positioning Relative to ROW Reserve

The concept plan shows the preliminary WQ pond in the northwest corner, partially adjacent to the 25-foot ROW reserve along Cameron Rd. If ROW dedication is required at site plan, the pond location may conflict with the dedication area. The pond must be located entirely within the post-dedication property boundary. This is a site layout risk that could require pond relocation or resizing.

**Next step:** Confirm pond footprint is entirely outside the 25-foot ROW reserve and any future dedication area. If not, redesign pond location or explore fee-in-lieu water quality option.

### PSF-3: Heritage Trees -- Protected Status Under LDC 25-8

The concept plan identifies 3 heritage trees (24+ inches: #15 Live Oak 28", #16 American Elm 29.5", #17 Ligustrum 33") and 3 protected trees (19-23.5 inches) to remain. Heritage tree removal in Austin requires City Arborist approval and is strongly disfavored. The concept plan shows all heritage trees remaining, with Critical Root Zone (CRZ) protection areas marked.

The site layout must demonstrate that construction activities (grading, utility trenching, paving) do not encroach into heritage tree CRZs. Any CRZ encroachment requires a tree permit with mitigation. The 4 trees proposed for removal (#7, #10, #11, #12) are all under 12 inches and non-protected -- no heritage tree removal is proposed.

**Next step:** Verify that the building footprint, parking areas, utility trenches, and pond construction do not encroach into any heritage or protected tree CRZ. Prepare tree protection plan per LDC 25-8, Subchapter B.

### PSF-4: 10-Foot Front Setback at Minimum -- Tight to ROW Dedication

The concept plan shows the building at exactly 10 feet from the Cameron Rd property line. With a 25-foot ROW dedication pending, the setback measurement basis is critical. If the 10-foot setback is measured from the pre-dedication property line, the building will sit 35 feet from the current ROW edge but only 10 feet from the post-dedication property line -- which is compliant. If measured from the post-dedication line, the building needs to be 35 feet from the current property line. The concept plan must clarify which measurement governs.

### PSF-5: Truck Turn Clearance -- Drive Aisle Width

The truck turn exhibit (Sheet 2) includes an engineer's note that the drive aisle distance needs to be 35-40 feet for a comfortable truck turn. This is not a zoning issue per se, but the site plan must demonstrate adequate fire lane width (minimum 20 feet clear per Austin Fire Code) and functional delivery vehicle access. The tight truck turn may constrain building placement relative to the southern property line.

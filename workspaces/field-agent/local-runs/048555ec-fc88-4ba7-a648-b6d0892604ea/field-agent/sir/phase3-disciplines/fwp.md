## 1. FEMA Flood Zone Determination

**FIRM Panel:** 48453C0230

**Finding:** The building footprint at 9101 Cameron Rd is most likely situated in **FEMA Zone X (unshaded)** — areas determined to be outside the 0.2% annual chance (500-year) floodplain. The Cameron Road corridor in the 78753 area is predominantly mapped Zone X. However, localized drainage corridors crossing the parcel may carry Zone AE or Zone A designations along narrow channel alignments.

**Basis:**
- Site elevations range 626-636 ft (NAVD 88), with the building positioned in the higher central-to-western portion of the site.
- The existing 24" storm line and grate inlets (top-of-grate elevations 629.49 ft and 632.84 ft) indicate channelized drainage that has been studied and partially piped, consistent with FEMA having mapped the primary flood hazard along the channel rather than across the upland building pad.
- No FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA) designation is expected over the proposed building footprint.

**Verification Required:** A formal FEMA Flood Zone Determination Letter and review via FloodPro (maps.austintexas.gov/floodpro/) must confirm the building pad is entirely within Zone X. If any portion of the building pad falls within a Zone AE or Zone A boundary, NFIP compliance (including base flood elevation certification and lowest-floor-above-BFE requirements) would apply.

**Risk Rating:** LOW for building area; MODERATE for peripheral site areas near drainage corridors.

## 2. City of Austin Floodplain (Local Regulatory Floodplain)

**Finding:** The City of Austin maintains locally modeled floodplains through FloodPro that may be **more extensive** than the FEMA regulatory floodplain. Under LDC Chapter 25-7, the more restrictive of the FEMA or locally modeled floodplain governs.

For this site in the Little Walnut Creek watershed (Urban classification), the local 100-year fully-developed-conditions floodplain is the operative standard. The CWQZ boundaries shown on the concept plan effectively delineate this local floodplain — the CWQZ in an Urban Watershed equals the 100-year fully-developed floodplain (LDC 25-8-92).

**Key Observation:** The concept plan positions the proposed building and primary parking areas **west and south of the CWQZ boundaries**, which means they are outside the locally modeled 100-year floodplain. The preliminary detention/WQ pond is intentionally sited **within** the CWQZ area in the northwest portion of the site, which is permissible as a water-related facility.

**Development Restriction:** No habitable structure or new impervious cover is permitted within the locally modeled 100-year floodplain. The building must be entirely outside this boundary. Based on the concept plan layout, this condition appears to be satisfied.

## 3. Critical Water Quality Zone (CWQZ)

**Regulatory Definition (LDC 25-8-92):** For Urban Watersheds, the CWQZ coincides with the **100-year fully-developed-conditions floodplain**, subject to minimum/maximum width constraints based on waterway classification:

| Waterway Class | Min CWQZ Width | Max CWQZ Width |
|---|---|---|
| Minor (< 64 ac contributing area) | 50 ft from centerline | 100 ft from centerline |
| Intermediate (64-320 ac) | 100 ft from centerline | 200 ft from centerline |
| Major (> 320 ac) | 200 ft from centerline | 400 ft from centerline |

**Site-Specific Analysis:**

The CWQZ boundaries cross the site in multiple locations, primarily along the northern and eastern portions. The drainage features on-site (existing grate inlets, 24" storm line) suggest channelized flow with a contributing drainage area that likely meets or exceeds the 64-acre threshold for intermediate waterway classification. The presence of the CWQZ itself confirms a contributing area of at least 64 acres (waterways under 64 acres in Urban Watersheds do not generate a CWQZ unless the floodplain modeling produces one).

**CWQZ Development Restrictions (LDC 25-8-261):**
- Development is **prohibited** within the CWQZ except for enumerated exceptions (fences not obstructing flood flows, parks, golf courses, open space, water-related facilities)
- No new impervious cover is permitted
- Utility crossings require "necessary crossing" determination
- Vegetation clearing and grading are restricted

**Building Placement Assessment:**

The proposed AutoZone building footprint (7,375 SF) is positioned in the central-to-western portion of the site, **outside the mapped CWQZ boundaries** as shown on the concept plan. This is the correct design approach. The CWQZ areas consume a significant portion of the 1.01-acre site along the north and east, materially reducing the buildable envelope.

The following site elements interact with the CWQZ:

| Element | Relationship to CWQZ | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Proposed building (7,375 SF) | Outside CWQZ | Compliant |
| Proposed parking/drive aisles | Primarily outside CWQZ | Compliant (verify edges) |
| Preliminary detention/WQ pond | Within CWQZ | Permissible (water-related facility) |
| Proposed storm line crossing | Crosses CWQZ | Requires "necessary crossing" approval |
| Potential ROW dedication area | Near/overlapping CWQZ | Dedication is permissible |
| Proposed sidewalk (Cameron Rd) | May encroach CWQZ | Requires verification |

**Net Buildable Area Impact:** The CWQZ exclusion zones, combined with CEF setbacks on the east/NE, reduce the effective buildable footprint to approximately 55-65% of the gross lot area. The 7,375 SF building at 17% lot coverage fits within this reduced envelope, but parking layout and maneuvering space are constrained.

## 4. Erosion Hazard Zone (EHZ)

**Trigger:** Development within 100 feet of a creek or the Colorado River requires an Erosion Hazard Zone analysis (DCM Appendix E; LDC 25-7 and 25-8).

**Finding:** Given that CWQZ boundaries and drainage features cross the site, portions of the property within 100 feet of the waterway centerline are subject to EHZ review. The Little Walnut Creek watershed has documented erosion issues — the City of Austin Watershed Protection Department has undertaken erosion remediation projects on Little Walnut Creek tributaries (James Tributary, Richmond Tributary).

**EHZ Analysis Requirements:**
- A geotechnical/hydraulic analysis must determine the lateral extent of potential channel migration and bank erosion
- If the EHZ extends into the proposed building footprint, structural setbacks or bank stabilization measures are required
- The EHZ analysis is submitted as part of the site plan review package

**Assessment:** The proposed building is positioned with apparent separation from the drainage corridor. The concept plan shows the building centrally located with CWQZ/drainage areas to the north and east. The building is likely outside the EHZ, but formal analysis is required to confirm. The detention pond, being within the CWQZ, will need erosion protection design (riprap, outlet stabilization).

**Risk Rating:** LOW-MODERATE. The building appears adequately set back, but EHZ modeling is mandatory and could impose additional constraints on grading and utilities near the drainage corridor.

## 6. Stormwater Detention and Flood Mitigation

**Requirement:** Austin's Drainage Criteria Manual requires on-site detention to limit post-development peak discharge to pre-development levels. For Urban Watershed sites, detention is typically combined with water quality treatment in a single facility.

**Concept Plan Approach:** The preliminary detention/WQ pond is shown in the northwest corner of the site, within the CWQZ area. This is an appropriate location because:
1. It is at the low point of the site (near Cameron Rd, elevation ~626-628 ft)
2. Detention/WQ facilities are permissible within the CWQZ as water-related facilities
3. It intercepts runoff before discharge to the public storm system

**Sizing Concern:** The pond dimensions are not labeled on the concept plan. Given the constrained site area and the CWQZ boundaries, the pond must be sized to provide both:
- Water quality volume (WQV) per ECM 1.6.0 for sedimentation/filtration
- Flood detention volume for the 2-yr, 10-yr, and 25-yr storm events per DCM

The 48-hour drawdown requirement for the WQ volume and the peak-flow attenuation for detention must be reconciled in the pond design. An undersized facility would trigger a request for revision during site plan review.

## 8. Required Submittals and Approvals

1. **FEMA Flood Zone Determination Letter** — confirm Zone X for building footprint
2. **FloodPro Review** — confirm locally modeled floodplain limits relative to building and improvements
3. **CWQZ Delineation** — formal 100-year fully-developed floodplain modeling by licensed engineer, submitted to Watershed Protection Department (WPD) for approval
4. **Environmental Resource Inventory (ERI)** — required for site plan; includes CEF identification and buffer determination
5. **Erosion Hazard Zone Analysis** — geotechnical/hydraulic study per DCM Appendix E
6. **Drainage Analysis and Detention Design** — full hydrology/hydraulics report per DCM, including pre- and post-development peak flow comparison and detention sizing
7. **Water Quality Pond Design** — sedimentation/filtration design per ECM 1.6.0-1.6.5, with 48-hour drawdown demonstration
8. **Floodplain Modification Application** — only if any grading or fill is proposed within the 100-year floodplain (not anticipated for building, but may apply to pond and utilities)

## 9. Feasibility Conclusion

**Overall Floodplain/Waterway Feasibility: FEASIBLE WITH CONDITIONS**

The proposed AutoZone building at 9101 Cameron Rd is positioned outside the CWQZ (which equals the 100-year fully-developed floodplain in this Urban Watershed), outside CEF setback areas, and most likely within FEMA Zone X. The building placement is feasible from a floodplain and waterway protection standpoint.

The primary constraints are spatial rather than prohibitive: the CWQZ and CEF setback areas consume a substantial portion of the 1.01-acre site, compressing the buildable envelope and constraining parking, access, and utility routing. The detention/WQ pond must fit within the CWQZ area at the site's low point, and its sizing must be validated through engineering analysis.

No floodplain variance or FEMA map amendment (LOMA/LOMR) is anticipated. The critical path item is the formal CWQZ delineation through fully-developed-conditions hydrology modeling, which will establish the definitive buildable boundary. Early engagement with the City of Austin Watershed Protection Department to obtain preliminary CWQZ confirmation is recommended before finalizing the site plan.

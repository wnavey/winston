# Tree Protection & Erosion Control Findings — 12713 Cinchring Ln

**Property:** 12713 Cinchring Ln, Austin, TX 78727 — Lot 12, Block M, Scofield Subdivision
**Proposed use:** Demolish existing SFR; build ground-up duplex (2 units)
**Lot:** ~7,840 sq ft (0.18 acres), SF-2 zoning, Walnut Creek watershed (Suburban)
**Date:** 2026-06-03

---

## Summary

- **No tree survey has been conducted.** This is the single largest data gap in this discipline. The house was built in 1993, giving the lot over 30 years of tree growth. Without a field survey by an RPLS with ISA-certified arborist support, the presence, species, and diameter of regulated, protected, or heritage trees cannot be determined, and no reliable building envelope can be drawn.
- **Heritage tree risk is plausible but unquantified.** Scofield-era lots in north Austin commonly have live oaks, cedar elms, and pecans — all heritage-eligible species under LDC 25-8-602(1). Any specimen at or above 24" DBH would trigger administrative variance, 300% caliper-inch mitigation, and prerequisite-variance sequencing that adds 2-4+ months to the entitlement timeline.
- **Standard erosion and sedimentation controls apply but pose low risk.** The lot is small (0.18 ac), in an urban/suburban watershed with no BSZ overlay, no floodplain, no EARZ, no known CEFs, and flat topography. ESC requirements are routine and should not constrain the project.
- **No environmental overlays (BSZ, EARZ, CWQZ, Hill Country Roadway Corridor) apply** to this site, eliminating the most severe tree and erosion regulatory pathways (phased ESC, SOS impervious caps, karst surveys, ERI).
- **The demolition itself triggers ESC plan and tree protection obligations.** Tree protection fencing must be installed before any demolition activity begins; demolition equipment, staging, and debris storage within a CRZ of any regulated tree constitutes a code violation.

## Findings

### 1. Tree Survey Not Conducted — Cannot Define Buildable Envelope

**Severity:** data-gap

**Citation:** ECM 3.3.1.A; ECM 3.3.2.A (RPLS-certified tree survey required within 5 years of submittal for all trees >= 8" DBH on site, on adjacent property with CRZ entering the LOC, or in the adjacent ROW)

**Finding:** No tree survey has been performed for this property. The lot has been developed since 1993, providing 30+ years for trees to reach protected (>= 19" DBH) or heritage (>= 24" DBH, listed species) status. Satellite and street-level imagery cannot substitute for field measurement — DBH at 4.5 ft above natural grade is the only valid metric for regulatory classification.

**Implication for the developer:** Without a tree survey, it is impossible to determine whether any heritage or protected trees exist, whether CRZs constrain the building footprint, or what mitigation costs may apply. All massing and site plan work performed before the survey is speculative and may require wholesale revision.

**Recommended next step:** Commission an RPLS-certified tree survey with ISA-certified arborist species identification and condition rating before investing in architectural design. The survey must include trees on adjacent lots and in the ROW whose CRZ (radius in feet = DBH in inches) reaches into the LOC. Budget approximately $1,500-$3,000 for the survey on a lot this size.

### 2. Potential Heritage Trees — Variance and Mitigation Exposure

**Severity:** moderate

**Citation:** LDC 25-8-602(1) (heritage species list); LDC 25-8-642 (admin variance for heritage < 30" stem); LDC 25-8-643 (LUC + EC variance for >= 30" stem); ECM 3.5.4.A.1 (300% mitigation rate)

**Finding:** Heritage species common to north Austin Blackland Prairie / Scofield-era landscaping include live oak (*Quercus virginiana*), cedar elm (*Ulmus crassifolia*), and pecan (*Carya illinoinensis*) — all listed under LDC 25-8-602(1). A tree planted at subdivision development (circa 1990-1993) could now exceed 24" DBH, qualifying as a heritage tree. Removal of a heritage tree requires an administrative variance (or LUC + EC variance if any stem >= 30"), prerequisite-variance exhaustion under LDC 25-8-646, and mitigation at 300% of removed caliper inches.

**Implication for the developer:** If a 24" heritage oak must be removed, mitigation alone would cost approximately $14,400 (24" x 300% = 72 caliper inches x $200/inch fee-in-lieu) — and fee-in-lieu is only available if the City Arborist determines the site is unsuitable for replanting. On-site replanting of 72 caliper inches of significant shade providers is the default and requires substantial planting area. The prerequisite-variance requirement (LDC 25-8-646) adds 2-4 months of calendar time before the heritage variance can even be filed.

**Recommended next step:** Complete the tree survey (Finding 1). If heritage trees are present, engage the City Arborist early to discuss preservation alternatives, potential MCRZ approval, or DDI status if condition is poor. Design massing around heritage CRZs rather than planning for removal.

---

### 3. Protected Tree Removal Likely — Mitigation Cost to Budget

**Severity:** moderate

**Citation:** LDC 25-8-624(A) (removal criteria); ECM 3.5.4.A.1 (100% mitigation for 19"+ Appendix F species; 50% for non-Appendix F 19"+; 50%/25% for 8-18.9")

**Finding:** On a 7,840 sq ft lot where the entire existing house, driveway, and yard will be demolished and replaced with a larger duplex footprint, removal of one or more protected trees (>= 19" DBH) is probable. Even trees in the 8-18.9" range require a removal permit and mitigation (50% if Appendix F species, 25% otherwise).

**Implication for the developer:** Mitigation costs for protected (non-heritage) trees are more manageable — a 20" protected Appendix F tree would require 20 caliper inches of replacement (100% rate), or $4,000 fee-in-lieu. Multiple removals could accumulate to $5,000-$15,000 depending on inventory. At least 75% of replacement caliper must be Significant Shade Provider species.

**Recommended next step:** After the tree survey, map all regulated trees against the proposed building footprint, driveways, utility trenches, and staging areas. Optimize the site plan to preserve the largest trees and minimize mitigation obligations.

### 4. Critical Root Zone Constraints on Duplex Footprint

**Severity:** moderate

**Citation:** ECM 3.5.2.A.1 (CRZ radius = DBH in inches, measured in feet; 50% preservation rule; Half CRZ <= 4" cut/fill; Quarter CRZ zero disturbance); ECM 3.5.2.A.3 (impacts measured at line of excavation, not face of improvements)

**Finding:** On a small lot (7,840 sq ft), the CRZ of even a single large tree can consume a significant portion of the buildable area. A 24" tree has a 24-ft CRZ radius (1,810 sq ft circle), a 12-ft Half CRZ, and a 6-ft Quarter CRZ with zero tolerance for any cut or fill. The CRZ of trees on adjacent lots or in the Cinchring Ln ROW that extends into this lot is subject to the same rules. Austin measures construction impacts at the line of excavation — over-excavation, form bracing, scaffolding, and utility trenching all count.

**Implication for the developer:** A heritage or large protected tree near the center or front of the lot could eliminate duplex configurations that require full-width building footprints. The Quarter CRZ prohibition is absolute and frequently constrains sidewalks, curbs, and utility laterals. The City Arborist may impose an enlarged CRZ (1.25x-1.5x) for Post Oak, further compressing the envelope.

**Recommended next step:** Once the tree survey is complete, plot CRZ, Half CRZ, and Quarter CRZ circles on the site plan at the concept stage, before committing to massing. Evaluate elevated foundation options within the Half CRZ (ECM 3.5.2.B.3) and like-for-like existing impacts (ECM 3.5.2.B.4.a) where the existing house slab already encroaches.

### 5. Erosion and Sedimentation Control — Routine Requirements

**Severity:** note

**Citation:** LDC 25-8-181; ECM 1.4 (ESC plan required for all development); ECM 1.4.4.B.3 (LOC-based quantities)

**Finding:** An ESC plan is required for demolition and construction. The site is small (0.18 ac), flat, in the Walnut Creek suburban watershed, outside the BSZ, and below all phasing thresholds (LOC < 25 ac). Standard ESC measures apply: silt fence at downslope LOC perimeter, stabilized construction entrance (>= 50 ft crushed stone pad, >= 8" thick), and inlet protection for any nearby storm drains. No phased ESC plan, no 18-month revegetation cap, and no cut/fill > 4 ft disclosure are required (urban/suburban watershed exemption).

**Implication for the developer:** ESC compliance is straightforward and should not affect project schedule or cost materially. The civil engineer will include the ESC plan on the site plan sheets at 1"=50' scale.

**Recommended next step:** Include ESC in the civil engineering scope. Ensure the stabilized construction entrance is feasible given the lot frontage and any existing street trees in the ROW.

### 6. Demolition-Phase Tree Protection Obligations

**Severity:** note

**Citation:** ECM 3.6.1 (tree protection fencing); SSM 610.4.A (chain-link >= 5 ft at CRZ boundary; trunk wrap within 5 ft); ECM 3.5.2.A.3 (construction impacts include staging and equipment)

**Finding:** Tree protection fencing must be installed before demolition begins — not just before new construction. Demolition equipment, debris staging, material storage, and truck access within the CRZ of any regulated tree (>= 8" DBH) constitutes a code violation and can trigger reclassification of the tree as "removed," with full mitigation consequences. The existing concrete driveway and house slab provide some protection during demolition if trees are behind the structure, but heavy equipment tracking across root zones outside the existing impervious footprint is prohibited without 8" mulch and plywood/mat protection.

**Implication for the developer:** The demolition contractor must be briefed on tree protection requirements. Demolition and tree protection plans should be coordinated — tree fencing is a precondition to the demolition permit, not a later site-plan item.

**Recommended next step:** Once the tree survey identifies regulated trees, prepare a tree protection plan that covers both the demolition and construction phases. Specify fencing locations and trunk wrap requirements in the demolition scope of work.

### 7. No Environmental Overlays — Favorable Regulatory Position

**Severity:** opportunity

**Citation:** Environmental research confirms: not in BSZ (LDC 25-8-481); not in EARZ (30 TAC Ch. 213); not in CWQZ; no known CEFs (LDC 25-8-281); no Hill Country Roadway Corridor; Zone X (no floodplain); no wetlands expected

**Finding:** The property is free of the environmental overlays that trigger the most burdensome tree and erosion requirements in Austin. There is no BSZ impervious cover cap, no phased ESC, no ERI requirement (no slopes > 15%, no EARZ, no wetlands within 150 ft), no karst survey, no CWQZ buffer, and no Hill Country Roadway Corridor native-tree replacement or 40% natural-area set-aside.

**Implication for the developer:** The regulatory pathway for tree protection and erosion control is limited to the standard citywide tree ordinance (LDC Ch. 25-8 Subchapter B) and standard ESC (LDC 25-8-181). This is the simplest regulatory posture available in Austin and should not introduce material schedule risk beyond the tree survey and any resulting variance proceedings.

**Recommended next step:** No action needed on environmental overlays. Focus diligence resources on the tree survey and heritage/protected tree analysis.

---

### 8. Like-for-Like Existing Impacts — Potential Opportunity

**Severity:** opportunity

**Citation:** ECM 3.5.2.B.4.a (rebuilding within footprint of existing Half- or Quarter-CRZ impacts is allowable if no deeper or wider)

**Finding:** The existing house, driveway, and concrete sewer vault have occupied portions of this lot since 1993. If any regulated trees have CRZs that overlap these existing impervious surfaces, the developer may claim like-for-like existing impacts — meaning new construction within the same footprint and depth as the existing improvements does not count as additional CRZ encroachment. This is particularly valuable on a small infill lot where the existing slab may already be within a Half or Quarter CRZ.

**Implication for the developer:** This opportunity is only quantifiable after the tree survey. If a heritage tree's CRZ overlaps the existing house slab, rebuilding within that same footprint avoids triggering the CRZ preservation rules for that area, potentially saving the project from a variance requirement.

**Recommended next step:** Document the existing house footprint, driveway, and sewer vault locations precisely (from the existing survey or demolition plan) before demolition. Overlay these against CRZs from the tree survey to identify like-for-like credit areas.

---

### 9. Landscape Replacement Requirements for New Duplex

**Severity:** note

**Citation:** LDC 25-2-1001 et seq.; ECM Section 2 (landscape and parking lot tree requirements)

**Finding:** The new duplex must comply with Austin's landscape requirements, including minimum tree plantings and landscaped area percentages. For residential development on a single lot, the requirements are less onerous than commercial or multifamily site plans, but tree replacement plantings (from any removal mitigation) count toward landscape compliance. At least 75% of replacement caliper must be Significant Shade Provider species per ECM Appendix F.

**Implication for the developer:** Landscape compliance is routine but should be coordinated with tree mitigation replanting to avoid double-counting issues. On a 7,840 sq ft lot with a duplex footprint, available planting area may be limited.

**Recommended next step:** Include landscape plan in the civil/site plan scope. Coordinate with the arborist on species selection for any mitigation replanting to satisfy both mitigation and landscape requirements simultaneously.

## Open Questions for the Engineer

1. **Tree survey:** Has any informal tree inventory or site visit been conducted? Are there any known large trees on the lot, on adjacent lots near the property line, or in the Cinchring Ln ROW? Even informal observations (species, approximate size, location) would help scope the RPLS survey and flag likely heritage candidates.

2. **Existing site survey:** Does the existing boundary survey show tree locations or canopy outlines? The seed data references a survey (FIRM panel, easements, building line) but does not mention tree symbology.

3. **Duplex massing flexibility:** How flexible is the building footprint? If heritage trees are found, can the duplex be reconfigured (e.g., stacked units, narrower footprint, shifted placement) to avoid CRZ conflicts, or is the program fixed?

4. **Existing house footprint dimensions:** What are the precise dimensions and location of the existing house slab, driveway, and sewer vault? These are needed to evaluate like-for-like existing impact credit (ECM 3.5.2.B.4.a) against any CRZ overlaps.

5. **Demolition sequencing:** Is there a preferred demolition contractor or timeline? Tree protection fencing must be in place before any demolition activity; this needs to be communicated to the demo team.

6. **Adjacent lot trees:** Are there large trees on the neighboring properties (especially to the sides and rear) whose canopies overhang or approach the property line? A tree with CRZ extending into this lot from an adjacent parcel is subject to the same protection rules as an on-site tree.

7. **Budget tolerance for mitigation:** What is the developer's budget tolerance for tree mitigation fees? Heritage tree mitigation at 300% can run $10,000-$25,000+ per tree; understanding cost sensitivity will help prioritize preservation vs. removal strategies once the survey is complete.

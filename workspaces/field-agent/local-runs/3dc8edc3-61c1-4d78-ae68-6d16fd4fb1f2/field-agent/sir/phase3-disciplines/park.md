# Parkland Findings — 12713 Cinchring Ln

**Property:** 12713 Cinchring Ln, Austin, TX 78727 — Lot 12, Block M, Scofield Subdivision
**Proposed use:** Demolish existing SFR; build ground-up duplex (2 units)
**Lot:** ~7,840 sq ft (0.18 acres), SF-2 zoning
**Jurisdiction:** City of Austin, full-purpose city limits
**Research date:** 2026-06-03

---

## Summary

- **Parkland dedication does not apply to this project.** Austin's parkland dedication ordinance (LDC Chapter 25-1, Article 14) triggers at subdivision (for single-family/duplex uses) or at site plan (for multifamily). This project involves building a duplex on an already-platted lot with no subdivision proposed, so parkland dedication is not triggered.
- **No fee-in-lieu is owed.** Neither the single-family fee-in-lieu (LDC 25-1-606) nor the parkland development fee (LDC 25-1-607) applies because no subdivision or plat action is required for this duplex on an existing lot.
- **HOME Phase 1 does not independently trigger parkland obligations.** HOME Phase 1 permits duplexes by right on SF-2 lots through the building permit process; it does not create a new parkland dedication trigger point.
- **No Chapter 26 (Texas Parks & Wildlife Code) concern identified.** No City parkland is immediately adjacent to the lot based on available data. The Scofield Farms neighborhood has existing parks and amenities, but the project does not propose construction on or across any public parkland.
- **This discipline is low-risk for the project.** No parkland-related approvals, fees, or instruments are required for the proposed scope.

## Findings

### Finding 1: Parkland Dedication Not Triggered — No Subdivision Proposed

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | note |
| **Citation** | LDC 25-1-601 through 25-1-612 (effective Jan 1, 2024, per Ord. 20231130-087); feasibility guide: "Parkland dedication in Austin attaches at site plan (for multifamily and hotel/motel) and at subdivision (for single-family residential)." |
| **Finding** | Parkland dedication for single-family and duplex residential uses is triggered at subdivision (plat approval), not at building permit. This project proposes a duplex on Lot 12, Block M, Scofield Subdivision — an already-platted lot recorded in Cabinet 91, Slides 264-265, Travis County Plat Records. No new subdivision, replat, or plat amendment is proposed as part of the duplex construction. The project proceeds via residential building permit only. |
| **Implication for the developer** | No parkland dedication (land or fee-in-lieu) is required. No Parkland Dedication Determination letter from PARD is needed. No parkland-related notes are required on the building permit application. |
| **Recommended next step** | No action required. If the project scope changes to include a subdivision or replat (e.g., to take advantage of HOME Phase 2 small-lot provisions to split the lot into two parcels), parkland dedication would be triggered at that point and this finding must be revisited. |

### Finding 2: HOME Phase 1 Does Not Create a Parkland Trigger

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | note |
| **Citation** | Ordinance No. 20231207-001 (HOME Phase 1); LDC 25-1-601(C) (applicability); programs.md (HOME Phase 1 assessment) |
| **Finding** | HOME Phase 1 permits duplexes by right on SF-2 lots and routes them through the residential building permit process — not through site plan or subdivision review. The parkland dedication ordinance's trigger points (site plan for multifamily, subdivision for single-family) are unchanged by HOME. A duplex built under HOME Phase 1 on an existing platted lot does not pass through either trigger point. |
| **Implication for the developer** | The developer can proceed with the duplex under HOME Phase 1 without any parkland obligation. No SMART Housing certification or affordability commitment is needed to avoid parkland fees, because no parkland fees apply in the first place. |
| **Recommended next step** | None. This is a confirmatory finding. |

### Finding 3: No Chapter 26 Concern Identified

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | note |
| **Citation** | Texas Parks & Wildlife Code, Chapter 26; ECM 5.4.1; feasibility guide: "Any construction... on land labeled 'City of Austin (Parkland)' or 'Dedicated Parkland' triggers Texas Parks and Wildlife Code Chapter 26." |
| **Finding** | Chapter 26 applies when any construction activity — including drainage outfalls, utility crossings, grading, or staging — occurs on existing City parkland. Based on available data, Lot 12, Block M does not abut dedicated City parkland. The Scofield Farms neighborhood contains community parks and greenbelts, but no parkland parcel has been identified as immediately adjacent to or abutting this lot. No project work is expected to touch public parkland. |
| **Implication for the developer** | No Chapter 26 process (Parks Board hearing, City Council easement, mitigation plan) is anticipated. This avoids a 6-9 month approval timeline that would otherwise be a long-pole schedule item. |
| **Recommended next step** | Confirm via Austin GIS parkland layer that no dedicated parkland parcels abut the lot. If any utility connections or drainage discharge routes cross parkland, this finding must be upgraded to significant severity and the Chapter 26 process must be initiated. |

### Finding 4: Parkland Adjacency — Verify via GIS

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | data-gap |
| **Citation** | ECM 5.4.1; Austin PARD parkland inventory |
| **Finding** | The Scofield Farms subdivision includes neighborhood parks and greenbelt areas. The exact proximity of dedicated City parkland to Lot 12 has not been confirmed through GIS or survey data. While Chapter 26 is only triggered by construction on parkland (not merely adjacency), the adjacency question also affects whether PARD would require a pedestrian/bicycle connection if a future subdivision were pursued. |
| **Implication for the developer** | Low risk for current scope. If parkland is directly adjacent and the project scope later expands to include subdivision, PARD may require connectivity improvements. |
| **Recommended next step** | Check Austin GIS parkland layer to confirm no dedicated parkland abuts the lot. This is a low-priority verification item given the current project scope. |

### Finding 5: Future Subdivision Would Trigger Parkland Dedication

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | note |
| **Citation** | LDC 25-1-601 through 25-1-612; HOME Phase 2 (Ord. 20240516); programs.md (HOME Phase 2 enables subdivision to lots as small as 2,000 sq ft) |
| **Finding** | HOME Phase 2 reduces the minimum lot size to 2,000 sq ft and enables subdivision of existing lots into smaller parcels. If the developer later decides to subdivide this ~7,840 sq ft lot (e.g., into two lots for separate fee-simple ownership of each duplex unit), that subdivision action would trigger parkland dedication. At that point, the single-family parkland calculation would apply: density-based multipliers (LDC 25-1-602(C)), fee-in-lieu (LDC 25-1-606), and parkland development fee (LDC 25-1-607) would all come into play. A Parkland Dedication Determination from PARD would be required. |
| **Implication for the developer** | Not applicable to current scope. If subdivision is pursued in the future, budget for parkland fees and the PARD Determination process. For a 2-lot subdivision in a suburban area, the fees are expected to be modest but nonzero. |
| **Recommended next step** | No action required unless the project scope changes to include a subdivision. If it does, request a Parkland Dedication Determination from PARD before filing the plat application. |

### Finding 6: SMART Housing Parkland Exemption — Not Needed

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | note |
| **Citation** | LDC 25-1-601(D)(3) (SMART Housing exemption); LDC 25-1-601(D)(4) (income-restricted unit exemption); programs.md (SMART Housing assessment) |
| **Finding** | SMART Housing certification and income-restricted unit designations exempt units from parkland dedication fees. For larger projects, this is typically the largest available fee reduction lever. However, since this 2-unit duplex project does not trigger parkland dedication at all (no subdivision proposed), the SMART Housing parkland exemption provides no incremental benefit in this context. |
| **Implication for the developer** | No need to pursue SMART Housing solely for parkland fee avoidance. SMART Housing may still be worth evaluating for its other benefits (expedited review, fee waivers) per the programs analysis, but parkland is not a factor in that decision. |
| **Recommended next step** | None for parkland purposes. |

## Open Questions for the Engineer

1. **Parkland adjacency confirmation.** Can the engineer or surveyor confirm via Austin GIS (PARD parkland layer) whether any dedicated City parkland parcels abut Lot 12, Block M? This would close the data gap in Finding 4 and fully rule out any Chapter 26 risk for utility connections or drainage discharge.

2. **Future subdivision intent.** Is there any possibility the developer will pursue a lot split or subdivision (e.g., under HOME Phase 2 small-lot provisions) to sell the duplex units on separate lots? If so, parkland dedication analysis should be revisited at that time, and a PARD Parkland Dedication Determination should be requested before plat application.

3. **Drainage discharge routing.** Does the proposed drainage plan route any discharge through or across public parkland or greenbelt areas? If so, Chapter 26 would be triggered regardless of the parkland dedication analysis above.

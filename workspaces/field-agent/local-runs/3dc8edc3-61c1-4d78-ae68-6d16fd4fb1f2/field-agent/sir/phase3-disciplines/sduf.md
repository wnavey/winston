# Site Plan & Form Findings — 12713 Cinchring Ln

**Property:** 12713 Cinchring Ln, Austin, TX 78727 — Lot 12, Block M, Scofield Subdivision
**Zoning:** SF-2 (Single-Family Residential — Standard Lot)
**Proposed use:** Demolish existing SFR (1993); build ground-up duplex (2 units)
**Lot area:** ~7,840 sq ft (0.18 acres)
**Date:** 2026-06-03

---

## Summary

- **The duplex is exempt from site plan under LDC Chapter 25-5.** Residential uses of four or fewer units do not trigger site plan review. The project proceeds via residential building permit only — no administrative site plan, no Site Plan Lite, no commission review.
- **No rezoning, conditional use permit, or Board of Adjustment action is required.** HOME Phase 1 permits a duplex by right on SF-2 lots. The procedural path is a straightforward residential building permit through Austin DSD.
- **Subchapter E (Design Standards and Mixed Use) does not apply.** Subchapter E is triggered only when a project requires a site plan under Chapter 25-5. Because the duplex is site-plan-exempt, no build-to, glazing, entrance orientation, articulation, or sidewalk-zone standards under Subchapter E attach.
- **McMansion / Subchapter F standards are superseded by HOME Phase 1 for two-unit development.** HOME Phase 1 incorporated the favorable Subchapter F setback reductions (15 ft front, 5 ft rear) and eliminated sidewall articulation and tent requirements for duplex/two-unit uses on SF lots.
- **Demolition of the 1993 house requires a separate demolition permit and an asbestos survey.** Pre-2000 construction triggers TCEQ Asbestos NESHAP survey requirements before any demolition work begins.

## Findings

### 1. Site Plan Exemption

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | opportunity |
| **Citation** | LDC Chapter 25-5 (site plan exemptions); LDC § 25-5-1 et seq. |
| **Finding** | A duplex (2 units) on an existing platted lot is exempt from site plan requirements under Austin LDC. The Chapter 25-5 exemption applies to residential uses of four or fewer units. No administrative site plan, Site Plan Lite, or commission site plan is required. |
| **Implication** | This eliminates 6-9 months of site plan review timeline and associated engineering costs. The project proceeds directly to residential building permit, with an expected review timeline of 4-8 weeks. No Subchapter E compliance is triggered. No pre-application conference is needed. |
| **Recommended next step** | Confirm exemption at DSD intake. File residential building permit application directly. |

### 3. Subchapter E Does Not Apply

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | note |
| **Citation** | LDC Subchapter E, § 1.2 (Applicability); LDC Chapter 25-5 |
| **Finding** | Subchapter E applies only when a project requires a site plan under Chapter 25-5 (§ 1.2.1). Because this duplex is site-plan-exempt, Subchapter E design standards — including build-to percentages, ground-floor glazing minimums, entrance orientation requirements, sidewalk zone dimensions, parking placement rules, and articulation standards — do not apply to this project. |
| **Implication** | The developer has full design flexibility on facade treatment, entrance placement, glazing, and building orientation. No build-to percentage, no principal-street glazing minimum, no supplemental zone requirement. This substantially simplifies design and reduces facade cost. |
| **Recommended next step** | No action required. Do not load Subchapter E standards onto permit drawings. |

### 4. McMansion / Subchapter F — Superseded by HOME Phase 1

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | note |
| **Citation** | HOME Phase 1 (Ord. 20231207-001); LDC Subchapter F (Residential Design and Compatibility Standards) |

The simplified design standards under HOME govern instead of the full Subchapter F framework. |

However, FAR (0.40:1) and building coverage (40%) still apply and are the primary massing constraints on this lot. |
| **Recommended next step** | Design to HOME Phase 1 dimensional standards. Note the 25 ft platted building line (see Finding 6) may override the 15 ft HOME front setback. |

### 6. Platted 25-Foot Building Line — May Override HOME Front Setback

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | moderate |
| **Citation** | Plat, Vol. 11863, Pg. 1147, Travis County; HOME Phase 1 (15 ft front setback for duplex) |
| **Finding** | The recorded plat shows a 25-foot building line on the front of Lot 12. HOME Phase 1 reduces the zoning front setback to 15 ft for duplex uses. However, the platted building line is a recorded encumbrance. Under Austin practice, the more restrictive requirement controls — meaning the 25 ft platted building line likely governs over the 15 ft zoning setback at the permitting counter. |
| **Implication** | If the 25 ft building line is enforced through DSD plan review, the buildable depth is reduced by 10 ft compared to the HOME setback. On a lot of this size, that is a meaningful reduction in buildable area. To use the 15 ft setback, the owner would need to replat or amend the plat — which triggers subdivision review and adds timeline and cost. |
| **Recommended next step** | Confirm with DSD whether the platted building line is enforced at building permit review or treated as a private covenant only. If enforced, design to the 25 ft front setback. If a replat is desired, budget 3-6 months and engage a surveyor. |

### 7. Demolition Permit and Asbestos Survey

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | moderate |
| **Citation** | TCEQ 30 TAC Chapter 113, Subchapter B (Asbestos NESHAP); Austin DSD demolition permit process |
| **Finding** | The existing house was built in 1993 — pre-2000 construction that may contain asbestos-containing materials (ACM) in roofing, siding, joint compound, floor tile, pipe insulation, or vermiculite attic insulation. TCEQ Asbestos NESHAP regulations require a thorough asbestos inspection by a licensed Texas asbestos inspector before any demolition. If ACM is found, abatement must be performed by a licensed contractor with proper notification to TCEQ before demolition proceeds. A separate demolition permit from Austin DSD is required; typical turnaround is 2-4 weeks. |
| **Implication** | The asbestos survey must be completed before demolition permit issuance. If ACM is found, abatement adds cost ($3,000-$15,000 depending on extent) and 2-4 weeks to the pre-construction timeline. Failure to survey before demolition is a TCEQ violation with significant penalties. Lead-based paint (LBP) is also a concern for pre-1978 construction but is less likely in a 1993 house (post-ban). |
| **Recommended next step** | Engage a licensed Texas asbestos inspector to perform a pre-demolition survey. Budget for potential ACM abatement. File demolition permit with DSD after survey is complete. |

### 8. Building Separation — IRC Fire Separation, No Zoning Minimum

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | note |
| **Citation** | HOME Phase 1 dimensional standards; IRC R302.1 (exterior walls — fire separation distance) |
| **Finding** | HOME Phase 1 eliminated any zoning-based minimum building separation requirement for duplex units. The only separation requirement is the IRC fire-separation-distance standard: if the exterior wall is less than 3 feet from the lot line or from another building on the same lot, fire-resistance-rated construction is required per IRC R302.1 (1-hour fire-resistive construction, no openings). For a duplex with attached units sharing a common wall, the shared wall must be a 1-hour fire-rated assembly per IRC R302. For detached duplex units, fire separation distance governs openings and fire-resistance rating of exterior walls. |
| **Implication** | The developer has flexibility to build attached (side-by-side or stacked) or detached units. Attached construction with a 1-hour rated party wall is the most space-efficient configuration on a 7,840 sq ft lot. If detached, wall openings and projections near the property line or the other unit are restricted by IRC fire separation distance requirements. |
| **Recommended next step** | Coordinate with the architect and building code consultant on the fire separation assembly for the chosen configuration (attached vs. detached). |

## Open Questions for the Engineer

1. **Platted building line enforcement:** Does Austin DSD enforce the 25 ft platted building line at residential building permit review, or is it treated as a private covenant enforceable only by neighbors/HOA? This determines whether the effective front setback is 15 ft (HOME) or 25 ft (plat).

2. **Lot width:** Does the lot meet the 50 ft minimum lot width required for duplex use under HOME Phase 1? The plat (Cabinet 91, Slides 264-265) should be reviewed to confirm lot dimensions.

3. **Asbestos survey results:** Has an asbestos inspection been performed on the 1993 house? If ACM is identified, what is the estimated abatement scope and cost?

4. **Unit configuration:** Are the two duplex units planned as attached (common party wall) or detached? This drives the IRC fire separation assembly requirements and affects building coverage efficiency.

5. **FAR feasibility:** At 0.40:1 FAR on ~7,840 sq ft, the maximum gross floor area is ~3,136 sq ft for both units combined. Does the intended unit program (number of bedrooms, unit sizes) fit within this limit? If a two-story design is contemplated, the 40% building coverage still caps the footprint at ~3,136 sq ft, but FAR governs total floor area across both stories.

6. **5-ft PUE location:** Where does the 5-foot public utility easement run on this lot (front, side, or both)? Structures and permanent improvements are prohibited within the PUE, which further constrains the buildable area.

7. **Existing sanitary sewer vault:** What is the disposition plan for the concrete sanitary sewer vault identified on site? Will it be removed, capped, or re-used? Coordinate with Austin Water.

8. **Tree survey:** Are there any protected trees (8 in. diameter or greater) or heritage trees (19 in. or greater) on the lot that would require preservation or mitigation?

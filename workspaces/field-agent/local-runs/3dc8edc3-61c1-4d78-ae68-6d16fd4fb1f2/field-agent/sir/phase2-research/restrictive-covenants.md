## 3. Likely Restrictions Based on Subdivision Character

Scofield Farms is a 1980s-1990s suburban single-family subdivision in north Austin. Based on the era of development and subdivision type, the CC&Rs are expected to contain the following provisions (typical for Texas residential subdivisions of this period):

### 3.1 Use Restrictions

**Expected:** Lots restricted to **single-family residential use only**. This is the standard restriction in Texas subdivision CC&Rs of this era and would prohibit duplex, two-family, or multi-family construction.

**Legal significance:** Under Texas law (Texas Property Code Chapter 202), deed restrictions are private covenants enforceable by the HOA and/or adjacent property owners through civil litigation. They operate independently of municipal zoning. The City of Austin's HOME Initiative explicitly does **not** override private deed restrictions.

### 3.2 Architectural Control

**Confirmed:** The Scofield Farms ROA maintains a 5-member **Architectural Control Committee (ACC)** that reviews and approves exterior modifications. ACC approval is required before any exterior construction, remodeling, or alteration.

**Implication for duplex:** Even if the CC&Rs do not explicitly prohibit duplex use, the ACC review process would apply to any new construction, including design, materials, height, setbacks, and site layout.

### 3.3 Building Setbacks

**Expected:** The CC&Rs may establish building setbacks that differ from (and are potentially more restrictive than) both the base zoning setbacks and the HOME Phase 1 modified setbacks. The 25-foot building line shown on the plat is likely incorporated by reference into the CC&Rs.

### 3.4 Height and Massing

**Expected:** CC&Rs may impose height limits (typically 1 or 2 stories) and/or maximum building footprint restrictions that could be more restrictive than the SF-2 zoning standards.

### 3.5 Outbuildings and Accessory Structures

**Expected:** Restrictions on detached structures, carports, and accessory buildings that could affect duplex site planning.

## 4. Legal Framework: Deed Restrictions vs. Zoning

### Texas Law

| Principle | Detail |
|---|---|
| Private enforcement | CC&Rs are private contracts enforceable by the HOA or individual lot owners through civil courts, not through city code enforcement |
| City cannot override | The City of Austin cannot waive or override private deed restrictions through zoning changes, including HOME Initiative amendments |
| Statute of limitations | Under Texas Property Code § 202.003, restrictive covenants in subdivisions with a property owners' association may be enforced indefinitely (no automatic expiration) |
| Amendment process | CC&Rs can typically be amended by a vote of the membership (usually requiring a supermajority, e.g., 67% or 75% of lot owners), or by petition under Texas Property Code Chapter 209 |

### HOME Initiative and Deed Restrictions

The HOME Initiative Phase 1 (Ord. 20231207-001) amended Austin's Land Development Code to permit duplexes by right on SF-2 lots. However:

- **HOME does not preempt private deed restrictions.** If the Scofield Farms CC&Rs restrict lots to single-family use, a duplex remains prohibited as a matter of private covenant regardless of city zoning.
- The city will issue a building permit for a duplex if the project meets zoning and code requirements — but the permit does not immunize the owner from HOA enforcement action.
- The HOA or individual lot owners could seek injunctive relief (a court order to stop or reverse construction) if the project violates the CC&Rs.

## 7. Summary

**The Scofield Farms HOA deed restrictions represent the single most significant feasibility risk for this duplex project.** While Austin's HOME Phase 1 permits a duplex by right on this SF-2 lot as a matter of zoning, private deed restrictions almost certainly restrict the property to single-family residential use. Until the full CC&Rs are reviewed and a legal strategy is developed, the duplex project should be considered **at risk of HOA enforcement action**.

This is not a zoning constraint — it is a private covenant constraint that operates outside the city's regulatory framework.

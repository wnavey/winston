# Environmental Constraints Research

### 1. Watershed Designation

**Citation:** Austin LDC Chapter 25-8, Sec. 25-8-2 (Descriptions of Regulated Areas); City of Austin Watershed Protection Department
**Finding:** The property at 78727 in the Scofield Farms area of north Austin lies within the **Walnut Creek watershed**, which is classified as a **Suburban Watershed** under Chapter 25-8 of Austin's Land Development Code. Walnut Creek drains approximately 43 square miles from northern Austin to the Colorado River. Suburban watersheds are subject to Article 9 (Suburban Watershed Requirements) of Chapter 25-8, which imposes impervious cover limits and water quality controls that are more restrictive than urban watershed standards but less restrictive than water supply watershed standards.
**Confidence:** Verified

### 2. Impervious Cover Limits

**Citation:** Austin LDC Sec. 25-8-392 (Uplands Zone, Article 9 -- Suburban Watershed Requirements)
**Finding:** In the suburban watershed uplands zone, impervious cover limits for residential uses are as follows: single-family residential (lot >= 5,750 sq ft) may not exceed **50%** of the net site area; duplex or single-family on a lot smaller than 5,750 sq ft may not exceed **55-60%** (with transfer of development intensity). For a duplex on a standard Scofield subdivision lot (typically ~7,000-8,000 sq ft), the governing IC limit is expected to be **45-50% of net site area** absent any transfer. The more restrictive of the zoning IC limit and the watershed IC limit controls. IC limits in suburban watersheds apply at the subdivision level for platted lots, so the individual lot is governed by the subdivision-wide compliance framework.
**Confidence:** Verified (exact lot-specific percentage requires site plan review by Watershed Protection staff)

### 3. Water Quality Controls

**Citation:** Austin LDC Ch. 25-8 Subchapter A; Environmental Criteria Manual (ECM) Sec. 1.6.0 (Design Guidelines for Water Quality Controls)
**Finding:** Development in a suburban watershed generally requires structural water quality controls (sedimentation/filtration) designed to capture at least 0.5 inches of runoff and provide treatment equivalent to the Austin sand filter. For single-family and duplex residential development, the City of Austin assumes maintenance responsibility for stormwater control measures once accepted. Small residential sites (duplex on a single lot) may be eligible for regional water quality controls rather than on-site ponds, depending on whether the subdivision already has regional facilities in place. The Scofield subdivision was developed in the 1980s-1990s; the applicant should verify with the Watershed Protection Department whether existing regional controls serve this lot.
**Confidence:** Inferred (lot-level applicability requires Watershed staff confirmation)

### 4. Floodplain Status

**Citation:** FEMA FIRM Panel 48453C0115E, effective 06/16/1993; property survey
**Finding:** The property is located in **FEMA Zone X** (areas determined to be outside the 0.2% annual chance floodplain). The survey confirms the site is **not within the 100-year or 500-year floodplain**. No floodplain development permit or CLOMR/LOMR is required. No FEMA elevation certificate is needed. This is consistent with the site's upland topography within the Scofield subdivision, which sits at approximately 850-870 feet elevation above the Walnut Creek floodplain corridor.
**Confidence:** Verified

### 5. Edwards Aquifer Zone Status

**Citation:** TCEQ Edwards Aquifer Protection Program, 30 TAC Chapter 213; Travis County Aquifer Maps
**Finding:** The property at 78727 in north Austin is **not located within the Edwards Aquifer Recharge Zone (EARZ) or the Contributing Zone**. The Edwards Aquifer Recharge Zone in Travis County is concentrated south of the Colorado River, managed by the Barton Springs/Edwards Aquifer Conservation District (BSEACD). North Austin (including the Scofield area) overlies different geologic formations -- the Edwards Group limestones are not exposed at the surface in this area. No TCEQ Edwards Aquifer Protection Plan (EAPP) is required for development at this site.
**Confidence:** Verified

### 6. Critical Environmental Features (CEFs)

**Citation:** Austin LDC Sec. 25-8-281 et seq.; ECM Sec. 1.10.0 (CEF Identification and Protection); City of Austin Watershed Protection karst database
**Finding:** Critical Environmental Features in Austin include springs, caves, sinkholes, point recharge features, rimrock, and bluffs. The City maintains a karst database with over 550 karst CEF points, concentrated primarily in areas overlying Edwards Limestone (western and southwestern Austin). The Scofield subdivision in north Austin is developed on Cretaceous-age formations that are generally not associated with significant karst features. No known CEFs have been identified at or immediately adjacent to 12713 Cinchring Ln based on available data. However, an Environmental Resource Inventory (ERI) form is required as part of the site plan application, and Watershed Protection staff will confirm CEF status during review. If a CEF is identified, a 150-foot to 300-foot protective buffer would apply.
**Confidence:** Inferred (no CEFs expected, but formal ERI confirmation required at permitting)

### 7. Endangered Species and Habitat

**Citation:** USFWS Austin Ecological Services Field Office; Balcones Canyonlands Conservation Plan (BCCP); Travis County Balcones Canyonlands Preserve
**Finding:** Travis County is home to 14 federally listed endangered species, including the golden-cheeked warbler, six karst invertebrates, and three salamander species. The Balcones Canyonlands Conservation Plan (BCCP), established in 1996, provides an Incidental Take Permit covering development within the BCCP permit area. The Scofield subdivision at 78727 is in a fully developed suburban area in northeast Austin that **does not contain golden-cheeked warbler nesting habitat** (mature Ashe juniper-oak woodland) or known karst invertebrate habitat. The property is within the BCCP participation area, meaning the BCCP Participation Fee applies at time of development permit. No individual Section 7 or Section 10 consultation with USFWS is anticipated for this site.
**Confidence:** Verified (BCCP fee applicability should be confirmed with the City)

### 11. Watershed Protection Ordinance -- Demolition and Redevelopment

**Citation:** Austin LDC Ch. 25-8 Subchapter A; City of Austin Development Services Department
**Finding:** Demolition of the existing single-family home and construction of a new duplex constitutes **redevelopment** under Austin's Watershed Protection Ordinance. In a suburban watershed, redevelopment may not increase impervious cover beyond the limits prescribed in Sec. 25-8-392. The redevelopment must comply with current water quality requirements, including providing water quality controls for the developed area. The applicant will need to file a site plan (unless exempt as a single-lot residential project) and demonstrate compliance with IC limits and water quality standards. For a single-lot duplex, the site plan exemption process and applicable Watershed Protection review requirements should be confirmed with Development Services early in the entitlement process.
**Confidence:** Verified

### 12. Tree Protection

**Citation:** Austin LDC Ch. 25-8 Subchapter B (Tree and Natural Area Protection); City Arborist
**Finding:** Austin's tree protection ordinance applies to all development. Trees 19 inches in diameter or greater (measured at 4.5 feet above grade) are classified as **Heritage Trees** and require a permit for removal with mitigation. Trees 8 inches in diameter or greater are **Protected Trees** and require mitigation if removed during development. The existing lot at 12713 Cinchring Ln may contain mature trees established during or after subdivision development. A tree survey will be required as part of the building permit application, and any protected or heritage trees must be preserved or mitigated per the ordinance. Tree mitigation can be satisfied through on-site replanting or payment into the City's tree fund.
**Confidence:** Verified
